₹25 ride: India’s bike taxis are cheap, fast and still fighting for a place on the road

3MINDIA LANDSMILL

Title: India’s Bike Taxi Boom: Affordable, Fast, Yet Regulated

Summary:
Bike taxis in India, priced as low as Rs 25 for short trips, are filling a crucial “last‑mile” gap by offering faster, cheaper rides than cars or autorickshaws. Platforms like Rapido connect passengers with independently owned motorcycles, leveraging technology without maintaining large fleets. While states such as West Bengal and Karnataka have begun issuing contract‑carriage permits, regulatory inconsistency remains, with many regions still imposing bans or unclear rules. The sector’s growth is driven by the abundance of low‑cost two‑wheelers, high urban congestion, and consumer demand for convenience and affordability. Estimates project the market could reach $1.5 billion by 2030, but sustainable unit economics and safety concerns—especially for passengers riding pillion—are critical. Electrification could improve profitability, yet requires financing and charging infrastructure. The industry also creates livelihoods, with operators earning roughly Rs 25‑30 k per month, and initiatives are emerging to promote safety, inclusion, and women riders. Ultimately, the sector’s future hinges on harmonized regulation that preserves affordability while ensuring safety and viable earnings.
