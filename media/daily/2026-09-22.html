Finding Alpha: Indian Capital Market Infrastructure – Growth, Opportunities & Valuation

CANFINHOME MCX BSE CDSL

The discussion on CNBC TV18’s “Finding Alpha” examined the rapid expansion of India’s options market, which now accounts for over 70% of daily turnover and has seen premium growth of ~60% versus ~19% in cash markets post‑COVID. Speakers highlighted the shift from cash to options due to leverage caps, the divergence between contract count (5× US) and premium turnover (¼ US) driven by expiry‑day trading, and the counter‑cyclical nature of options revenue. They compared NSE and BSE market shares, noting NSE’s 90% dominance but a stabilising competitive landscape, and stressed significant upside in commodity options, which remain a fraction of global peers. Transaction‑cost structures were broken down (≈₹30 per ₹1 m cash trade vs. ₹350‑₹400 per ₹1 m options trade), with NSE’s margins hovering around 70% after one‑off costs. Valuation rationale for Indian exchanges was defended: higher growth prospects, product‑penetration opportunities, and strong operating leverage justify premium multiples (40×+ earnings) versus global averages (20‑25×). The broader market‑infrastructure ecosystem—brokers, depositories, AMCs/RTAs—was evaluated using a framework of core growth, diversification, cost efficiency, regulatory risk, and valuation. Margin‑lending (MTF) book size was noted at ~1.1× cash ADTO, lower than developed markets (2‑2.5×), indicating room for growth with brokers managing risk via short‑term borrowing limits and caps‑based leverage. Finally, depositaries stand to gain 3‑5% earnings from India’s robust IPO pipeline, while RTAs offer growth through international expansion and fee‑based leverage, making them attractive despite high valuation multiples
