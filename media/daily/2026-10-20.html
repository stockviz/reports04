Polycab case highlights misuse of IBC for modest dues

POLYCAB INDORAMA 3MINDIA ALKALI PYRAMID

The admission of a ₹2.79 cr operational debt petition against financially robust wire‑and‑cable maker Polycab India sparked concern that India’s Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) is being weaponised as a payment‑pressurisation tool. Despite Polycab’s ₹4,194 cr cash balance and ₹12,009 cr shareholder net worth, the NCLT admitted the case, prompting a swift stay by NCLAT. Experts warn that operational creditors—often MSMEs—can trigger insolvency for debts of just ₹1 cr, creating regulatory fallout, investor unease, and commercial disruption for large corporates. Supreme Court and NCLAT rulings (2026, 2025) have cautioned against using IBC for simple debt recovery, emphasizing its purpose for genuine insolvency. Data show over 4,100 operational creditor petitions since June 2026, with many withdrawals, settlements, or liquidations. For small suppliers, even a few crores can jeopardise working capital, yet insolvency proceedings rarely ensure payment and add legal costs. Recent policy moves—the 2026 amendment to the MSME Development Act, creation of MSEFCs, the Samadhaan portal, and TReDS—aim to speed dispute resolution and financing, but challenges remain as arbitration awards are often contested and many MSMEs still lack access to redress mechanisms. The Polycab episode underscores the need to curb IBC’s misuse while strengthening effective, proportionate remedies for genuine payment disputes

India Plans Security Checkpoint Device for Foreign Satellite Services like Starlink

JIOFIN

New Delhi is considering a hardware “security layer” that would sit at subscriber sites or ground stations to monitor and control traffic from foreign LEO satellite operators such as Starlink, OneWeb and Jio Satellite. The device, to be developed by public‑sector R&D units with possible private participation, aims to ensure national‑security compliance and enforce Indian regulations on foreign sat‑com services. The move follows Starlink’s resistance to Indian rules requiring domestic infrastructure and concerns about its unilateral decisions, especially after complaints from Iran, Namibia, South Africa and Ukraine. All satellite operators must meet security conditions to obtain spectrum, and the government seeks to prevent untrusted foreign transport carriers from threatening India’s critical communications

California Secures $6 Million Settlement Over 23andMe Data Breach

JARO

The California Attorney General reached a $6 million settlement with 23andMe’s bankruptcy estate, ending the state’s legal challenges to the company’s $305 million asset sale after a 2023 breach that exposed the genetic data of 6.9 million users, including 855,000 Californians. The state had argued the sale violated the Genetic Information Privacy Act because customers had not given explicit consent for their data to be transferred. The settlement follows court rulings that barred California from blocking the sale or suing the buyer, while customers retain the right to delete their data. 23andMe also agreed to pay $46.75 million directly to breach victims

Yes Bank Evaluates Capital Measures After Supreme Court Ruling

SUMICHEM YESBANK

Yes Bank expects its CET1 ratio to fall to 11.4% following the Supreme Court’s reversal of the 2020 write‑down of its AT1 bonds, a move that has triggered a 17% share price drop. The bank’s overall Tier 1 ratio should stay around 13.7%, above regulatory minimums. Ferrari Expands in India with EU Trade Pact Advantages and Localized Design Innovations

BCG

Ferrari is accelerating its expansion in India by opening dealerships in Chennai and Ahmedabad, joining its existing locations in New Delhi, Bengaluru, and Mumbai. The move aligns with the company’s strategy to leverage potential benefits from the proposed EU-India Free Trade Agreement (FTA), which could reduce import duties on luxury cars like Ferrari from 110% to as low as 10%, boosting demand.

Ferrari’s CEO, Benedetto Vigna, emphasized the brand’s commitment to India, calling the exhibition its first step toward a long-term relationship with Indian Ferraristi (customers and enthusiasts). Francesco Bianchi, President of Ferrari Far and Middle East, highlighted the FTA’s potential to revolutionize the luxury sports car market, with Ferrari being among the first brands to signal readiness to pass on tariff benefits to customers.

The company has partnered with KUN Group (Chennai) and Infinity Group (Ahmedabad) for new dealerships. Additionally, Ferrari unveiled its electric vehicle (Luce) in India, though the FTA does not currently cover electric imports from Europe.

In a unique collaboration, Ferrari worked with students from India’s National Institute of Design to create digital models inspired by 10 Indian cities (Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru, Chennai, Jaipur, Hyderabad, Kolkata, Ahmedabad, Varanasi, and Goa), aiming to infuse local cultural elements into future designs if prototypes advance

VEDL HINDZINC

India Smartphone Shipments Drop to Decade Low Amid Price Shock

VIVOBIOT

India’s smartphone shipments fell 26% YoY to 35.8 million units in Q3 2026, the lowest in ten years, as component shortages drove up prices and dampened festive-season demand. Online sales slumped, hurting Chinese brands whose market share fell to 68%, while Samsung reclaimed the top spot with 6.7 million units and Apple entered the top five with 3.9 million units, boosted by premium models and financing incentives.
