Meta Suspends Non‑Voting Share Plan Amid Investor Lawsuit

JPL

Meta has paused its proposal to create Class C non‑voting shares that would let CEO Mark Zuckerberg sell up to half of his stake without losing voting control, following a Delaware lawsuit filed by investors such as CalSTRS and PFA. The suit alleges the board breached fiduciary duties and acted under undue pressure from Zuckerberg, potentially disadvantaging ordinary shareholders. The plan, originally aimed at boosting financial flexibility for AI investments and allowing future capital raises without diluting Zuckerberg’s control, is now on hold pending trial in April

AMDIND

Anthropic raised $81.3 billion in its IPO, pricing shares at $83 and selling 980 million shares—surpassing SpaceX’s record—despite reporting a $104 billion net loss for the nine months to September 30. The offering underscores Wall Street’s bet on AI growth despite significant financial risks

Allana Leverages Meat‑Sourcing Edge to Enter India’s Pet‑Food Market

BCG GANESHCP

The firm focuses on building consumer trust via samples, vet partnerships, and a tiered brand strategy, while managing channel conflicts between e‑commerce and specialist retailers. Expansion plans include wet‑food manufacturing, broader consumer‑goods investments, and a goal to double ACPL’s revenue to ₹4,000 cr within three years

Wise CEO Criticizes RBI’s ₹25 Lakh Cap on Fintech Cross‑Border Payments

STANLEY AXISBANK SIL FINOPB

Wise co‑founder and CEO Kristo Krmann says RBI’s ₹25 lakh per‑transaction limit for payment‑aggregator cross‑border (PA‑CB) firms hampers Indian exporters and freelancers. The cap, introduced in RBI’s 2023 PA‑CB framework and retained in the 2025 master direction, forces payments above the limit to route through banks. Krmann notes Wise processes about $15 billion of inbound remittances (≈10% of India’s total) but struggles to explain the restriction to customers. RBI aims to monitor new fintech players for AML/KYC compliance before easing limits. Wise, authorised for inward payments and foreign‑currency sales via an AD‑II licence, launched its travel card in Dec 2025 and seeks to onboard Indian banks to its Wise Platform, currently used by global banks like Morgan Stanley and Standard Chartered. The article also mentions other fintechs gaining cross‑border licences and the broader competitive landscape with PayPal and Revolut

India’s Q2FY27 Earnings Surge Amid Price Hikes, Yet Margin Pressure Looms

SUPREMEINF TMPV MBECL JSWDULUX NAUKRI

Indian companies posted a 30% YoY rise in adjusted net profit in Q2FY27—the strongest in three years—driven by a 22% jump in net sales across large, medium, and small firms. After stripping out ~₹1 trillion of one‑off gains from the prior quarter, growth reflects genuine recovery, especially for banks, financial services, and commodity‑linked firms. However, raw material costs surged ~30% YoY, compressing aggregate net profit margins to 11.3% from 12% in Q3FY26 and 15.5% a year earlier. Price hikes (10‑30%) have underpinned revenue growth, but rising food, oil, freight, and metal costs threaten sectors like automobiles, FMCG, and oil & gas. Outlook suggests continued earnings momentum if input‑cost volatility eases, with metals, retail, infrastructure, and textiles expected to hold margins, while banks may benefit from a higher‑rate environment. Overall FY27 earnings are projected to grow ~18% in H2, raising full‑year growth forecasts above prior estimates

The Cambay Offshore Block Dispute: Vedanta vs. Government Over Operational Control

ONGC VEDL

The Cambay offshore block (CB-OS/2) dispute centers on Vedanta’s refusal to accept the government’s decision to transfer operations to ONGC, following the rejection of Vedanta’s 2021 request to extend its production-sharing contract (PSC). The block, covering 3,314 sq. km in Gujarat’s shallow waters, produces 3,400 barrels of oil/day and 3.4 lakh cubic meters of gas/day, with Vedanta (40% operator), ONGC (50%), and Invenire Petrodyne (10%) as stakeholders.

Key issues: 1. Legal battle: Vedanta challenged the government’s decision in Delhi High Court (2025), lost before a single-judge bench (July 2026), and is appealing to a division bench (judgment pending). ONGC took over operations in July 2026 after the interim stay was lifted. 2. Financial impact: Vedanta incurred a ₹379 crore impairment charge (Q1 FY27) and stopped recognizing revenue from the block post-court ruling. 3. Broader concerns: Vedanta chairman Anil Agarwal argues the move sends a “wrong signal to investors”, threatening regulatory stability and private-sector confidence in India’s oil/gas sector. Vedanta plans a $5B investment to boost production to 300K–500K barrels/day but warns disruptions could deter global investors. 4. Policy debate: Agarwal advocates for self-certification over approvals-based regulation and highlights India’s potential to emulate Guyana’s oil boom if continuity is ensured.

Priority in the dispute: - Continuity for the existing operator (Vedanta) is critical to maintain investor trust, operational stability, and long-term private-sector participation in India’s energy sector. - The government’s transfer to ONGC risks contractual uncertainty, asset impairment, and deterring future investments amid India’s push for domestic oil/gas production to reduce import dependence
