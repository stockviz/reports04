Qualcomm Initiates Layoffs in India Amid Global Restructuring

Qualcomm has started cutting jobs in India, with roughly 1,000 positions—about 7% of its global workforce—potentially at risk, primarily at its Bengaluru site. Affected employees will receive severance equal to three months’ pay, plus an extra month’s salary if they sign release documents within three days. The restructuring follows similar cuts in the US and is part of a phased global effort, with another round in India expected in January. The moves accompany the company’s broader shift toward data‑center and AI chip markets, aiming for growth beyond smartphones

Airbus A350-1000ULR Sets World Distance Record

Airbus successfully completed a 24‑hour, 12,503‑nautical‑mile nonstop flight from Melbourne to Toulouse, establishing a new Guinness World Record for the longest commercial‑airliner distance. The test flight, part of Project Sunrise’s A350‑1000ULR validation for Qantas, demonstrated the aircraft’s ultra‑long‑range capability, involved 13 crew members, and confirmed the aircraft’s readiness for Qantas’ planned Sydney‑London and Sydney‑New York non‑stop services, with the first delivery slated for April 2027

Nvidia Shares Plunge Amid Spy-Related Allegations Involving CEO

ABMINTLLTD

Nvidia’s stock fell roughly 10% over two trading sessions after reports that CEO Jensen Huang had an alleged relationship with a Chinese businesswoman suspected of being a Beijing spy, sparking concerns over corporate governance and possible illicit shipments of $7.5 billion worth of H100, H200 and A100 chips to China. The drop briefly pushed Nvidia below Microsoft in market value before it recovered to second place behind Apple. Nvidia’s board has hired external legal counsel to investigate, and the FBI is examining whether Huang was targeted in a foreign‑intelligence operation. Investors are also uneasy about the profitability of Nvidia’s heavy AI investments, while U.S. officials call for tighter export restrictions

Alleged Nvidia Chip Smuggling Network Tied to CEO’s Personal Relationship

AHL CENTENKA

According to The Information, Alice Huang—identified as a top executive of Singapore‑based Megaspeed and alleged former lover of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang—helped move at least 240,000 Nvidia H100/H200 GPUs, valued around $7.5 billion, to China despite U.S. export bans. Megaspeed, possibly a front company, is accused of purchasing restricted chips and rerouting them via shell entities, including Shanghai Century Litong and Malaysia‑based Speedmatrix. U.S. authorities have opened investigations into the network, while Nvidia has formed an independent committee to assess any security breaches. Recent seizures of AI chips in Malaysia and a Commerce Department probe further highlight concerns over evasion of export controls

Indian Garment Makers Shift Away From US Market After Tariff Shock

PAGEIND GOKEX ORIENTABRA INTELLECT

Indian apparel exporters, led by firms like Orient Craft and Gokaldas Exports, are rapidly diversifying away from the United States following steep tariffs that peaked at 50% in 2025. US shipments fell from 84% to under 10% of sales, while exports to Europe, the UK, the UAE and the domestic market are growing. Companies are targeting Germany, France, Italy and the Netherlands, leveraging duty‑free access under the India‑UK deal and potential EU agreements, and are expanding in cost‑friendly hubs such as Ranchi. Though the US remains a key market, manufacturers see diversification as essential to protect margins and the livelihoods of thousands of workers

India’s Diwali travel sees first post‑Covid flight cut, fares surge

INDIGO

Airlines reduced October‑November capacity by about 6% – the first decline since 2020 – as higher fuel costs, a weak rupee and airspace restrictions drive up fares. IndiGo trimmed capacity 1% and saw a 34% rise in passenger yields but posted an ₹18.8 billion loss. One‑way tickets now cost around ₹20,000, comparable to last year’s round‑trip prices, making festive travel less affordable for passengers

Honda to Invest $3 Billion in Ohio Hybrid Vehicle Plant, Adding 2,000 Jobs

HONDAPOWER

Honda Motor announced a $3 billion Ohio plant to produce hybrid cars, targeting 240,000 vehicles annually from 2029 and creating 2,000 jobs, raising U.S. capacity to 1.3 million units. The company also pledged C$867 million for Canadian operations, including a C$166 million Alliston expansion backed by a C$313 million government investment. Additional plans include $338 million for another Ohio project and new investments in Georgia and Alabama, alongside a shift from EV goals to hybrid expansion. Other automakers, such as Audi, are also making large U.S. investments amid tariff considerations

Inditex pledges $50 million to green‑loan fund for suppliers

GENESYS

Inditero, the world’s largest fashion retailer, has committed $50 million to a new fund, led by the World Bank’s IFC, that will provide low‑cost loans to suppliers for energy‑efficiency upgrades. The initiative, the first public‑private partnership of its kind, aims to help small manufacturers, especially in developing countries, transition to net‑zero operations as the textile sector accounts for up to 8% of global greenhouse‑gas emissions. IFC plans to raise an additional $400 million to expand the program

Google Secures Tightened Privacy Safeguards for Spirit Airlines Data Sale

Alphabet’s Google has reached an agreement to add stronger privacy protections to its $10 million purchase of employee data from the bankrupt Spirit Airlines, clearing the path for a bankruptcy judge’s approval. The deal now requires a more rigorous de‑identification process, exclusion of health and medical information, independent verification of the de‑identified data, a ban on re‑identification attempts, and secure, confidential handling. The agreement follows concerns raised by lawmakers and a privacy ombudsman, who urged the exclusion of personal passenger data from the 100 million emails and 500 million Teams messages slated for AI training

Anthropic’s IPO: OpenAI Rivalry, Pentagon Disputes, and Founders’ Connections

BANKBARODA

Anthropic, an AI company founded in 2021 by ex-OpenAI researchers—including CEO Dario Amodei and his sister Daniela Amodei—is preparing for a high-profile IPO with a $70–$78/share price range and potential proceeds of $64.35 billion (at the top end). The company, which rivals OpenAI (developer of ChatGPT), was born from internal disagreements over AI safety and governance.

Key Connections & Controversies

  • OpenAI Split: Dario, Daniela, and co-founders like Jared Kaplan (now CSO) and Chris Olah (housemates of the Amodeis) left OpenAI in 2020 to launch Anthropic, citing clashing visions on AI development.
  • Major Investors: Backed by Amazon, Alphabet (Google), Microsoft, Nvidia, and Founders Fund, with a $965B valuation (surpassing OpenAI in 2025).
  • Pentagon Dispute: Anthropic rejected Pentagon requests to use its Claude AI for domestic surveillance and lethal autonomous weapons, leading to a ban on federal use under Trump’s administration and a “supply-chain risk” designation.

Investor Considerations

  • Rapid growth but heavy losses: High computing costs and revenue expansion risks.
  • Regulatory scrutiny: Government tensions and AI safety debates could impact valuation.
  • Market test: The IPO will gauge investor confidence in AI’s long-term commercial viability amid uncertainty over returns and infrastructure needs.

Most likely to shape investor confidence: AI’s commercial potential (e.g., Claude’s adoption) and government/safety disputes (e.g., Pentagon bans, regulatory crackdowns
