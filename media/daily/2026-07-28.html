Leadership in a New World Order: Deepali Goenka on Scaling Indian Manufacturing, Sustainability, and Women Empowerment

In a virtual session at MAKE 2026, Deepali Goenka of Wellspun highlighted how her Vapi plant in Gujarat produces the iconic Wimbledon towels, emphasizing design, sustainability, and the reuse of old towels. She described the current “BANI” world—brittle, anxious, non‑linear, incomprehensible—and stressed resilience through vertical integration, close control of the cotton‑to‑finished‑goods supply chain, and strong customer partnerships. Goenka outlined Wellspun’s playbook for scale: shifting focus from cost to capability, becoming a partner of choice, leveraging deep consumer insights across 60 countries, extensive patents, and a 360° ecosystem of warehouses, managed inventory, blockchain traceability, and on‑shore pillow factories in the U.S. She discussed the impact of new FTAs, the need for on‑site manufacturing, and India’s competitive advantages—green energy, abundant manpower, and a robust MSME base—positioning the country as the next global textile hub. On technology, she described a move toward Industry 5.0, using AI for predictive analytics, shop‑floor control centers, and upskilling workers while keeping technology in service of people. Goenka forecasted India’s domestic market growing to 10‑12 % of Wellspun’s revenue as per‑capita consumption rises, driven by diverse regional tastes and religious tourism. Capital‑expenditure will target debottlenecking and efficiency, balanced with disciplined debt management. Finally, she championed women’s participation in manufacturing, detailing the “Jagruti” program, family‑support initiatives, and her pledge for 50 % female representation in at least one facility, arguing that women’s leadership is essential for India’s economic ascent
