Indian Discount Brokers Push SEBI for Lower MDR on UPI Capital-Market Transactions

ANGELONE GROWW

Discount brokerages like Groww, Zerodha, Angel One, and Upstox have urged SEBI to revise its proposed Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) framework for UPI transactions in capital markets, citing concerns over cost burdens and customer impact. Key demands include:

  1. Lower MDR caps: Proposed 2 basis points (bps) for capital-market transactions, with an absolute fee cap of ₹2–5 per transaction (vs. the current 300 cap, deemed irrelevant due to high transaction limits).
  2. Higher MDR exemption threshold: Raising the current ₹2,000 threshold to ₹20,000, as 80% of capital-market transactions are lumpsum payments (95% UPI-driven), with 70% already exceeding ₹2,000.
  3. Exclusion of customer fund movements: No MDR on UPI deposits (when clients add funds) or running-account (RA) payouts (refunds of unused funds), arguing brokers shouldn’t bear costs for non-revenue-generating transactions.

The industry argues the current rules could increase costs for brokers and customers, with MDR fees disproportionately affecting high-value transactions. The proposals reflect off-the-record inputs from multiple firms
