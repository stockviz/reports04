ABDL

ABDL’s MD Amar Sinha outlined a three‑year transformation focused on boosting premium‑and‑above (P&A) brands, increasing international presence to 60‑70 countries, and strengthening backward integration with a new malt plant. Triumph Tracker 400 Review: Design, Engine, and Verdict

BAJAJ-AUTO MRF TV18BRDCST

The Tracker 400 blends flat‑track styling—wide handlebars, minimalist single‑seat, dual‑purpose MRF tyres, and flat‑track‑inspired number plates—with Triumph’s typical build quality and fit‑and‑finish. It houses the 349 cc liquid‑cooled single‑cylinder TR engine, the most performance‑tuned version in Triumph’s 350‑cc range, delivering ~40 PS and 33 Nm, which is smooth and powerful above 4,000 rpm but lacks punch at low revs. Riding is comfortable for highway cruising (≈100 km/h at 6,000 rpm) with a slightly aggressive posture; handling is confident and brakes are adequate. Priced around ₹2.46  lakh, it undercuts the Thruxton 400, offering a fun, well‑built bike, though tyre quality, low‑end torque, and riding position may not suit everyone. Overall, the Tracker 400 is a stylish, enjoyable road‑oriented motorcycle with respectable performance for its segment

Honda ZR‑V Review: Premium Hybrid Crossover with Civic‑Like Dynamics

HONDAPOWER

The new Honda ZR‑V (ZRV) is a CBU‑imported premium SUV built on the 11th‑gen Civic platform, featuring a 2.0 L Atkinson‑cycle engine paired with Honda’s 4th‑gen e:HEV system (total output 327 PS, 315 Nm). It delivers 0‑100 km/h in 7.9 s and a claimed 22.8 km/l. At 4.6 m length, 1.6 m height and 1.6 t weight, it’s lower and lighter than typical SUVs, offering hatchback‑like proportions and engaging, light‑yet‑accurate steering, a multi‑link rear suspension, and sport‑mode sound‑enhanced acceleration. Inside, the cabin feels premium with soft‑touch materials, a 10.2‑in digital cluster, 9‑in touchscreen, Bose audio, wireless charging, dual‑zone climate, 360° camera and ambient lighting; however, ventilated seats, a sunroof and a spare wheel are missing. Pricing around ₹40 Lakh would make it competitive; above ₹50 Lakh it faces stiff competition from higher‑priced crossovers and entry‑level luxury models. Overall, the ZR‑V promises a refined, efficient driving experience for buyers who value hybrid efficiency and Civic‑derived dynamics over sheer size or off‑road capability

FORVIA’s $200 Million Indian Expansion Aims for $1 Billion Business by 2030

Global automotive supplier FORVIA (Faurecia‑HELLA) will invest about 2,000 crore INR ($200 M) in India through 2030 to boost its revenue from ₹450 M to over ₹1 B. The plan focuses on expanding electronics, clean‑mobility, lighting and a new dedicated seat‑manufacturing plant, with electronics revenue targeted to rise from ₹100 M to ₹400 M. The company is shifting to frugal, India‑specific product design, decentralising decision‑making, and leveraging the combined strengths of Faurecia, HELLA and Clarion to deepen its local footprint, viewing India as a key growth pillar and the first phase of a longer‑term expansion

Why Global Automotive Suppliers Must Shift From Europe to Local Decision‑Making in India

Martin Fischer, CEO of FORVIA, explains that India is evolving from a cost‑engineered support hub to a market‑centric center where products are conceived, engineered, validated, and launched locally. To meet faster customer expectations and regional supply‑chain resilience, suppliers need “local for local” strategies, integrating software and electronics into vehicle systems and empowering Indian engineering teams with ownership and decision‑making authority. This decentralised, technology‑agnostic approach ensures responsiveness across diverse powertrain trends and builds trusted, accountable local organisations
