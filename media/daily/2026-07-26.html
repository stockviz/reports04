Key Guidelines and Common Mistakes for FY26 Income Tax Return Filing (India

3MINDIA

The episode explains that FY26 returns must be filed under the Income Tax Act 1961, marking the last year before the 2025 Act takes effect. The new tax regime is now default, with higher exemption limits (₹4 Lakh) and a larger standard deduction (₹75,000), leading many taxpayers with zero liability still needing to file. Mandatory filing applies to anyone earning above the basic exemption, directors, shareholders of unlisted companies, and those with foreign assets, regardless of tax due. New disclosures include separate reporting of F&O and intraday trading, ATG donation details, a mandatory secondary address, and Schedule AL asset‑liability statements for assets over ₹1 Crore. Form eligibility is clarified: ITR‑1 (Sahaj) for residents with ≤₹50 Lakh income and no business income (up to two house properties allowed); ITR‑2 for non‑business filers not meeting ITR‑1 criteria; ITR‑3 for business/professional income; ITR‑4 (Sugam) for presumptive income businesses ≤₹2 Crore. Deadlines are 31 July for most taxpayers, extended to 31 August (or 31 October if audit‑required) for those with trading/business income. Common pitfalls to avoid are selecting the wrong ITR form, mismatches with Form 16, Form 26AS, or AIS, omitting exempt income, gifts, non‑monetary transactions (e.g., property redevelopment, inheritance), foreign assets, incorrect bank details, and failing to e‑verify the return within 30 days. Following this checklist helps ensure timely, accurate filing and avoids notices or demands
