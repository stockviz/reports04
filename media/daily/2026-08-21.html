The Psychology of Money: 5 Common Investment Biases

ANANDRATHI

The discussion explores five behavioral biases that distort financial decisions: 1) Loss aversion – fear of losing outweighs the pleasure of gaining, causing investors to stay in losing positions or keep cash idle, eroding real returns. 2) Herd mentality (FOMO) – people follow crowd behavior without analysis, often buying late or over‑leveraging due to social pressure. 3) Anchoring – investors cling to specific price points or past valuations, ignoring changing fundamentals and missing better opportunities. 4) Confirmation bias – we seek information that supports our pre‑existing views, especially on social media, leading to echo chambers; deliberately seeking counter‑arguments can mitigate this. 5) Disposition effect – the tendency to sell winners quickly and hold losers too long; overcoming it requires evaluating each position on its own merits, asking if you’d add more capital, and making decisions based on conviction rather than past performance. The conversation emphasizes awareness, rational analysis, and disciplined, zero‑basis decision‑making to counter these biases

Verify rates against Google or bank dashboards and beware of Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC), which can add 5‑6% if you opt to pay in INR abroad. Carry some cash for places that don’t accept cards or impose surcharges (e.g., small shops in Thailand). Avoid common mistakes: blindly choosing zero‑markup cards without reviewing fees, ignoring DCC, and overlooking reward structures or ancillary benefits. Tailor the final choice to your travel frequency, expected spend, and whether the annual fee is justified by rewards and protections
