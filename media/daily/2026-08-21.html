Geopolitical Shifts Reshape India’s Private Equity Landscape: The Rise of Resilience-Driven Investments

KHAITANLTD 5PAISA BNALTD BCG

Geopolitical tensions in the Gulf and broader global uncertainties are fundamentally altering how private equity (PE) investors approach deals in India. While investment volumes have declined—with traditional PE down 17% in 2025 and deal volumes dropping 8%—capital is becoming more selective, prioritizing resilience over sheer growth potential.

Key trends: 1. Geopolitics as a Core Factor: Once a footnote, geopolitical risks (tariffs, sanctions, supply-chain disruptions) now demand dedicated analysis, with investors scrutinizing supply-chain dependencies, tariff exposure, and business continuity plans. Due diligence now includes sanctions compliance, shipping routes, and alternative sourcing strategies.

  1. Sector Shifts: Capital is flowing into domestic-focused sectors (manufacturing, financial services, consumer retail) and export-oriented industries (pharma, auto components, electronics) aligned with India’s self-reliance agenda. Sectors reliant on global supply chains or thin margins face tighter scrutiny.

  2. Evolving Deal Structures: Investors are using earn-outs, deferred payments, and stricter “material adverse change” (MAC) clauses to mitigate risks. Founders must now justify supplier locations, sales destinations, and contingency plans early in negotiations.

  3. Regulatory Hurdles: Cross-border deals require deeper foreign investment screening, merger controls, and beneficial ownership assessments, adding complexity.

  4. India’s Long-Term Appeal: Despite risks, India remains a top PE destination due to:

    • Structural shift from “China+1 hedge” to a genuine manufacturing alternative (backed by PLI schemes and global supply-chain diversification).
    • Strong fundamentals: Moderating interest rates, resilient consumption, and government spending.
    • Large funds betting big: KKR ($20B over a decade), Temasek ($50B+ exposure), and ChrysCapital’s $2.2B Fund X.
    • Improving exits: Trade sales surged in FY25, though overall exits declined, signaling a focus on valuation discipline.

Bottom Line: PE capital isn’t disappearing—it’s becoming more disciplined, risk-aware, and operationally engaged. The new edge isn’t just spotting high-growth assets but ensuring they can **thrive in a volatile world
