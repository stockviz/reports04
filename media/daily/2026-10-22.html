Nikhil Kamath on India’s AI Strategy: Why Foundational Focus May Be Misplaced & How to Compete

TCS INFY HCL-INSYS GFSTEELS

Nikhil Kamath breaks down India’s AI landscape into five key layers: 1. Hardware/compute (chips, data centers) 2. Frontier models (cutting-edge LLMs like those in the U.S./China) 3. Inference/optimization (efficient deployment of open-source models) 4. Training/data provision (companies like Turing or Mercause) 5. Applications/services (end-user products).

Key Insights: - Frontier AI (Layer 2) is nearly impossible for India to compete in due to lack of risk capital and global-scale experimentation (unlike China/U.S.). The odds of an Indian company breaking through here are low. - Inference (Layer 3) is a viable opportunity: India’s 30–40% cheaper compute costs (power, labor) and open-source models create a niche for efficient, localized AI services—especially for enterprises wanting private, cost-effective deployments. - China’s success stems from state-driven “capitalism”: Provinces/municipalities compete like private firms, with land leases functioning like ownership. India could replicate this by treating states as autonomous economic entities (e.g., CMs as “CEOs”), retaining local revenue upside. - India’s strength lies in applications/services (Layer 5): While Q-commerce/e-commerce thrive domestically, Kamath’s Foundry initiative aims to build global consumer brands (e.g., shampoo, toothpaste) targeting markets like the U.S.—not just India. He highlights a stark contrast: Nadella’s daily life in Silicon Valley touches no Indian products, only Chinese ones, underscoring the need for India to export more to strengthen its economy.

Critique of Indian IT giants (TCS/Infosys/HCL): They missed the AI boom by over-investing in foundational layers (e.g., training data, generic services) instead of specializing in inference or niche applications where India’s cost advantages shine. The debate over their missed opportunities ignores that not all layers are winnable—strategy matters more than just talent or capital
