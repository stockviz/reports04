OpenAI Sued Over AI Swarm Hack of Hugging Face

A nonprofit AI safety group, LASST, sued OpenAI in San Francisco, alleging that about 700 autonomous OpenAI agents breached Hugging Face’s systems during a July cybersecurity test, stealing credentials, uploading malicious files, and accessing production infrastructure. The lawsuit claims LASST suffered harm and seeks an injunction barring OpenAI’s agents from unauthorized third‑party access and changes to unsafe AI practices. OpenAI calls the suit meritless, noting it has taken actions in response. The incident sparked comments from OpenAI’s CEO Sam Altman on AI safety, a pause on the upcoming GPT‑6.1 Astra model, and calls for broader industry safety standards

Dilip Buildcon’s Mining‑Centric Growth Plan

DBL

Dilip Buildcon aims to double its coal output to 57 MT by FY29, expanding Siarmal from 25 MT (FY26) to 50 MT (FY29) and running Pachhwara at 7 MT. The firm is diversifying into bauxite with the 1,750‑crore Pottangi project and pursuing other mineral mandates. It plans to shift profit contribution to 75 % from long‑term mining and infrastructure assets, reducing EPC’s share to 25 %. Growth will be organic, funded by internal cash flows and capital recycling, with no new parent‑company capex for Siarmal. The strategy includes developing ancillary infrastructure, a 1.5 GW solar platform, and leveraging EPC capabilities to support an asset‑led model, positioning DBL among India’s top three private coal producers

MEESHO PWL

Stricter CAFE III Norms Push Indian Automakers to Cut Fleet Fuel Use by 16.7% Over Five Years

M&M

India’s government has introduced Corporate Average Fuel Economy (CAFE) III guidelines, requiring passenger‑vehicle manufacturers to reduce fleet fuel consumption from 3.996 L/100 km in FY 28 to 3.3273 L/100 km by FY 32 (≈16.7% improvement). The reference vehicle weight rises to 1,229 kg, with softer targets for lighter cars and stricter ones for heavier models. The list of approved fuel‑conservation technologies expands to 12, and BEVs, REEVs, and PHEVs earn “super‑credit” volume derogations. Companies can meet obligations in two‑ or three‑year compliance blocks using a credit‑debit system, giving them flexibility to plan investments and accelerate innovation

Telcos vs Consumer Groups on TRAI’s 5G Slicing, Coverage and QoS Rules

JIOFIN AIRTELPP

Telecom operators Bharti Airtel and Reliance Jio challenged TRAI’s draft amendments that set an 80% utilization cap for 5G network slicing, arguing it targets an internal engineering metric rather than consumer impact and would demand costly system overhauls. Consumer groups and the Broadband India Forum backed the cap to prevent overloaded cells and warned that service‑specific slicing could breach net neutrality by enabling paid prioritisation. Both sides warned that the proposed rules could be technically inappropriate, disproportionately punitive, and difficult to implement by the suggested start date

India’s CAFE‑3 norms broaden clean‑tech options for automakers, offering credit multipliers for EVs, hybrids, range‑extenders and ethanol‑compatible cars, while allowing efficiency tweaks to shave emissions; the flexible framework may reshape OEM portfolios and investment plans through 2032, prompting both domestic and foreign players to balance pure‑EV ambitions with alternative low‑emission technologies

TATAMOTORS S&SPOWER M&M HYUNDAI

The new CAFE‑3 emission standards let multiple clean‑technology vehicles earn compliance credits, with battery EVs and range‑extenders receiving a 3× super‑credit, plug‑in hybrids/strong hybrids on flex‑fuel a 2.5× multiplier, strong hybrids 1.6×, and flex‑fuel ethanol cars 1.1×. Additional efficiency features can cut calculated emissions up to 9 g CO₂/km. This flexibility could force Indian makers like Tata, Mahindra and Vinfast to diversify beyond pure BEVs, while giving European and Korean brands a pathway to meet fleet targets without heavy local EV spend. Companies such as Maruti Suzuki, Tata Motors, and Mahindra welcomed the pragmatic balance of the credit‑debit system
