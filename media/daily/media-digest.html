Modes: The Deep Moats That Keep Companies Profitable for Decades

VBL AARON

The episode explains that a “mode” (or moat) is a long‑term competitive advantage—like strong branding, vast distribution, regulatory barriers, technology or customer stickiness—that protects profits over time. Modes manifest in financials as high margins, stable cash flow, strong returns and low volatility. Examples include trusted brands (e.g., FediCall), extensive distribution networks (e.g., Barun beverages), and platform lock‑in (e.g., InfoEdge/Nokia). Companies with durable modes can sustain pricing power and weather economic cycles, creating predictable, compounding wealth. The next episode will explore market cycles and their impact on even the strongest businesses

Three Key Checks Before Investing in a Mutual Fund

AXISBANK ABSLAMC BIRLAMONEY

The discussion highlights three essential criteria for evaluating mutual funds: (1) Consistency of performance – use rolling returns to gauge how steadily a fund performs across market cycles rather than relying on short‑term past returns. (2) Fund house credibility – assess the AMC’s reputation, integrity, and any adverse reports before committing capital. (3) Portfolio fit – ensure the fund complements your overall asset allocation and avoids concentration in a single market segment. The conversation also notes that risk ratios (Sharpe, Sortino, Treynor, volatility) are useful mainly for filtering out extreme cases, not for picking winners, because they are based on historical data that may not predict future outcomes. Diversification across caps, styles, and asset classes is emphasized as a prudent strategy
