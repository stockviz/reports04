Compass vs. CRMLS: Legal Battle Over California Home Listing Rules

CALSOFT IBREALEST

Compass Inc. sued California’s largest real estate listing service, CRMLS, alleging its one-business-day rule for listing homes violates antitrust laws. Compass argues the policy unfairly restricts its three-phase marketing strategy—holding listings private initially to boost sales prices—while CRMLS claims Compass exploits the system by withholding data from competitors.

The dispute highlights tensions between traditional MLS practices (shared databases used by Zillow, etc.) and private listing networks like Compass’s. CRMLS preemptively sued Compass in New York, seeking to enforce its rules. The conflict mirrors broader industry fights over data access, competition, and homeowner choice, with recent legal skirmishes involving Zillow and regional MLS services.

Compass’s recent acquisition of Anywhere Realty (including Coldwell Banker) has intensified scrutiny over market consolidation, with lawmakers like Senator Elizabeth Warren and New York’s AG probing its dominance. The case underscores debates over who controls listing timelines: homeowners/agents or centralized MLS authorities

Record Peak Power Demand in India Surpasses 250 GW in September

IEX

India’s electricity grid is hitting unprecedented load levels, with peak domestic demand exceeding 250 GW on most September days and reaching a historic 260 GW on October 5. The surge is driven by cooling and irrigation needs amid weak monsoon rains, prompting calls for more baseload from thermal plants alongside renewables. Day‑ahead market prices stay above ₹7 per unit, while coal inventories at power stations have dwindled to about 18 million tonnes, with 82 of 190 coal‑fired plants in a critical fuel‑shortage state. The situation is expected to ease as temperatures drop and cooling demand falls

Regulatory Overhaul Boosts Indian Insurance Outlook, but Predictability Needed for Long‑Term Plans

HDFCBANK ABMINTLLTD

ERGO International’s COO Theodoros Kokkalas says India’s rapid insurance regulatory reforms—far faster than Europe’s—are positive, yet insurers require clearer timelines to adapt. He stresses the need for predictable changes to enable long‑term business planning, as the market struggles with high combined ratios (110‑115%). ERGO targets an 80‑85% ratio through disciplined underwriting, pricing, claims handling, cost control, and AI‑driven efficiency. The firm projects 6‑10% growth through 2030, driven largely by its US arm ERGO NEXT and sees India as a top market due to size and low penetration, though expansion depends on rising incomes. ERGO retains its 49% stake in HDFC ERGO, favoring the long‑standing partnership over full ownership or an IPO, citing no capital need
