Bharat Biotech Reboots Post‑Covid: New Vaccine Pipeline, Gene‑Therapy Push & IPO Plans

AJOONI

After a two‑year reset, founder Krishna Ella says Bharat Biotech is “back in action,” retaining its vaccine focus while expanding into cell‑ and gene‑therapy, monoclonal antibodies and antimicrobial‑resistance solutions. The TB vaccine has cleared Phase 2 and is moving toward Phase 3; pipelines also include Nipah, Ebola, chikungunya, malaria and Zika vaccines. Ella emphasizes affordable gene‑therapy for India’s growing cancer burden, and notes the company’s debt‑free ₹2,000 crore revenue, recent plant investments and acquisitions. An IPO is being considered to fund these new ventures, with his children already leading vaccine and veterinary divisions

TV Ratings Blackout Extends, Broadcasters Press for Restoration Ahead of Festive Season

PRSMJOHNSN VISACHROME 3MINDIA ZEEL CRISIL SUNSHINE RADIOCITY DEVIT

The Indian government’s ministry of information and broadcasting has prolonged the suspension of TV audience measurement, withholding BARC’s ratings until its registration complies with new guidelines mandating cross‑screen measurement, 33% independent board representation, and removal of landing‑page data. BARC’s panel count nears its 80,000 target, and it plans to appoint a fifth independent director to meet the requirement. Industry players, including broadcasters and the IBDF, warn that the blackout erodes advertiser confidence, revenue visibility, and media planning during the lucrative festive period. While the ministry aims to foster competition with new entrants like TAM India and Chrome DM, concerns arise over the financial viability of multiple agencies and potential data conflicts. Despite the suspension, BARC’s operations remain financially stable, reporting 297 cr revenue and a modest profit for FY 2024‑25

Mundra Power Plant to Operate at Full Capacity Until December

TATAPOWER RVNL

India’s power ministry is likely to extend Section 11 provisions, keeping Tata Power’s 4,000 MW imported‑coal Mundra plant running at full capacity through December to meet unusually high peak demand of 271 GW. The plant, currently under a special power purchase agreement with Gujarat Urja Vikas Nigam and negotiating similar deals with Rajasthan, Haryana and Punjab, needs such agreements to pass through volatile imported‑coal costs. With domestic coal mixes not feasible due to plant design, the ministries of power and coal are exploring cost‑reduction measures while the Section 11 directive, used in past peak‑demand periods, is expected to be renewed

Symplr Secures $175 Million Equity Injection and Debt Restructuring Plan

5PAISA DELTACORP BCG

Symplr’s private‑equity owners, Clearlake Capital and Charlesbank Capital, agreed to inject roughly $175 million of preferred equity as part of a broader recapitalization. The plan adds a $103.5 million first‑out second‑lien loan from Ares and other lenders, defers interest on junior paper, and raises coupons by 100 bps while extending maturities (first‑lien term loan to 2030). Non‑participating investors will be pushed to the bottom of the capital structure. The restructuring follows steep declines in Symplr’s loan prices and a Moody’s junk‑rating downgrade, aiming to preserve cash and stabilize the distressed healthcare‑software firm

Maharashtra FDA’s Wide‑Scale Hygiene Crackdown Faces Industry Pushback

TOYAMSL TATACOFFEE DODLA

Maharashtra’s Food and Drug Administration is intensifying inspections across food categories, targeting hygiene violations even in high‑profile establishments like Olympia Coffee House and Iskcon Temple. Commissioner Tukaram Mundhe says the agency is driven by data, moving from sample testing to thorough inspections, and is examining festive foods and infant products. While licenses have been suspended or improvement notices issued for issues such as unhygienic kitchens, expired stock, and improper labeling, industry bodies (NRAI, HRA, AHAR) urge a balanced, consultative approach that allows corrective action before strict enforcement. The FDA stresses a uniform, 360‑degree public‑health focus regardless of a business’s size

Ichor Biologics seeks $80‑100 million PE funding for minority stake sale

BMWVENTLTD 5PAISA DHUNINV AJOONI ANTHEM ALIVUS HALEOSLABS

Hyderabad‑based Ichor Biologics is looking to raise ₹765‑950 crore from private‑equity investors by selling a significant minority stake. Promoters Ananda Reddy Sudini (50.5%) and Sujana Sudini (44.5%) hold most of the company, with Time Cap Pharma Labs owning 5%. Alvarez & Marsal is handling the process. Ichor, founded in 2021, makes plasma‑derived therapeutic proteins (e.g., Hemalb, Clotnine) for neurology, oncology, immunology and other areas, selling domestically and exporting. FY25 revenue fell to $14.7 million from $17 million and EBITDA halved to $2 million. The firm recently expanded plasma processing capacity to 281,000 L (up from 130,000 L) with a ₹165.33 crore investment. Competitors include Reliance Life Sciences and PlasmaGen; the Indian plasma protein therapy market is projected to hit $500 million by 2030. Existing backers are Artian Investments, Ashish Kacholia, Eight Roads Ventures and FPrime Capital

States and Writers Guild Settle Lawsuits, Clearing Path for Paramount‑Warner $81 B Merger

LGBBROSLTD PARACABLES

Twelve U.S. states and the Writers Guild of America have settled antitrust lawsuits against Paramount’s $81 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, removing the final regulatory obstacle. Under the agreement announced by California Attorney General Rob Bonta, Paramount will commit an extra $1.5 billion to U.S. film production over five years, monitor editorial independence of its news operations, prohibit writer layoffs at CBS News for five years, and pay $17.5 million to the WGA health fund and cover litigation fees. The settlement still requires judicial approval. While officials say the deal will boost Hollywood and create more entertainment options, critics warn it will further consolidate the industry, reduce competition, and potentially raise costs for consumers
