Mahindra slashes EV prices with ₹8 lakh battery‑as‑a‑service cut

M&M HYUNDAI TATAMOTORS

Mahindra & Mahindra has introduced a flat ₹8 lakh reduction across its electric SUV range by moving the battery cost into a separate Battery‑as‑a‑Service (BaaS) scheme. This brings the BE 6 SPORTEQ’s advertised price down to ₹11.45 lakh, the XEV 9S to ₹12.65 lakh, and the XEV 9e to ₹13.90 lakh, positioning them against 12‑15 lakh petrol/diesel SUVs. The BaaS model charges a fixed battery usage fee (≈₹3.75 per km for Mahindra) rather than a discount, effectively creating dual financing. Competitors like Tata and JSW MG use similar schemes, but Mahindra’s larger ₹8 lakh cut makes higher‑capacity (59‑79 kWh) SUVs appear in the mass‑market price bracket, expanding their reach to buyers who start their search based on budget rather than powertrain. While battery subscriptions remain a small share of sales, the lower headline price serves as a powerful customer‑acquisition tool, reshaping the EV‑vs‑ICE comparison landscape

India Raises 5G EMF Limits to Global Standard

JIOFIN IDEA AIRTELPP

India’s government will double the permissible power density for 5G base stations from 5 W/m² to 10 W/m², aligning with International Commission for Non‑Ionizing Radiation Protection guidelines followed by many countries. The change, effective next month, aims to improve coverage, reduce the need for new towers, and lower capital costs for operators such as Vodafone Idea, Bharti Airtel, and Reliance Jio. The previous stricter limits, set at 1 W/m² and later 5 W/m², were considered inadequate for 5G’s higher traffic and technical demands. Telcos argue the new limits pose no health risk, citing studies and global norms

Banks Challenge Resolution Professional’s Role and Net‑Worth Computation in Subhash Chandra Insolvency Case

INDUSINDBK HMVL IDBI BCG ZEELEARN RBLBANK DHUNINV CANBK HDFCBANK DAMCAPITAL CREST

Bankers have raised serious concerns over the handling of Subhash Chandra’s 22,000‑crore borrowing case, questioning the calculation of his personal net worth, the admission of related‑party creditors, and the conduct of resolution professional (RP) Shiv Nandan Sharma. They argue that Chandra’s net worth was drastically reduced from over 40,562 crore (when guarantees were given) to 32 crore in 2025, yet no forensic audit or asset‑tracing was ordered. Related parties—entities linked to Chandra’s family—captured about 75 % of the vote on a 6.5‑crore settlement, sidelining banks, which held only 19 %. Judicial member Nilesh Sharma dismissed banks’ objections, relying on a narrow interpretation of Section 79(2)(g) of the Insolvency Code that focuses on share‑holding rather than actual control. The RP was cleared of fault, with the tribunal noting the RP lacks the broad investigative powers of a bankruptcy trustee. Banks say the case sets a dangerous precedent and may be taken up to the Supreme Court

TVS Motor Appoints International Business Head as New CEO

TVSMOTOR

TVS Motor Company has announced that Peyman Kargar, currently president of international business, will succeed K. N. Radhakrishnan as director and CEO on January 27, 2027. Radhakrishnan will remain CEO until then and become a non‑executive director until the AGM in July 2027. Kargar brings over three decades of global automotive experience; his division accounts for 29% of TVS’s sales and is growing at 33%. The move follows TVS’s FY26 sales of 5.9 million vehicles and a 30% revenue increase, as the company aims to expand further in India and mature markets

CIL Warns Coal‑Gasification Incentive Rules May Undermine Its Competitive Edge

COALINDIA

Coal India (CIL) argues that the government’s 37,500‑crore coal‑gasification incentive scheme is biased toward private firms because of a brief 60‑day initial bidding window and the exclusion of captive power‑plant costs from eligible project expenses. CIL sought a six‑month application period, noting that only firms with ready DPR/PFR can compete effectively, and questioned whether captive power plants using washery rejects could count toward project costs. The ministry rejected both requests, stating captive plants are not part of the project definition, potentially limiting CIL’s ability to leverage its existing coal and washery assets in upcoming bids

Food Safety Concerns Prompt NRAI Call for Problem‑Focused Enforcement

PML NDTV TOYAMSL PNB DODLA ASHOKLEY

India’s top restaurant chains—including Paul, Big Chill, and Punjab Grill—are under scrutiny for hygiene lapses such as unhygienic kitchens, expired stock, mislabeling, and improper segregation of vegetarian and non‑vegetarian foods. The National Restaurant Association of India (NRAI) president Sagar Daryani urges a focus on fixing issues rather than assigning blame, stressing fair, procedure‑driven enforcement under the Food Safety and Standards Act. Daryani advocates collaborative regulation to improve public health outcomes without undue reputational damage

California Gov. Newsom’s Wildfire Liability Shield Blocked

ABMINTLLTD PGEL

Governor Gavin Newsom’s effort to bar insurers from suing publicly traded utilities for wildfire-related payouts—known as subrogation—was rejected by the state Senate, halting a plan to protect PG&E, Edison International, and Sempra from massive fire liabilities. The setback triggered sharp stock declines for the utilities and raised concerns that insurers might raise premiums. Lawmakers must finalize any policy changes before the legislative session ends, while the governor continues to push for faster victim payments, executive bonus bans, and restrictions on hedge‑fund speculation in wildfire claims

Vodka’s Rapid Rise in India’s Spirits Market

RADICO

Vodka’s share in India’s liquor market has jumped from 3.8% in 2025 to 6.1%, marking the biggest gain for a white spirit in the country. Driven by younger consumers and women, as well as flavored and mixable products, the growth has come largely at the expense of whisky, rum and brandy. Radico Khaitan, holding about 60% of the vodka segment, reports its Magic Moments vodka sales up 43% YoY to 3.25 million cases. The company’s premium‑push strategy—expanding its upscale whisky range and reducing reliance on low‑priced liquor—has boosted its prestige‑segment contribution to 52% of the portfolio, while still maintaining regular‑price offerings where margins allow. This shift gives India significant headroom, as global vodka accounts for ~28% of spirits, far above India’s current 6% share

IGL Raises Delhi‑NCR CNG Price by Rs 3.89/kg Amid West Asia LNG Surge

IGL

Indraprastha Gas Limited increased CNG rates to Rs 86.98 per kg (Rs 95.59 in Noida/Ghaziabad, Rs 95.47 in Meerut, Rs 92.01 in Gurugram), marking the fifth hike this year and the first since May. The hike reflects higher imported LNG costs driven by the West Asia conflict, and is expected to raise freight and passenger fares for auto‑rickshaws, taxis, and goods carriers across the region
