Retail Investors’ FOMO and Market Rotations: Lessons from the South Korean AI Rally

OFSS

The episode explores why retail investors repeatedly buy at market peaks, using the recent 40% drop in South Korea’s KOSPI as a case study. Hosts Alex and Kirtan Shah explain that hype‑driven FOMO, over‑concentration (half the index weight in Samsung and SK Hynix), and leveraged ETFs amplified losses for unsophisticated investors. They stress that winners rotate across asset classes, countries, market caps, and sectors, so chasing a single theme—especially AI‑driven tech—is risky. Key take‑aways include: avoid leverage; diversify broadly; maintain a core domestic allocation (≈70% Indian equities) and a modest international tilt (≈15% Nasdaq, 15% gold) for currency hedge and downside protection; focus on value rather than momentum bets; and regularly assess whether decisions are driven by greed or fear. Macro cues such as rising U.S. bond yields and geopolitical tension further underscore the need for disciplined, contrarian positioning
