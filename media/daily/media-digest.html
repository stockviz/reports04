China and India Propping Up Global Coal Demand Amid Export Decline

While global coal consumption fell about 10% from 2019 to 2025, China and India drove demand up 13% and 28% respectively, keeping total demand near 9 billion tons annually. Domestic support in those countries shields miners, but exporters like Russia, Colombia, South Africa, the US, Indonesia and Australia face severe losses, with many mines unprofitable. Falling export volumes could tighten the seaborne market, temporarily lifting prices, yet the overall trend mirrors the decline of coal and LNG as transport costs and cheap renewables erode competitiveness. The industry’s future hinges on local consumption, while the broader climate goal of rapid demand reduction remains unmet

Government Eases PSC Extensions Boosting HOEC and Vedanta Oil

VOGL

The Indian government has introduced a policy allowing production‑sharing contracts (PSCs) to be extended up to the end of a field’s economic life, with a maximum term of 30 years. Extensions will be granted subject to reserve audits, development commitments and regulatory clearances, and will carry higher royalty and SES rates (e.g., an extra 10% for the first decade, 5% for the next, and 2.5% thereafter). This removes previous regulatory hurdles, potentially increasing output and earnings for companies like Hindustan Oil Exploration Co. (HOEC) and Vedanta Oil & Gas, which have fields (e.g., RAVA) under such contracts. However, the higher royalties also raise operating costs, creating a mixed impact. The policy also covers pre‑NELP blocks and smaller discovered fields, signaling a broader industry shift
