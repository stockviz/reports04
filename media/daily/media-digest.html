Volkswagen Approves Additional 50,000 Job Cuts in Major Restructuring

Volkswagen’s supervisory board endorsed a “Future Plan” that adds 50,000 jobs to the 50,000 already being cut, roughly 8% of its workforce, while targeting a 9% operating margin by 2030. The plan calls for up to a 50% reduction in its vehicle lineup by 2035, explores alternative uses for several plants but rules out immediate closures, and commits €135 bn to capex and R&D through 2031. Labor representatives secured guarantees against forced layoffs through 2030 and maintained co‑determination rights, while Porsche SE backs the accelerated transformation

Carrefour’s New India Playbook: Franchise‑Driven Expansion with 50 Stores Planned

BCG

After a decade-long hiatus, Carrefour re‑enters India through a franchise partnership with Dubai‑based Apparel Group, opening its first 50,000‑sq‑ft store in Noida. The retailer plans to roll out about 50 stores across the National Capital Region and Uttar Pradesh over the next 3‑4 years, later expanding to other regions, focusing on clusters of 5‑10 stores. Unlike its earlier cash‑and‑carry model, Carrefour will prioritize supermarkets and hypermarkets, leveraging its global scale, private‑label offerings, and Made‑in‑India products. While quick commerce is on the agenda, it will be tested later using existing stores as fulfilment points rather than launching dark‑store networks immediately. The partnership reflects a shift from equity‑owned stores to a franchise model to navigate India’s retail regulations and evolving consumer habits

PI Industries pivots to in‑house innovation and diversification amid agro‑chem challenges

PIIND KOTAKBANK

PI Industries, facing falling agro‑chemical prices, Chinese competition, geopolitical risks and a 39% Q1 profit drop, is shifting from contract manufacturing to high‑margin, in‑house product development. It is investing $200 million over ten years in R&D, targeting 90 new assets, with the first insecticide, Pioxaniliprole, slated for domestic launch and later exports to the US, Mexico and Brazil. The firm is also expanding into pharmaceuticals, specialty chemicals and electronics to hedge against sector volatility. Analysts have cut FY28 EPS forecasts and note lowered FY27 revenue guidance, but expect a rebound from export recovery, new launches and pharma/biological scale‑up in the second half of FY27. The strategy aims to build moats through innovation despite long gestation periods and inherent risks

India’s Regional Aviation Needs Broad Policy Support Beyond UDAN

INDIGO SHBAJRG

ATR chief Nathalie Tarnaud Laude says India’s vast untapped tier‑2/3 travel market requires profitable regional airlines backed by more airports, lower fees and tax incentives, not just the UDAN scheme. She envisions 200‑300 turboprops over the next two decades, noting that only 3 % of 4.6 billion ground trips are by air and a 70‑seat turboprop can cut fuel costs by ~45 % on 400‑nm routes. While UDAN 2.0 boosts funding, half of its routes are non‑operational due to infrastructure and financial issues. ATR’s order of 40 ATR 72‑600s from Fly91 (2027‑2032) will raise its global backlog to ~200 aircraft, but scaling will need additional production capacity and possibly Indian assembly. Success hinges on financially viable airlines, improved airport infrastructure, and supportive policies to make regional turboprop operations sustainable

Electronics Makers Cut Costs Through Material Substitution and Localisation

BLUESTARCO PGEL CROMPTON BAJAJELEC LGEINDIA VOLTAS

Indian electronics manufacturers are tackling soaring commodity prices—driven by the West Asia crisis, AI boom, and freight spikes—by replacing expensive copper with aluminium and steel, localising key materials like resin, and intensifying value‑engineering efforts. Companies such as LG Electronics India, Blue Star, and PG Electroplast report 2‑6% cost savings per product, yet margins have dropped from double‑digit to single‑digit levels. Despite three rounds of 10‑12% price hikes on major appliances and 20‑40% hikes on memory‑intensive devices, firms continue to absorb 7‑10% of costs to protect demand, while expanding local production of components like AC compressors. The overall strategy aims to sustain profitability amid rising input costs, supply bottlenecks, and a 30‑40% import reliance

Wine Fraudster Sentenced to Six Years for $97 Million Loan Scam

Stephen Burton, founder of London’s Bordeaux Cellars, was sentenced in Brooklyn to six years in prison after pleading guilty to wire‑fraud and money‑laundering conspiracies. He ran a Ponzi‑style scheme from 2017‑2019, promising investors loans secured by rare wine collections that largely didn’t exist, defrauding over 140 investors of $97 million. Victims included a woman who lost $200,000 of life savings. Burton, with a history of fraud convictions worldwide, was arrested in Morocco in 2022, extradited in 2023, and cooperated with authorities. His former partner, James Wellesley, received a ten‑year sentence

Meta will enforce a default two‑hour daily limit for teenage users on Facebook and Instagram, with parental overrides, and automatically block access from midnight to 6 a.m. Push notifications will be muted during school hours, like counts hidden, and certain filters disabled to curb social comparison. Think Gas demands 30% cut in LNG terminal fees

EFCIL SUMICHEM GUJGASLTD

City gas distributor Think Gas, backed by I Squared Capital, Osaka Gas and Sumitomo, told the PNGRB that high regasification (₹73‑104/MMBTU) and truck loading charges (₹81‑120/MMBTU), compounded by a 5% annual escalation, make third‑party LNG terminal usage unviable. The firm urged a mandatory 30% reduction across all Indian LNG terminals, arguing that “unreasonably high” boil‑off gas fees—despite no actual boil‑off—inflate costs for consumers and hinder market growth. With eight terminals (≈58.5 mtpa) under‑utilised, Think Gas cites lower fees, swift pipeline connectivity, and longer cargo evacuation windows as essential for the proposed Indian Gas Exchange (IGX) platform to succeed. The PNGRB currently does not regulate terminal regasification fees
