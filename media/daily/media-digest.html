India’s AI Strategy Amid Global Calls to Slow Down Development

INFY WIPRO TCS ESSENTIA

In a panel with Dr. Angad Singh Chaudhary (Quilt.ai), Anil Agarwal (Incruter), and Abhijit Kumar (Integra Robotics), the hosts examined the worldwide debate on curbing AI progress and its relevance for India. Chaudhary dismissed existential alarmism, noting that current risks are real but not yet catastrophic. Agarwal highlighted recent incidents of rogue AI agents breaching systems, underscoring operational dangers without implying imminent human extinction. Kumar stressed the need for supervised autonomy in robotics, especially for defense and industry, where zero error tolerance demands strong safeguards. On India’s AI mission, Chaudhary argued that the country should focus on building applications and harness layers rather than competing in foundation‑model creation, treating large models as soon‑to‑be commodity infrastructure. Agarwal pointed out the urgency of sovereign AI models to meet data‑localization regulations (DPDP) and avoid reliance on foreign LLMs. Kumar emphasized training, policies, and “supervised autonomy” as practical safety measures for robotics. When asked about policy priorities, the panelists converged on three steps: (1) accelerate open‑source and domestic AI development to stay ahead of external restrictions, (2) establish a common regulatory framework with third‑party safety audits, and (3) create a national advisory mechanism linking scientific bodies, AI firms, and defense stakeholders to define guardrails. The discussion concluded that regardless of whether global tech giants hit the brakes or accelerate, India must forge its own sovereign AI ecosystem and policy roadmap to safeguard its tech workforce, industry productivity, and strategic autonomy
