Government Drafts Airfare, Surge Pricing and Baggage Charge Rules

INDIGO

The government has filed draft regulations on domestic airfare caps, surge pricing, and excess‑baggage fees with the Supreme Court, promising finalisation within three weeks and threatening to ground non‑compliant airlines. Currently, India has no blanket fare ceiling; airlines use dynamic fare buckets, with a limited exception under the Udaan regional connectivity scheme (₹2,500 cap on designated seats for 500 km routes). The DGCA monitors fares but does not set them, and temporary caps have been imposed during crises, such as the December Indigo fare cap of ₹18,000. Critics warn that caps could force airlines to cut frequencies, redeploy aircraft or drop unviable routes, reducing capacity. Experts suggest a targeted approach—regulating excessive surge pricing during disruptions, setting benchmark limits, and enhancing price‑transparency—rather than blanket caps, to protect passengers without harming airline economics
