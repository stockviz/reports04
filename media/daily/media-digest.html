Health Insurers Request Expense‑Cap Adjustments for Mandatory Products

STARHEALTH

Standalone health insurers, led by Star Health, have urged the IRDAI to exclude mandatory government‑backed products such as motor third‑party, crop insurance and government health schemes from the premium base used to compute expense‑of‑management (EoM) limits. They argue these products involve no intermediaries and should not count toward the 25% cap, which would otherwise disadvantage them under the regulator’s proposed common EoM framework for general and health insurers. The IRDAI plans to lower expense limits to 20% over five years, with an interim target of 25% within two years, and shift the calculation from gross written premium to gross direct premium income, excluding reinsurance premiums. Currently, EoM caps stand at 30% for general insurers and 35% for standalone health insurers. The insurers seek a level playing field, citing different distribution economics between mandatory products and retail health policies

West Asia Conflict Fuels Surge in Corporate Working‑Capital Demand at Indian Banks

KOTAKBANK HDFCBANK FEDERALBNK AXISBANK RBLBANK ICICIBANK INDUSINDBK

Working‑capital demand from banks has risen 15‑20% in recent months due to higher raw‑material costs and longer shipping times caused by the West Asia conflict. Mid‑ and large‑sized firms see 12‑15% (up to 20% in some cases) higher cash‑conversion cycles, boosting short‑term borrowing. This has added to corporate loan growth in Q3, with major banks reporting strong gains: RBL Bank’s corporate loan book up 58% YoY, Axis Bank 34%, ICICI Bank 30.6%, Kotak Mahindra 25%, Federal Bank 17%, HDFC Bank 16.4% and IndusInd 10%. The growth is primarily driven by working‑capital needs

India’s CBG expansion hits manure market bottleneck as plants struggle to sell organic fertilizer

India’s push to scale compressed biogas (CBG) faces a critical hurdle: while plants can generate clean gas, they produce large volumes of solid and liquid manure that lack a reliable off‑take market. With only 217 of 1,908 registered CBG plants commissioned and many more under construction, operators risk mounting storage costs, reduced capacity, and shutdowns. Farmers prefer subsidised chemical fertilizers, and recent government manure‑sale incentives have been withdrawn, threatening the sector’s financial viability and jeopardising continued state support

Unauthorised cross‑border crypto transfers: ED raids Mudrex and Instadapp

The Enforcement Directorate (ED) raided three premises in Bengaluru and one in Madhya Pradesh on Oct 26 under FEMA, alleging that Mudrex and Instadapp facilitated unauthorised crypto‑based cross‑border money transfers worth over ₹4,900 crore. The probe claims Mudrex advertised instant overseas transfers without RBI approval and used crypto tokens to bypass the regulated foreign‑exchange system. Instadapp, a DeFi platform, is accused of similar violations, with preliminary findings showing contraventions amounting to about $40 million (₹380 crore). The ED’s investigation focuses on potential breaches of FEMA, including transfers involving tax‑haven jurisdictions

Rising Diesel Prices Threaten Higher Gold Production Costs for Agnico Eagle

DECNGOLD

Agnico Eagle warned that higher diesel prices could add $50‑$60 per ounce to its gold production cost in Q4, up from $30 previously, potentially raising 2027 costs by up to $80 per ounce. Diesel makes up about 11% of operating expenses, and while the company’s Canadian mines benefit from hydro‑electric and nuclear power and some fuel hedging, it still faces broader cost pressures from labor, sulfur, trade tariffs, and geopolitical supply disruptions. This could push its all‑in sustaining costs toward the top of its $1,400‑$1,550 per ounce guidance range

Tata Group Governance Disputes: Chandrasekaran Reappointment and Mehli Mistry Challenges

NSIL TVSMOTOR ONEPOINT

A boardroom clash has erupted over N Chandrasekaran’s five‑year reappointment as Tata Sons executive chairman. Noel Tata, representing the Tata Trusts, claims procedural violations—lack of agenda inclusion, missing Selection Committee, and insufficient trustee votes—demanding meeting recordings, minutes, and disclosure of a TVS Motor transaction involving Chandrasekaran’s family. Tata Sons counters that the resolution was valid, citing legal opinions and asserting no disclosure breach. Simultaneously, former trustee Mehli Mistry, whose term ended after opposition from three trustees, has appealed to the Maharashtra Charity Commissioner over alleged governance lapses and conflicts of interest at the Sir Dorabji Tata Trust and the Bai Hirabai Jamsetji Tata Navsari Charitable Institution. He also questions Noel Tata’s eligibility as a trustee under Zoroastrian requirements. The trusts reject his standing, seeking a preliminary dismissal before substantive review. Both disputes center on adherence to board procedures, voting rules, and transparency of disclosures

IndiGo’s Q2 Loss Spurs Fare Hike Justification Amid Fuel Surge

INDIGO

IndiGo posted a Rs 1,879.1 crore (≈$20.5 bn) loss for the quarter ended 30 Sept, though revenue rose 26% to Rs 233.64 bn and expenses jumped 19.2% to Rs 263.57 bn, with fuel accounting for 37.8% of costs. CEO Willie Walsh said fare increases are unavoidable and the airline will tweak capacity—cutting more international seats while keeping domestic demand strong for the festive period. Passenger revenue per available seat kilometre (PRASK) is expected to rise over 25% YoY, yield climbed to 6.30 from 4.69, but load factor fell to 80.3% from 82.5%. The carrier projects low‑single‑digit capacity growth in Q4 and a measured network plan responsive to demand, fuel prices and geopolitics

Weil Gotshal & Manges Plans Wind‑Down Amid Partner Exodus

GRAUWEIL

Weil, Gotshal & Manges, a storied New York firm with $2 billion in 2023 revenues, has recommended winding down after a cascade of partner defections and a failed merger. The partnership must still vote on the plan, and the firm will pursue an out‑of‑court wind‑down with restructuring advisers, avoiding bankruptcy if solvency is maintained. Hundreds of associates and dozens of partners will flood the legal job market, while rival firms race to recruit them. Weil must honor professional duties to clients such as Apple and Goldman Sachs, continue paying staff for 90 days plus 2026 bonuses, and settle obligations to landlords, lenders, and other creditors. Its biggest challenge will be retaining and placing its lawyers and staff while ensuring continuity of client service and meeting financial and contractual commitments

Nike Launches Vegam Run: India’s First Dedicated Running Shoe

Nike’s Vegam Run, priced at ₹3,795, is the brand’s first running shoe tailored for Indian athletes. Developed with local runners, it features an engineered mesh upper, full‑length foam cushioning, and a durable textured rubber outsole for breathability, comfort, and all‑day versatility. Available online via Flipkart and select stores from November 1, the shoe aims to support India’s fast‑growing running community with an affordable, performance‑focused option
