FNP Accelerates Quick‑Commerce Push and Prepares for FY29 IPO

EXCEL

Ferns N Petals, after initially avoiding quick‑commerce, is now expanding its presence on instant‑delivery platforms, boosting its quick‑commerce revenue from ₹8 cr in FY25 to ₹65 cr in FY26 and targeting ₹125 cr this year. The company is investing in procurement, cold‑chain and AI to improve margins, aiming for quick‑commerce to represent 20‑25% of sales. It plans FY27 revenue of ₹1,400 cr and a FY29 IPO at ₹2,200‑2,400 cr turnover. FNP’s product mix now includes fresh and artificial flowers, cakes, hampers and a premium Luxe brand, with overseas operations in the Gulf contributing ₹500 cr. After a loss post‑2023 funding, the firm returned to profitability with ~3% EBITDA margin, focusing on technology and cost control while expanding company‑owned stores in metros and franchising elsewhere. The organized gifting market in India is seen as having strong growth potential

Ekart Leverages Its Core Logistics Network to Power Flipkart’s Quick‑Commerce Expansion

NYKAA 3MINDIA

Ekart, Flipkart’s in‑house logistics arm, has repurposed its existing fulfillment centres to feed over 1,000 dark‑stores for the Flipkart Minutes quick‑commerce service, enabling 10‑minute to 2‑day deliveries across 130 cities, including tier‑III and IV towns. By treating fulfillment centres and dark‑stores as a single supply chain, Ekart reuses warehousing, line‑haul and last‑mile capacity, achieving 60‑70% automation (targeting 80‑90%) and cutting unit costs by ~40%. While the network gives Minutes a geographic head start, experts warn that true competitive advantage will depend on building dark‑store density, hyperlocal demand forecasting, and dedicated resources to avoid operational strain during peak periods. The franchise model now opens the supply chain to MSMEs, D2C, and FMCG brands, aiming for 1,000 outlets by end‑2026

Ratan Tata Endowment Fund’s Cash, Share and Loan Dealings with Tata Group Raise Governance Concerns

NSIL PAISALO TATAPOWER TATATECH INDHOTEL TATAMOTORS TATACAP

A Mint investigation reveals that the Ratan Tata Endowment Fund (RTEF), set up in 2022 per Ratan Tata’s will, received ₹45 crore in cash, a 1% stake in Tata Digital, and an unsecured ₹147 crore loan from Tata Group entities, all approved by board resolutions but undisclosed in Tata Sons’ annual reports. Using the loan, RTEF bought Tata Technologies shares at a discount, sold part of them in July 2024 for a ₹158 crore profit, and repaid the loan with interest. The fund, chaired by Natarajan Chandrasekaran since February 2025, is slated to receive more than half of the 0.83% Tata Sons shares Ratan Tata owned, but a complaint over those shares has triggered a Maharashtra charity commission probe. Experts question why a philanthropic endowment depends on group companies for funding, the lack of public disclosure, and potential advantages over minority shareholders. The fund’s FY25 income of ₹164 crore is largely derived from the Tata Technologies share sale, with minimal other earnings

Tata Communications targets $1 billion data‑centre connectivity market in India

TATACOMM DATAPATTNS TCS BCG KOTAKBANK ISEC

Tata Communications sees a $1 bn opportunity in linking the country’s rapidly expanding data‑centre capacity, aiming to capture a larger share of intra‑data‑centre networking and managed services. Managing director Ganesh Lakshminarayanan plans to boost digital‑first revenues, improve EBITDA, and leverage the firm’s network, cloud, and security offerings despite a recent profit dip. Analysts are optimistic, projecting digital services revenue could reach ₹28,000 cr by FY27‑28, while the company’s diversified connectivity (subsea, terrestrial, satellite) mitigates risks from undersea cable disruptions. The firm holds a 26% stake in ST Telemedia’s Indian data‑centre unit after selling the majority of its own data‑centre assets

Ellisons Brace for $9.8 Billion Hit if Paramount‑Warner Deal Falls Apart

LGBBROSLTD 5PAISA DELTACORP PARACABLES

Larry Ellison and his family could be liable for a $9.8 billion payout—$7 billion termination fee plus a $2.8 billion Netflix settlement—if Paramount’s acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery collapses over regulatory challenges. The fee would be covered by Ellison purchasing new Class B shares at $16.02 each, far above Paramount’s current $8 share price. Paramount has delayed closing until June or until lawsuits from 12 states and the Writers Guild are resolved, and it will not need extra borrowing for the fees. If the deal succeeds, ongoing quarterly “ticking” fees of about $650 million will also be backed by Ellison’s equity commitment. The potential loss comes as Ellison’s fortune has fallen 31% this year, partly due to concerns over Oracle’s AI and data‑center spending
