Satyaki Ghosh’s Turnaround Blueprint for Raymond Lifestyle

RAYMOND RAYMONDLSL

New CEO Satyaki Ghosh aims to revive Raymond Lifestyle by consolidating operations, closing underperforming stores, and focusing on “premiumisation” (higher‑margin luxury shirts, suits) and “casualisation” (growth brands like ColorPlus and Park Avenue). The plan leverages Raymond’s integrated value chain for better quality and margins, expands high‑growth casualwear and e‑commerce (now 12‑13% of sales), revamps the Ethnix wedding‑wear model to made‑to‑measure, and strengthens emerging businesses (innerwear, home, sexual‑wellness). Export growth, especially in garmenting, is also targeted, with a goal to double growth quality over the next five years while restoring shareholder confidence

Instant‑Help Apps Face Profitability Challenges as They Expand into Low‑Density Areas

URBANCO

Instant‑help platforms such as Snabbit, Pronto and Urban Company’s InstaHelp rely on high worker utilization in dense micro‑markets, where professionals can complete 8‑10 jobs per 9.5‑hour shift with travel distances under 300‑400 m, keeping service costs near ₹95‑100 per hour. In lower‑density neighbourhoods, travel distances rise to 1‑3 km, cutting utilization and forcing the model to break unless prices double to ₹180‑200 per hour. Companies are attempting to improve utilization by clustering jobs, surge/peak pricing, and targeting high‑demand clusters, but average order value is falling (Urban’s AOV dropped to ₹138) and per‑order EBITDA losses remain high. Expansion into less‑dense areas may require new pricing levers (subscriptions, distance fees, premium services) and investment in personal mobility to reduce travel time. Profitability is projected only when utilization reaches 75‑80% and prices rise significantly, shifting more costs to customers while maintaining worker earnings of ₹35‑40 k per month

AI‑Driven Outsourcing Cut Hits Wipro as Meta Scales Back Digital Marketing Spend

WIPRO TANLA BIRLACORPN

Meta has trimmed at least 25% of its outsourced IT work to Wipro, reducing annual revenue from the account to about $75 million and benching roughly 200 Gurugram staff. The cut follows Meta’s AI‑led shutdown of its digital marketing arm and a broader move to internalize capabilities, affecting other vendors too. For Wipro, this loss follows a similar hit from Estée Lauder and comes as the company struggles to reverse declining revenues under new CEO Srini Pallia, with analysts expecting flat or modestly negative growth in FY27 amid industry‑wide pressures from automation and constrained demand

Global Insurers Eye India’s 100% FDI Opening

HDFCLIFE M&M LIBERTSHOE GODIGIT PRUDMOULI LICI JIOFIN DABUR BCG

Global insurers such as South Korea’s Hanwha and South Africa’s Discovery are assessing entry or expansion in India following the government’s lift of the foreign ownership cap to 100%. The move follows earlier deals by Allianz‑Jio and Prudential‑Bharti AXA and prompts existing foreign JV partners (e.g., QBE, Aviva, Liberty Mutual) to increase stakes or buy out Indian partners. With the Indian insurance market’s AUM at $780 bn, premiums around $125 bn, and penetration only 3.7%, the sector promises strong growth, higher household incomes and regulatory reforms. However, a limited pool of scalable insurers and valuation gaps pose challenges, pushing some entrants toward greenfield builds or smaller acquisition targets, especially in non‑life, health and distribution assets. Transactions are expected to pick up over the next 12‑18 months as supply and price align

India’s Top Firms Trim Fresh Hires Amid Automation and Economic Uncertainty

TATASTEEL TECHM SBIN AIRTELPP HDFCBANK DELTACORP RELIANCE ASIANPAINT ADANIPORTS KOTAKBANK AXISBANK

A Mint analysis of 30 Sensex companies shows a 27% drop in new hires between FY24 and FY26, with nine firms hiring fewer staff while only two increased hiring. Most firms cite higher productivity, cost controls, and automation—especially AI‑driven tools—reducing the need for transactional roles. Public sector banks SBI and Power Grid buck the trend, reporting significant hiring spikes in FY26. Geopolitical tensions, such as the West Asia conflict, further dampen hiring momentum. The shift also reflects a move toward higher‑skill, higher‑pay positions, raising revenue per employee but curbing overall headcount growth

Toyota Faces Profit Decline Amid Weak Sales, Rising Costs, and Earthquake Disruptions

LEXUS

Toyota is expected to post a fifth consecutive quarterly profit drop, with operating profit projected at 1.11 trillion yen, down 5% YoY. The decline is driven by a 3% fall in global sales (notably a 28% slump in China and a one‑third drop in the Middle East), higher material costs linked to the Middle East conflict, and production halts at four Japanese plants due to a recent Kyushu earthquake. Analysts will watch for updates on the earthquake’s impact, potential changes to the full‑year profit outlook, and the rollout of the new RAV4 model

Prysmian Nears $4‑Billion Cash Deal for Atkore

BANSALWIRE TCNSBRANDS AVONMORE DELTACORP

Italy’s Prysmian is in advanced talks to acquire U.S. electrical manufacturer Atkore for roughly $4 billion in cash, potentially finalizing the deal soon. Atkore, valued at about $2.5 billion, makes wiring routing and protection systems, while Prysmian, a €36.3 billion market‑cap cable maker, has been expanding its U.S. presence through acquisitions like Encore Wire and Channell Commercial. The transaction follows Atkore’s strategic review with Citi and JPMorgan amid activist pressure, and aligns with Prysmian’s broader growth plans, including a U.S. dual listing. The deal could reshape the U.S. electrical manufacturing landscape, though final terms and timing remain uncertain
