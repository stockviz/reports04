Air India’s International Traffic Drops 36% Amid War and Airspace Restrictions

TCIEXP TENNIND INDIGO

International passenger carriage for Air India fell 36% in the April‑June quarter, dropping to 27.6 lakh from 43.3 lakh a year earlier. The decline is tied to the US‑Iran conflict and a 14‑month closure of Pakistani airspace, which forced Indian carriers onto longer, costlier routes. Overall international travel to and from India fell 10.5% in the quarter. While Indian airlines saw passenger numbers dip (domestic carriers down 26%, IndiGo down 15%), foreign airlines such as Emirates, Etihad, Lufthansa and British Airways gained market share, with Emirates up 7% on India routes. The prolonged airspace ban remains a major challenge for Indian airlines, prompting calls for a diplomatic solution

Women Set to Inherit ₹73 Trillion: Barclays Report Highlights Investment Preferences and Advisory Needs

The Barclays Private Bank India report projects that Indian women will inherit about ₹73 trillion over the next decade, signaling a major shift in wealth control. The study, based on data from listed firms (₹1,500 crore+ market cap) and family structures, estimates inheritance distribution equally among next‑generation members. While 86 % of women leaders aim for significant wealth growth, only 14 % feel comfortable with alternative investments, indicating a knowledge and advisory gap. Women are characterized as “risk‑intelligent,” seeking calculated risks rather than being risk‑averse. Notable findings include a strong desire (≈40 %) to allocate to alternatives, a rising share of women fund managers (≈20 %), and a preference for wealth managers who act as partners rather than mere executors. The report underscores the need for tailored advice to help women capitalize on their future inheritance

Delhi High Court orders cancellation of Razenta’s “Daplogin” trademark in favor of Dr Reddy’s “Daplo

DRREDDY APLLTD

The Delhi High Court directed the trademark registrar to cancel Razenta Pharmaceuticals’ “Daplogin” mark within six weeks, finding it deceptively similar to Dr Reddy’s “Daplo” trademark. Both drugs contain the same active ingredient for treating type‑2 diabetes, and the court held that phonetic similarity creates a likelihood of confusion, violating Sections 9(1)(a), 9(2)(a) and 11(1)(b) of the Trade Marks Act, 1999. The court also recognized “Daplo” as a well‑known mark, granting Dr Reddy’s statutory and common‑law rights and deeming any use by others as infringement and passing‑off
