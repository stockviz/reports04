Air New Zealand Appoints Former Air India CEO Campbell Wilson and Ex‑CFO Robert McDonald to Board

NDTV ATHERENERG BCG TENNIND

Air New Zealand will add former Air India chief Campbell Wilson and former CFO Robert McDonald as directors, effective September 24 at its annual shareholders’ meeting. Investcorp announced a 500 crore investment in 20Cube 3PL to build a logistics platform, targeting a warehouse footprint of 20 million sq ft and further acquisitions of up to 750 crore. OnePlus Raises Prices Again Amid Falling Market Share and Retail Pullback

BEL VIVOBIOT

OnePlus has increased smartphone prices for the fifth time in nine months, now up to 26% across its lineup, as component costs surge. Simultaneously, the brand is scaling back its physical retail presence, limiting shipments to select large retailers in southern India and cutting off over 50,000 smaller retailers. This strategy shift, alongside a narrowed product portfolio, follows a decline in market share—from 7% of overall sales in 2023 to just 2% in early 2026—and a loss of premium‑segment focus. Industry analysts attribute the downturn to OnePlus’s foray into budget devices, while rumors of a possible merger with sister BBK brands persist. Retail bodies have appealed to the government over perceived unfair practices, but OnePlus maintains it is pursuing a focused D2C and selective offline approach

Top 10 Private Banks Cut Over 10,000 Jobs in FY26 as AI Reshapes Hiring

IDFCFIRSTB INDUSINDBK RBLBANK BANDHANBNK KOTAKBANK YESBANK FEDERALBNK AXISBANK ICICIBANK HDFCBANK

The combined workforce of India’s ten largest private banks fell by 10,114 employees in FY26, a 2.6% drop to 739,452, after a 9,637‑person reduction the previous year and a sharp hiring surge in FY24. The four biggest lenders—ICICI, HDFC, Axis and Kotak Mahindra—accounted for 14,364 cuts, with ICICI shedding the most (6,633). Mid‑size banks added staff (e.g., Federal +1,570, IndusInd +1,491). Despite headcount cuts, the top four expanded their branch network by about 1,289 locations. AI and automation are driving productivity gains: Axis’s AI assistant aids 84,000 employees, ICICI runs 100+ bots, Federal automated 385 processes, and IDFC First’s voice bots outperform human agents. Recruitment slowed dramatically, attrition remained high, and banks are redeploying talent from back‑office to customer‑facing roles

Indian Refineries Face Rising Crude Costs Amid Gulf Premiums and Vanishing Russian Discounts

TIMESGTY

Indian refiners are paying higher prices than global benchmarks as Gulf and West African crude premiums climb, driven by supply constraints in the Red Sea and Strait of Hormuz. Gulf sellers demand $3‑4 per barrel over Dubai‑Oman, itself $6‑7 above Brent, pushing effective Gulf costs $10 above Brent. Term‑deal supplies are limited, forcing refiners to use spot markets and riskier shipments. Discounts on Russian oil have disappeared and Venezuelan discounts have narrowed, while U.S. sanctions threaten further Russian supply. To offset Gulf shortfalls, refiners are looking to US, Brazil, and Guyana crude

Meta Faces Multi-State Lawsuit Over Child Exploitation Claims

TANLA

A federal trial in Oakland sees 29 states suing Meta Platforms for allegedly designing Facebook and Instagram to hook children, harvest their data, and conceal risks, violating state consumer‑protection laws and the federal Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act. Prosecutors allege Meta misled parents, encouraged compulsive use, and collected data from users under 13, seeking billions in penalties that could total up to $1.4 trillion. Meta denies wrongdoing, claims it has improved safety tools, and argues broader industry responsibility. The case, focusing on platform design rather than user‑posted content, could set precedent amid growing global backlash against social‑media companies’ impact on youth

Pooled Gas Mechanism Needed as West Asia Tensions Hit India’s City Gas Supply

MGL RELIANCE ATHERENERG

City gas distributors in India are urging the government to reinstate the pooled gas mechanism introduced in March, after its July withdrawal left a supply shortfall amid renewed West Asian tensions. The pool, which offered 7‑8 mmscmd at about $11.60 per mmBtu, helped stabilize prices and ensure supplies. With domestic APM and NWG covering only 40‑50% of CGD sourcing and LNG imports disrupted by attacks on Qatar’s Ras Laffan complex, companies are turning to mid‑term purchases and awaiting market stability for longer contracts. Restoring the pool is seen as a way to cushion consumers and firms from volatility
