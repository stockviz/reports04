Human Labs’ $47 M Series A Fuels Rapid Global Expansion in Physical AI Data Infrastructure

FRONTSP HALEOSLABS

Human Labs, founded by Manish Agarwal, closed a $47 million Series A (including $26 M equity led by Axel and $21 M venture debt) after hitting $50 M ARR within six months across 20+ countries. The company captures human task data to create training‑ready datasets for robots, selling high‑quality intelligence and deployment services to robotics and foundation‑model firms. Their addressable market is a $10 B physical AI data infrastructure space, with roughly 50‑60 % currently viable. Early revenue comes mainly from the US, Europe, and LATAM, while India serves as a playbook market for scaling operations. Human Labs emphasizes consent‑driven data collection, sees a hybrid future where humans supervise multiple robot arms, and aims for $1 B annual revenue, acknowledging the need for further milestones and capital to reach that scale

US‑India Strategic Outlook: Trade Deal, Geopolitics, and the AI Surge

AMDIND

Mukesh Shagi, president of the U.S.–India Strategic Partnership Forum, discusses how recent “power‑trial” talks among the U.S., China and Russia ease pressure on India by keeping diplomatic channels open with Russia and Iran, while the Ukraine and Middle‑East conflicts remain costly for India’s oil and LPG imports. He argues the Trump administration is unlikely to rush a U.S.–India trade pact; tariff levels (10‑18 %) are being re‑evaluated amid concerns over U.S. consumer prices and the pending Section 301 investigation. From India’s side, the status‑quo of preferential tariffs is preferable while trade continues to grow. Shagi highlights the rise of Global Capability Centers (GCCs) and data‑center investments in India, emphasizing the country’s massive data pool and skilled talent as drivers of higher‑value activities beyond simple labor arbitrage. He warns Indian retail investors to be cautious about the AI “bubble,” noting massive U.S. capital inflows into firms like Anthropic and OpenAI, but stresses that returns may take 10‑15 years. Finally, he projects steady U.S.–India partnership through 2027, buoyed by India’s 8 % growth, expanding purchasing power, and a talent shortage in the U.S. that makes India an attractive hub for innovation, manufacturing, and defense collaborations

Dutch Court Blocks BYD’s Ti7 SUV in EU Over Defender Design Infringement

TATAMOTORS

A Hague district court granted JLR a preliminary injunction, barring BYD from selling or producing its Ti7 SUV in the EU, citing infringement of Jaguar Land Rover’s Defender design. The ruling highlights visual similarities such as a boxy silhouette and squared wheel arches, and prevents BYD from using its new Hungarian factory for the model. The decision, rare for early-stage design disputes, does not affect BYD’s UK affiliate. BYD plans to appeal, while JLR emphasizes protection of its intellectual property

Boba Bhai Secures $18 Million Series B to Accelerate Expansion and New Brands

BMWVENTLTD

Bengaluru‑based Boba Bhai raised $18 M in a Series B round led by Left Lane Capital, Big Capital and existing investors, after a ₹40 cr seed round in March. The valuation wasn’t disclosed but is higher than the previous round. The company, now with 120 stores and over 300,000 monthly orders, posted FY26 revenue of ₹68 cr and an ARR of about ₹150 cr, targeting FY27 revenue of ₹160‑170 cr. Plans include scaling to 150‑160 outlets by FY27 and 650‑700 outlets within three years, expanding beyond Tier‑1 cities into Tier‑2/3 markets. Funds will fuel aggressive store roll‑out, launch of new brands under Boba Bhai Ventures (e.g., Oreco Mexican Kitchen), team building, technology upgrades, and customer acquisition. About 40 % of sales come from walk‑ins (most profitable) and 60 % from delivery/quick‑commerce. Repeat‑buyer rate is 48 %. Bubble tea drives foot traffic (40 % of volume) while food accounts for 60 % of revenue. The company sees potential for international expansion in the next 12 months after consolidating the Indian market. The name “Boba Bhai” blends bubble‑tea (“boba”) with the Indian slang “bhai” to reflect a home‑grown, youth‑friendly brand

IndusInd Bank Terminates Ex‑CEO Kathpalia and Initiates Full Clawback

INDUSINDBK

IndusInd Bank has ended former CEO Sumant Kathpalia’s employment and will seek to recover all compensation earned from his start in March 2020, following a disciplinary inquiry into the accounting irregularities on derivative trades that caused a $230 million loss in 2025. Kathpalia resigned in April 2025 after the scandal, was suspended in June, and the board invoked malus and clawback provisions. The bank did not disclose the inquiry’s findings. Earlier reports indicated similar clawback actions against former deputy CEO Arun Khurana. While such clawback clauses have been allowed in Indian banking contracts since 2019, they are rarely enforced. IndusInd’s loan and deposit growth has resumed, and its share price has rebounded 16% this year after a 10% decline in 2025

HONDAPOWER

Nvidia’s Past Allegations of CEO Jensen Huang’s Abuse

Bloomberg reports that two former Nvidia employees—former in‑house lawyer Stephen Pettigrew and former HR senior VP Michael Winston—filed lawsuits in the mid‑2000s alleging that Jensen Huang verbally abused, threatened, and made sexually explicit remarks toward staff. Pettigrew claimed he was fired for raising concerns; Winston said he was dismissed after objecting to Huang’s conduct. Both cases were settled confidentially, with reported payouts exceeding $1 million. The allegations resurfaced as Huang recently resigned amid disclosures of affairs and a possible espionage investigation. While some board members and employees support his return, the board has so far rejected reinstating him. The summary notes the historical nature of the claims, the settlements, and the current controversy surrounding his potential reinstatement
