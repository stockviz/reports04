Adani Group Pursues 5 GW Pumped‑Storage Projects in Uzbekistan

BCG

The Adani Group is negotiating with the Uzbek government to develop four pumped‑storage hydropower projects totaling about 5 GW, potentially costing around ₹30,000 crore. The talks, held in August with officials including investment minister Laziz Kudratov and Uzbekhydroenergo’s deputy chairman, aim to bolster Uzbekistan’s renewable‑energy mix as the country seeks 50 % renewable electricity by 2030 and net‑zero by 2050. Uzbekistan’s hydropower potential (≈12 GW) is largely untapped, and pumped storage would aid grid stability and renewable integration. For Adani, the deal extends its growing storage portfolio—already about 3 GW in India and numerous projects under review—and deepens India’s strategic and commercial presence in Central Asia amid competition from China, Turkey, Iran, Saudi Arabia and the UAE. Experts note the venture could strengthen India‑Uzbekistan ties and regional energy trade

Hindustan Copper Eyes 28% Ore, 17% Metal Growth in FY27; Chile JV Progress Awaits Reserve Report

HINDCOPPER

Hindustan Copper Limited (HCL), India’s sole vertically integrated copper producer, projects 28% YoY growth in copper ore production and 17% growth in metal-in-concentrate output for FY27, targeting 4.7MMT ore (vs. 3.67MMT in FY26) and 32,000MT metal (vs. 27,400MT). Key drivers for 81% YoY revenue growth in Q1FY27 included: - 14% YoY ore production rise and 16% higher metal-in-ore/concentrate quality. - 21% growth in metal-in-concentrate sales and a LME copper price uptick ($13,300/MT vs. $12,800/MT YoY). - Flat costs (low fuel dependency; renewable energy integration) led to 163% YoY net profit growth.

Production Growth Initiatives: - Khetri Copper Complex (Rajasthan): Kolihan mine recovery post-accident; Khetri mine expansion. - Malanjkhand (MP): 75% revenue contributor, targeting 10% production growth. - Indian Copper Complex (Jharkhand): Surda mine fully operational; Kendadih mine restart; Rakha mine reopening by Dec 2026.

Chile JV with CODELCO: - Four copper blocks shortlisted; transaction advisor’s reserve report expected in 2–3 months. - Joint venture formation in progress (legal/structural work underway); potential for other Indian PSUs to join. - Reserves may take 6–8 years to develop if mature; seen as a strategic mineral security boost.

MMDR Amendment Act, 2026: - HCL welcomes the act for predictable fiscal framework, reduced compliance burden, and uniform royalty/tax regimes. - Aims to enhance domestic mineral supply, attract investment, and reduce import dependence for critical minerals like copper. - Highlights benefits for small miners, tech adoption, and sustainable employment in mining regions

Kotak Banker Pushes Shift from Loans to Bonds for Corporate Funding

FEDERALBNK KOTAKBANK

Paritosh Kashyap, a senior Kotak Mahindra Bank director and potential CEO candidate, argues that India’s corporate borrowing must move from bank loans to the bond market to free up bank capital and spread risk. While banks still dominate financing—especially for non‑AAA/AA+ issuers—only about 21% of debt is in corporate bonds, far below China and the US. Kashyap notes Kotak’s growing “credit‑substitute” investments (now 7.6% of assets) and cites higher yields on commercial paper during volatile periods as a reason to favor debt‑market exposure, which also sidesteps priority‑sector lending mandates. He stresses that a deeper, more mature bond market and stronger borrower credit profiles are needed for a broader shift, acknowledging that the transition will be gradual. Kashyap is among the internal contenders to replace CEO Ashok Vaswani

Noel Tata challenges legality of N. Chandrasekaran’s reappointment as Tata Sons chairman

NSIL

Tata Trusts chairman Noel Tata alleges that the Tata Sons board illegally reappointed N. Chandrasekaran for a third term by using the wrong constitutional provision and by ignoring agreed safeguards. He says the board applied Article 118 in 2022 but switched to Article 121 in 2026, despite board members being promised a legal opinion before any public disclosure. Noel cites minutes, a legal opinion from former Chief Justice D.Y. Chandrachud (favoring a majority of Trust‑nominee directors) and argues that the casting vote by chairman Harish Manwani cannot overturn the required affirmative vote of Trust nominees. The board instead relied on another lawyer’s opinion, announced the decision prematurely, and failed to obtain shareholder approval at an AGM, rendering the resolution null and void. Tata Trusts and governance analysts echo Noel’s view, calling the move a breach of the Articles of Association and a dangerous precedent for corporate India

Four Hours at Bombay House: How Five Tata Sons Directors Outflanked Noel Tata

TRENT HINDUNILVR TCS NSIL

In a tense September 17 board meeting at Tata Sons, Noel Tata—chair of Tata Trusts, which owns 65.9 % of the holding company—found himself isolated against the other five directors. After a brief pre‑meeting showdown with Harish Manwani, the board discussed two critical agenda items: the RBI’s directive to list Tata Sons and the re‑appointment of chairman N. Chandrasekaran for a third five‑year term. Despite Noel’s legal brief citing a 40‑page opinion by former Chief Justice D.Y. Chandrachud and his insistence that a Trust‑nominated director’s dissent should block any decision, the other directors, led by Manwani, relied on an alternative lawyer’s view (Sudipta Sarkar) that allowed the vote to proceed. Four directors voted in favor of Chandrasekaran’s continuation; Noel was the sole dissenter. The board also agreed to “take steps” to comply with the RBI’s listing order without a detailed discussion. After the meeting, an email from the company secretary confirmed the re‑appointment and a press release announced both outcomes. Noel subsequently issued multiple statements branding the board’s actions illegal and engaged senior counsel Abhishek Manu Singhvi, while Chandrasekaran’s side retained former solicitor general Harish Salve. The episode highlights a rapid, high‑stakes power struggle within the Tata group, pitting the Trusts’ controlling shareholder against a majority of independent directors
