UK‑India Trade Deal Slashes Scotch Prices in India by 10‑15

WELSPUNLIV PML PIONDIST

Following the July 15 India‑UK free trade agreement, Diageo and Pernod Ricard have begun passing reduced tariffs into retail prices, cutting John nie Walker Black Label to about ₹3,800 from ₹4,250 and J&B to ₹1,700 from ₹2,300 in key states (Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Goa, Uttar Pradesh). The tariff on British whisky fell from 150% to 75% (eventually to 40% over ten years), prompting price‑reduction proposals to state excise authorities. The moves, the first major consumer‑price response, could narrow the gap with Indian single malts and intensify competition in the premium whisky segment. Pernod Ricard is seeking further cuts for Chivas, Ballantine’s, and The Glenlivet, while local producers await market impact post‑festive season

Supreme Court Limits HC‑Ordered Forensic Audit to Fortis Transactions

FORTIS AHCL AXISBANK YESBANK

The Supreme Court upheld the Delhi High Court’s order for a forensic audit of banks and financial institutions in the Daiichi Sankyo case, but clarified that the audit will focus solely on transactions among Fortis Healthcare Ltd, its holding company, and former promoters Malvinder and Shivinder Singh. It ruled out a broad “fishing and roving” inquiry into the entire affairs of the 17 banks involved, specifying that the audit will examine only bank records related to the disputed Fortis share transactions. The High Court’s directive for a comprehensive, court‑monitored audit by S Ramanand Aiyar & Co will thus be confined to the commercial dealings between the judgment debtors and the banks, not a wider investigation of the banks’ overall operations

Air India’s 10,000‑crore fundraise faces lender scepticism over cashflows

NSIL PNB CRISIL TENNIND BANKBARODA SBIN BCG

Air India is looking to raise roughly ₹10,000 crore in fresh bank loans to fund fleet expansion, aircraft refurbishment and operating costs. Despite its AAA rating and strong Tata Group backing, private banks are demanding clearer visibility on cash‑flows and the airline’s turnaround plan before extending new credit, while state‑owned banks are more willing to renew existing facilities. The airline’s consolidated loss more than doubled to ₹22,238 crore in FY 2026, driven by a sharp rise in foreign‑exchange losses. Tata Sons, holding a 74.9% stake, has approved a fresh capital infusion of over ₹10,000 crore and promises to retain at least a 51% share, but lenders fear loan repayment may rely on continued equity injections. Air India’s total debt stands near ₹40,000 crore, with State Bank of India as the largest creditor. The fundraise comes as the carrier strives to become profitable after its 2021 acquisition, positioning Tata as India’s second‑largest aviation player

Media Industry Shifts to an Attention‑Driven, Tech‑Enabled Model

ZEEL SUNSHINE PFOCUS

Senior executives at India’s Ficci Frames conclave highlighted that the media and entertainment sector is moving beyond the TV‑vs‑digital split toward an attention economy powered by technology, data, and AI. CEOs of Zee Entertainment, JioStar, Sony Pictures Networks India, and Amazon Prime Video emphasized integrating content with tech, building homegrown IPs, personalising experiences, and monetising both attention and intent. They noted a strategic pivot from spending on rights acquisition to developing IP, with connected TV, non‑fiction, and regional content emerging as key growth drivers. The industry must adopt new monetisation formats and redefine success metrics to capture shrinking audience attention spans

Britannia Announces Leadership Shuffle: New Deputy MD and CFO Appointments

EICHERMOT GODREJCP BRITANNIA HAL

Britannia Industries has appointed its current CFO, N Venkataraman, as Deputy Managing Director effective October 1, while Ramamurthy Jayaraman will take over as CFO. The changes, announced under CEO Rakshit Hargave, follow a broader trend of FMCG firms promoting finance chiefs to top roles. Venkataraman, with Britannia since 2007, will now oversee finance, strategy, legal, secretarial and IT functions, while Jayaraman moves up from Vice President of Corporate Finance to lead the entire finance function

Essar‑Backed Mesabi Metallics to Build $15 Billion U.S. Steel Plant

ESSARSHPNG

Essar‑backed Mesabi Metallics will invest $15 billion to construct the largest U.S. steel complex in Iowa, producing 10 million tonnes annually using iron ore from its newly opened $3 billion mine in Minnesota. Combined with the mine, the project totals $18 billion, the biggest single‑site steel investment in the country. The plant will use electric‑arc furnace technology with direct‑reduction iron pellets, promising lower carbon emissions. President Donald Trump praised the fully domestic “100% American” steel operation, noting it benefits from high tariffs on imported steel. Rewant Ruia, chairman, emphasized the integrated, “made‑in‑USA” approach
