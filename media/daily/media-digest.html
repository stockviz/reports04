India’s Critical‑Mineral Gap: From Rich Deposits to Domestic Manufacturing

LT RBA COALINDIA

India’s abundant critical‑mineral reserves remain under‑utilised due to a lack of mid‑stream processing and refining capacity. The 2025 China rare‑earth export curtailment exposed the nation’s dependence on imports for lithium, cobalt, nickel, copper and permanent‑magnet materials. While the government’s National Critical Mineral Mission targets 1,200 exploration projects, self‑sufficiency in processing five minerals, 1,000 patents and four regional processing parks by FY31, major bottlenecks persist: commercial‑scale lithium carbonate/hydroxide production, rare‑earth extraction‑to‑metal conversion, and battery‑grade graphite refining. Recent initiatives include a ₹7,280 cr magnet‑capacity scheme, dedicated rare‑earth corridors, and a ₹1,500 cr recycling programme, alongside international partnerships for technology transfer. Experts stress the need for “bankable” large‑scale plants, long‑term purchase commitments, and layered financing to turn ore resources into high‑value domestic products and secure India’s EV, renewable‑energy and semiconductor ambitions

AI Deflation in Indian IT: Optimism vs. Caution as Leaders Clash Over Revenue Pressures

TCS TECHM WIPRO HCLTECH

Nearly four years after ChatGPT sparked the generative AI boom, India’s top IT executives remain divided over whether AI-driven revenue deflation—where automation reduces demand for legacy services like coding and maintenance—has peaked or is still worsening. Their conflicting views reflect broader industry struggles to offset shrinking contracts with AI-native growth.

  • HCL Technologies (led by CEO C. Vijayakumar) warns deflation is in its early phases, citing persistent erosion despite $219M in AI revenue (up 28% sequentially). The company remains cautious about pricing and scaling automation.
  • TCS and Wipro suggest the worst may be over, with TCS CEO K. Krithivasan estimating half the deflationary impact has already materialized, while Wipro CFO Aparna Iyer expects an inflection point in a few quarters as multi-year contracts renew.
  • Tech Mahindra dismisses deflation as overstated, framing AI-driven productivity demands as an evolution of past trends (e.g., consolidation, agile, automation).

Financial performance remains modest: TCS (+0.2% QoQ), HCLTech (+2%), Wipro (-0.1%), and Tech Mahindra (+2%) all saw single-digit growth, with AI revenues (where disclosed) growing faster but not yet compensating for legacy declines. Analysts like BMO’s Keith Bachman warn that rising efficiency hurdles will require more AI-related work to offset losses.

The debate underscores structural uncertainty: clients demand automation, legacy contracts shrink, and AI revenue streams are still nascent. Global peers like Accenture echo the trend, calling deflation predictable but persistent, while Bank of Baroda Capital expects 12–24 months of compression as firms self-cannibalize to retain clients. Investor sentiment remains jittery, with IT shares underperforming broader markets

Private sector banks in India—including ICICI Bank, HDFC Bank, and Axis Bank—experienced a year-on-year decline in net interest margins (NIMs) in Q2 due to rising funding costs from foreign currency non-resident (bank) deposits (FCNR(B)). Analysts attribute the drop to higher deposit costs and lower margins from leveraged FCNR(B) funds, which offer slim returns (50–60 basis points abroad). These deposits also displaced lower-cost CASA (current and savings account) deposits, further pressuring margins.

  • HDFC Bank: NIM declined 11 bps to 3.16% (from 3.27%), citing asset repricing delays and FCNR(B) costs. CEO Sashidhar Jagdishan noted margins may improve as the rate cycle shifts and lending shifts toward retail/MSME segments.

Banks anticipate continued margin pressure until FCNR(B) effects stabilize, though long-term retail loan growth could offset losses
