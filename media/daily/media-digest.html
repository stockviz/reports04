RBI’s Draft Ban on Revolving Credit for NBFCs – Impact Assessment and Market Outlook

TV18BRDCST BAJFINANCE TATACAP IIFL

In a CNBC TV18 round‑table, experts Krishnan Chaitaraman (Chrisil) and Vinit Jay (Careage) discussed the Reserve Bank of India’s consultation paper that seeks to prohibit NBFCs from offering revolving credit facilities, aiming to curb loan ever‑greening. They agree that such products—flexi‑loans, line‑of‑credit facilities—constitute a small share of NBFC portfolios (e.g., Bajaj Finance ~15‑20%, Tata Capital ~10%) and thus pose limited systemic risk. Krishnan expects minimal disruption, noting NBFCs’ history of quickly adapting to regulatory changes, though a transition period may cause a few basis‑point rise in NPAs. Vinit highlights that the RBI’s definition of “term loan” (fixed principal, defined repayment schedule) could also affect margin‑funding, loan‑against‑property, and demand‑loan products, pending clarification. Both foresee only modest, short‑term NPA upticks, primarily reflecting existing borrower stress rather than new defaults. The panel also noted that housing finance companies (HFCs) have limited exposure to revolving credit, so impact there should be minimal. Overall, credit growth remains robust (≈18‑20% YoY) driven by improved bank funding, and asset quality is largely tied to borrowers’ cash‑flow health, with micro‑finance showing resilience. The experts conclude that RBI’s move is likely targeted at specific ever‑greening cases rather than a broad sectoral clamp‑down
