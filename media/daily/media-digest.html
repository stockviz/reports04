India Cautious on Adani’s Airline Plans Over Airport‑Airline Conflict Concerns

TANAA INDIGO BCG TENNIND GMRAIRPORT

The Indian government will tread carefully on Adani Group’s proposal to launch an airline, despite its aim to break the IndiGo‑Air India duopoly. Civil Aviation Minister Ram Mohan Naidu flagged potential conflicts of interest from cross‑ownership of airports and airlines, noting a 2006 clause that limits Delhi and Mumbai airport operators to a 10% stake in any scheduled carrier. Adani Airports’ CEO Arun Bansal sought its removal, arguing the airline would extend the group’s aviation ecosystem. Officials warned that amending the clause could favor a single firm and hurt consumers, echoing opposition from airlines like IndiGo’s Rahul Bhatia. The matter requires Union Cabinet approval. Meanwhile, the government urges regional connectivity through the UDAN scheme, targeting at least five airlines with 100 aircraft each and a stringent “challenge mode” criteria for airport selection

Credit Card Issuers Shift to EMI Loans as Revolver Model Falters

SBICARD AXISBANK

Indian credit‑card issuers are moving away from traditional revolving balances, whose share of spend has dropped from ~21% to 11%, toward EMI‑based loans, merchant offers, and fee‑driven products. Consumer awareness and reminder platforms have reduced accidental revolving, squeezing interest‑bearing receivables. As a result, banks like Axis and SBI are emphasizing EMI conversions and personal‑loan‑on‑card products to grow ending net receivables and sustain margins despite a 27% CAGR in card spend. Bernstein estimates revolver balances now represent only ~2.8% of spend (down from ~7% in 2019), while interest‑earning assets have fallen to 55% of receivables

Extended Green Clearance Validity Boosts Port Development Prospects

BBOX

The Indian government has amended the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 to extend the validity of environmental (green) clearances for ports, harbours, breakwaters, and dredging projects from 10 to 20 years, with two optional five‑year extensions subject to expert committee review. The change applies to new projects and existing ones still under development, aiming to address long gestation periods, phased development, and litigation delays that previously caused clearances to lapse. Stakeholders, including the Mumbai Port Authority, Karnataka Maritime Board, and industry advisors, view the reform as a catalyst for larger domestic and foreign investments, smoother project implementation, and reduced time‑consuming re‑clearance procedures, supporting the goal of expanding port capacity to 10,000 MMTPA by 2047

India Mandates BIS Quality Standards for Smartphone Screen Protectors

OPTIEMUS

The government has amended the Electronics and Information Technology Goods (Requirement of Compulsory Registration) Order to include smartphone screen protectors under mandatory BIS quality norms, aiming to curb low‑quality imports, primarily from China, and boost domestic manufacturing. The standards take effect on April 1 2027, giving manufacturers six months to comply. Local firms like Optiemus Infracom, partnering with Corning, plan to expand capacity by 20 million units. Industry bodies expect the move to create 60,000 jobs, generate ₹2,000 crore in GST, and position India as a global hub for screen protector production
