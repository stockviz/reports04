Rabies Prevention: Timely Action Saves Lives

NDTV

Rabies is a fatal, yet fully preventable disease. Immediate wound washing, prompt post‑exposure prophylaxis (PEP) with vaccine and, when needed, rabies immunoglobulin (RIG) are essential before symptoms appear. India accounts for 36 % of global rabies deaths, with many victims, especially children, suffering due to delayed care, lack of awareness, and inadequate RIG access. Modern cell‑culture vaccines are free under the national program and require only four‑five intradermal or intramuscular doses, replacing the outdated 14‑dose regimen. Key preventive steps include: wash wounds with soap and water for at least 15 minutes, seek medical care immediately, complete the full vaccine schedule, and obtain RIG for severe category‑III bites. Misconceptions—such as assuming a single scratch is harmless or waiting for a dog to die—must be dispelled. Pet owners should keep dogs vaccinated, leashed, and well‑fed; community members should support stray‑dog vaccination, sterilisation, and proper waste management. Collective action by health authorities, veterinarians, NGOs, and the public aims to achieve a rabies‑free India by 2030
