IRDAI Proposes Major Overhaul of Insurance Distribution: Commission Caps, Dark‑Pattern Ban, and MII Framework

HGS

The IRDAI has released a 160‑page consultation paper (feedback open until 25 Oct) calling for sweeping reforms in Indian insurance distribution. Key proposals include stricter caps on commissions and expenses of management (EOM) by line of business, a ban on “dark patterns” that force consumers to share personal data before seeing product pricing and features, and the creation of Mutual Insurance Intermediaries (MIIs) – modeled on Bhima Sugam – with a 5 % commission ceiling. Former regulator Thomas Devasya views the caps as aggressive but necessary, arguing that past EOM limits alone failed to curb arbitrage and that a shift toward risk‑based capital is essential. Swami Saransharma warns that sharp commission cuts could squeeze distributor margins, reduce operational scale, and may not boost penetration as the TER reduction did for mutual funds, given the fundamental difference between investment and risk‑cover products. Both panelists note implementation challenges, especially for digital aggregators needing lead data, and stress that while the reforms aim to lower distribution costs, they could strain intermediaries unless paired with robust capital and data‑infrastructure measures

GPPL COCHINSHIP MYSORPETRO

Gujarat’s Deendayal Port Authority and Assam Petro‑Chemicals Ltd will build a 150‑tonne‑per‑day e‑methanol facility at Kandla, powered by renewable energy, water and biogenic CO₂. Purposeful Plating: Storytelling on the Plate

NILAINFRA ITC TIMESGTY INDIANB

Indian fine‑dining chefs are turning plates, cutlery and vessels into narrative tools, using bespoke, often globally sourced tableware to enhance the dining experience. With menus changing frequently—up to 900 plates for a 14‑course dinner at Tresind—chefs like Sarfaraz Ahmed, Rydo Anton, and Varun Totlani design each course to spark curiosity, evoke nostalgia, or convey place, while ensuring practicality (no sauce spilling, easy to eat). Trends such as gastrotourism, higher disposable incomes, molecular gastronomy and social media have driven this shift from simple garnishes to immersive storytelling. A consistent rulebook emerges: every element must be edible, purposeful, and not overwhelm the food; aesthetics should serve, not hinder, taste. The visual appeal now competes for attention on phone cameras, making plating the primary hook for diners, yet chefs stress that the true reveal must happen in the mouth, not on the screen

CCPA fines Amazon, Flipkart, JioMart for illegal herbicide sales

The Central Consumer Protection Authority imposed penalties of ₹10  lakh each on Amazon and Flipkart, and ₹5  lakh on JioMart for listing and advertising the unregistered “Cyclosinone Herbicide” on their platforms. The regulator ordered immediate removal of the product, a self‑audit, and compliance reports within 15 days. The action stemmed from a complaint by the Crop Care Federation of India, after the Ministry of Agriculture confirmed the chemical isn’t listed under the Insecticides Act, 1968. CCPA highlighted that the marketplaces failed to verify the product’s regulatory status, rejecting the defense that responsibility lies solely with sellers, and cited violations of the E‑Commerce Rules on unfair trade practices. The order focuses solely on the e‑commerce entities’ consumer‑rights breaches, not on the product’s scientific aspects

Northeast India’s Emerging Fashion Frontier

AHLEAST SANDESH VIPCLOTHNG

The Northeast’s rich textile heritage is fueling a new wave of contemporary fashion brands that blend traditional handloom techniques with modern design. Designers like Hannah Khiangte, Moala Longchar, Easternlight Zimik, and brands such as Homeland Elegies, Kintem, Tribe Fiction, and 11 Tareng are gaining national and international attention, aided by celebrity endorsements and social media. Despite these obstacles, the region’s designers are redefining perceptions of indigenous textiles, targeting broader markets, and building loyal customer bases, signalling a promising shift in India’s fashion landscape

India’s Ethanol Glut Sparks Hunt for New Markets

3MINDIA PRAJIND

India’s ethanol capacity has swelled to 20 bn L (with 4 bn L more this year) while the E20 blending mandate and non‑fuel uses absorb only about 14–15 bn L, leaving a surplus of nearly 7 bn L. Distilleries run at 60‑75% utilisation, and the government has paused plans for higher blends (E25/E30). Industry calls for differential pricing of blends (E10, E20, E85) and new buyers, including limited second‑generation ethanol exports to Africa and potential Nepal deals. Praj Industries proposes bio‑isobutanol (bio‑IBA) blending in diesel, which could unlock >₹3,000 cr if a 2% mandate is adopted. Non‑fuel demand (liquor, pharma) accounts for ~18.7% of usage. The oversupply persists as the E20 roadmap stays capped until Oct 2026, with full impact expected only after 2027

GEPIL TENNIND

The Federation of Indian Pilots (FIP) has written to Civil Aviation Minister K Rammohan Naidu urging that its technical submissions on the AI‑171 crash be formally considered before the Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau (AAIB) finalises its report. FIP seeks nomination as an independent technical participant in the draft report’s review, noting reports that the government may require an independent committee to examine the draft. The AAIB’s investigation into the June 12 2025 crash of Air India’s Boeing 787‑8 (VT‑ANB) in Ahmedabad, which killed 260 people, is in an advanced stage, but officials have not yet responded to FIP’s request. Earlier, FIP asked the AAIB to evaluate electrical system evidence for Boeing 787s. The aircraft, built in 2013, had 41,868 flight hours and an Airworthiness Review Certificate valid until May 2026. The AAIB has indicated the final report will be released after completing all investigative activities and international reviews
