FCNR Deposits Drag Down Private Banks’ Net Interest Margins in Q2

HDFCBANK AXISBANK ICICIBANK

Private sector banks in India—including ICICI Bank, HDFC Bank, and Axis Bank—experienced a year-on-year decline in net interest margins (NIMs) in Q2 due to rising funding costs from foreign currency non-resident (bank) deposits (FCNR(B)). NIM, a key profitability metric, reflects the difference between interest earned on loans and paid on deposits.

Analysts attribute the drop to higher deposit costs and lower margins from leveraged FCNR(B) funds, which offer slim returns (50–60 basis points abroad). These deposits also displaced lower-cost CASA (current and savings account) deposits, further pressuring margins.

  • Axis Bank: NIM fell 34 bps to 3.46% (from 3.80% YoY), with 11 bps linked to FCNR(B) leverage and liquidity management.
  • ICICI Bank: NIM dropped 12 bps to 4.18% (from 4.30%), with lingering pressure expected as FCNR(B) impacts unfold.
  • HDFC Bank: NIM declined 11 bps to 3.16% (from 3.27%), citing asset repricing delays and FCNR(B) costs. CEO Sashidhar Jagdishan noted margins may improve as the rate cycle shifts and lending shifts toward retail/MSME segments.

Banks anticipate continued margin pressure until FCNR(B) effects stabilize, though long-term retail loan growth could offset losses
