Tata Steel Shifts Focus to High‑Margin Value‑Added Products

TATASTEEL

Tata Steel Ltd is prioritizing growth in high‑margin downstream products—such as precision tubes, specialty steel wires, coated sheets, and tinplates—over aggressive crude steel capacity expansion. CEO T.V. Narendran says the company aims for 55‑60% of volumes to be downstream, with the possibility of sourcing upstream steel externally. Rising iron‑ore costs and changing market dynamics are driving this strategic shift toward value‑added steel rather than merely increasing production capacity

Bengaluru Restaurants Threaten Boycott of Swiggy and Zomato Over High Commissions

5PAISA ZOMATO SWIGGY

Bengaluru’s restaurant association has given Swiggy and Zomato an August 15 deadline to cut commissions, deductions, and unauthorized discounts, or 20,000 outlets will stop taking orders on the platforms. While Zomato says it has met with the association and will address concerns, the restaurants demand a “42‑rupee charge” on a 100‑rupee order to cover their costs. Experts note the duopoly’s dominance remains strong, with Swiggy and Zomato generating modest profit margins (≈4.5% per order) after heavy capital burn. New entrants like Rapido, offering zero‑commission delivery, lack the scale and capital to challenge the incumbents immediately, especially as growth in food delivery is slowing and investors now prioritize profitability over growth. The dispute highlights ongoing tensions over commission rates, transparency, and the limited alternatives for restaurants

Puerto Rico Approves $2 B Coastal Luxury Development Amid Environmental Opposition

ADVANIHOTR KIRLOSBROS OAL FAZE3Q

The Puerto Rican government has authorized the $2 billion Esencia project on 2,000 acres in Cabo Rojo, featuring hotels, residences, golf courses, an equestrian center, a school and a medical center. Developers claim over 75% of the site will be conserved, powered by renewables, and will use reclaimed water, pledging $40 million for public improvements. Environmental groups and local activists argue the project threatens wetlands, dunes, mangroves, and water resources, criticizing the lack of public participation. The development promises up to 17,000 jobs and aims to open its first phase by late 2029

Amazon sued over misleading seafood sustainability claims

INDOTHAI FMNL AARON THEINVEST

Consumers have filed a proposed class‑action lawsuit in Seattle accusing Amazon of greenwashing its seafood offerings, alleging that labels such as “dolphin safe,” “responsibly sourced,” “sustainable,” “wild‑caught,” and MSC certification are unsubstantiated and deceptive. The complaint says many fishing vessels are not tracked, and a significant portion of imported wild‑caught seafood isn’t responsibly sourced. The suit targets dozens of tuna, salmon and other products from brands like Bumble Bee, Chicken of the Sea, StarKist, and Amazon’s 365 line, seeking compensatory and punitive damages and restitution under Washington consumer protection laws. Amazon has not commented, and the parent companies of the seafood brands are not named as defendants

Blackstone Mortgage REIT Shares Tumble on Office‑Loan Pressures

CAPTRUST

Blackstone Mortgage Trust (BXMT) saw its shares fall up to 14%—the steepest two‑day drop since early 2020—after executives warned that $1 billion of office‑secured loans, now on a “watch list,” are likely to impair earnings in Q3. The watch‑list loans represent about 5% of the REIT’s portfolio, and the firm is cutting its office exposure (now 21% of assets) and reducing average investment sizes to diversify. Management expects to lower exposure to legacy and office loans by over 40% by year‑end
