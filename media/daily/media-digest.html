UPI MDR May Prompt Merchants to Shift to Cash, Split Transactions, or Peer‑to‑Peer Payments

NDTV

Sachin Taparia of Local Circles reports that only 17 % of 32,000 surveyed merchants find the new 0.4 % MDR on UPI payments over ₹2,000 acceptable, while acceptance jumps to 50 % if the fee drops to 0.04 %. Consequently, merchants may (1) divert consumers to cash or other payment modes, (2) break large payments into multiple sub‑₹2,000 transactions, or (3) bypass the fee by using peer‑to‑peer transfers into personal accounts instead of business accounts—a practice already seen in one‑third of merchants. While overall transaction volume might stay roughly stable, the mix could shift: peer‑to‑peer moves keep volume unchanged, cash usage could reduce UPI counts, and split transactions could inflate the number of UPI hits. Notably, transactions above ₹2,000 constitute just 5 % of total UPI payments but account for 65 % of value, making the MDR impact particularly significant. Regulators are expected to monitor split‑transaction tactics, and banks face additional pressure as ATM closures limit cash alternatives

RBI Sets Clear Rules for NBFC Principal Business and CIC Classification After Tata Sons CoR Rejection

NSIL

The RBI rejected Tata Sons’ request to surrender its certificate of registration and, within days, issued FAQs defining a “principal business” for NBFCs—financial assets must exceed 50% of total assets and income. Companies primarily engaged in agriculture, industry, trade, services, or real‑estate, with only minor financial activity, remain outside RBI regulation. For groups with multiple prospective Core Investment Companies (CICs) whose combined assets exceed ₹100 crore, each CIC must obtain a CoR, unless only one holds public funds and does not fund others. NBFCs wishing to become CICs can apply for exemptions on capital adequacy and exposure norms. Entities not meeting the 50% asset/income thresholds are not required to register as NBFCs. Tata Sons, with assets of ₹2.01 lakh crore as of March 2026, exceeds the NBFC‑Upper Layer threshold, prompting continued regulatory scrutiny and a mandated public listing
