India’s Smartphone Market Sees Festive Slowdown in Sales but Higher Value Amid Premiumization

RUBICON TIMESGTY

India’s smartphone market is experiencing a 12% dip in sales volume during the festive season, despite a 5% rise in market value, driven by higher memory costs and delayed upgrades, according to Counterpoint Research. The shift reflects a trend toward costlier devices, with demand fueled by replacements rather than first-time buyers.

Key factors supporting demand include financing options, exchange offers, and buyback programs, though deep discounts are limited. The challenge lies in the shrinking active consumer base, as higher prices deter discretionary purchases, pushing upgrades to replacement cycles.

Trade-ins and premiumization are rising, with Servify reporting a 50% spike in trade-in volumes during October-November. Upgrades in the Rs 60,000–1 lakh range rely heavily on financing, while above-Rs 1 lakh devices (including foldables) attract premium users upgrading every 12–24 months. This trend extends beyond metros to tier-2 and tier-3 cities.

Budget segment volumes (sub-Rs 10,000) are projected to fall 20% in 2026, as brands raise prices and reduce low-end offerings. Affordability remains a hurdle, making exchange values, financing, and buyback guarantees critical for festive sales

Industry concerns, discussed in a Forex Dealers Association of India (FEDAI) meeting, include implementation gaps, such as documentation requirements for smaller deals, risk of speculative rebooking, and cost allocation if contracts are canceled. Banks may now demand upfront documents even for deals below $5 million, adding complexity.

Historical parallels, like China’s 2015 forex reserve policy, show limited success in reversing currency depreciation, raising doubts about the rupee’s sustained stability post-RBI curbs
