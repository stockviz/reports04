Goldman Sachs Warns of Long‑Term Copper Supply Crunch

A Goldman Sachs Research report finds that global copper supply faces a structural, not cyclical, shortage due to declining discoveries, falling ore grades and soaring development costs. The median new mine now takes over 16 years from discovery to production, and output from existing mines is expected to fall from 2030 onward. Higher prices alone cannot spur sufficient new supply for two decades, pushing investors to value existing producers and mature projects more. Capital intensity rises ~50 % from pre‑feasibility to operation, and projected costs are 25‑90 % higher than company estimates. The firm estimates about 1 Mt/yr of new capacity could be added by 2035 from a group of growth projects, contributing roughly $20 bn in net asset value, but timing will hinge on execution, investment decisions, and cost management
