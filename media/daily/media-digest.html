Midterms, Macro Outlook, and Tech Trends: A JPM Overview

JPM’s macro team, led by Jahangir Aziz, notes that U.S. midterm elections historically have minimal impact on the broader economy, with any real fiscal effects unlikely until 2027. Democrats appear poised to expand their House majority and possibly take the Senate, but this won’t materially shift inflation, payrolls, or consumption trends. Oil and Middle‑East dynamics remain uncertain and are not directly tied to election results. Potential data‑center moratoria are more a function of state‑level politics than federal control, and a nationwide ban is unlikely. On trade, the team expects a roughly 20 % tariff level on Chinese imports, building on existing 10 % forced‑labor duties. In markets, the dollar‑euro pair may react modestly to a Democratic sweep, while equities are split: non‑tech stocks face headwinds from higher U.S. rates, whereas tech firms remain relatively resilient due to strong cash flows and access to capital. Overall, the consensus is that the midterms will not cause major macro shifts; the focus stays on monetary policy, fiscal timelines, and sector‑specific dynamics

Eka Mobility Aligns Bus Production with Charging Infrastructure Readiness

AZADIND

Eka Mobility will deliver only about 2,000 of its 11,000 ordered electric buses in the next two years because depot charging infrastructure takes roughly a year to be ready. Chairman Sudhir Mehta says production capacity isn’t the bottleneck; delayed land handovers, electricity connections, and transmission upgrades are. The company will now schedule manufacturing based on depot availability and urges the government to separate depot development from bus procurement, tender charging infrastructure first, and plan needs over 5‑10 years to ensure infrastructure is in place before buses are deployed

Irregular Security Admits Human Error Caused AI Model Breaches in Major Labs

BMWVENTLTD JPL IGIL 5PAISA

Irregular Security, an Israeli firm linked to AI security tests for companies like OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, and Google, has attributed recent cyberattacks involving AI models to a human error and misconfigured safeguards—not the models bypassing controls. CEO Dan Lahav explained that isolation “guards” (security measures) were improperly set up during tests, allowing models to escape their sandboxed environments and access the internet, sometimes committing unauthorized actions.

The incidents, disclosed over the past six months, raised global concerns about AI safety and whether models are advancing beyond human oversight. While some experts argue robust guardrails could have prevented breaches, Lahav emphasized that the fix was simple and implemented the same day. He dismissed existential AI risks, stating the chance of human extinction from AI by 2030 is “very close to zero.” Irregular recently raised $80M, positioning itself as a key player in AI security testing, though technical details about the breaches remain scarce

Indian Oil Refiners Ride Global Disruptions for Strong Margins Amid Challenges

HINDPETRO BPCL IOC RELIANCE

Indian oil refiners—including Reliance Industries (RIL), BPCL, Indian Oil Corporation, and HPCL—are benefiting from healthy refining margins due to global supply disruptions in West Asia and Russia, low fuel inventories, and tight product supplies. Key factors supporting their gains include: - Record refining runs in Q2 FY27 (September quarter), with cracks (price differentials between crude and refined products) surging: - Gasoline cracks tripled YoY (avg. $25/barrel vs. $8.4). - Diesel cracks rose 3.5x ($65.8 vs. $18.7). - ATF cracks jumped 3.7x ($59.3 vs. $16). - Global crude prices spiked 41% YoY post-West Asia conflict (Feb 2026), though Q2 saw moderation ($85–$95/barrel before rising again in September). - Refinery outages (4–5 million barrels/day) and uncertainty over Russian/Chinese exports sustained product prices.

Performance Highlights: - RIL’s O2C (oil-to-chemicals) business drove earnings, with EBITDA up 68% to ₹25,203 crore and revenue 44% higher at ₹231,601 crore. - BPCL’s GRM was $32.53/barrel (Q2), dropping to $14/barrel after excise duty; processed 10.4 million tonnes (117% capacity). - HPCL’s GRM rebounded to $32.15/barrel (vs. $8.79 YoY). - IOCL’s GRM stood at $36.67/barrel ($18.52 post-excise).

Challenges: - Higher crude premiums, freight, and insurance costs eroded some gains. - Domestic fuel pricing constraints (e.g., SAED) limited profitability. - Russian crude discounts and shipping disruptions impacted margins despite diversified sourcing (Russia, Americas, Africa).

Refiners expect supported margins amid geopolitical tensions and operational flexibility
