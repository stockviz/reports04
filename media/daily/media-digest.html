Ultraviolette Launches X‑47 Venom EV Motorcycle on New VX‑100 Platform

Ultraviolette introduced the X‑47 Venom electric motorcycle at a launch price of Rs 2.65 lakh for the first 5,000 customers, with bookings opening on Oct 22 and deliveries slated for Nov 2026. Built on the new VX‑100 architecture, the bike features the UV LIGHTFURY motor, SRB‑11 battery technology, a 343 km IDC range, 30 kW (40 hp) peak power, 155 km/h top speed, 0‑60 km/h in 2.7 s, and a 180 kg weight (20 kg lighter than its predecessor). It supports 12 kW DC fast charging (first for an Indian two‑wheeler), a 1.6 kW onboard charger, a 7‑inch TFT touchscreen, and advanced rider‑assistance systems including radar‑based safety, blind‑spot monitoring, lane‑change aid, crash alerts, dashcams, and park assist. The VX‑100 platform offers a 20 % higher energy‑density SRB‑11 battery with doubled cooling performance and 50 % faster heat dissipation, plus a pyro‑fuse for rapid fault isolation. Additional highlights are the reusable B‑MAX safety vest, VIOLETTE AI connectivity (movement, fall, towing, remote lockdown, crash alerts), and an extendable warranty up to 8 lakh km. The X‑47 Venom will debut in Europe at EICMA 2026 in Milan on Nov 3

SBI Considers Cyber Insurance Claim After Data Leak

SBIN OAL

State Bank of India is investigating a suspected breach that exposed thousands of customer records on the dark web. The bank plans to notify its insurer, Oriental Insurance, about a potential claim under a Rs 1,425‑crore cyber‑insurance policy. Coverage will depend on the policy terms, the incident’s nature, and any qualifying losses, with possible inclusion of liabilities under data‑protection laws but potential exclusions for regulatory fines. SBI has activated its incident response plan and engaged an independent cybersecurity firm via CERT‑In

I‑T to Issue Notices on Undisclosed Foreign Property

3MINDIA

The Income‑Tax Department will soon send notices to resident Indians holding undeclared overseas immovable assets, based on information received through spontaneous exchanges and case‑by‑case requests from foreign jurisdictions. Unlike the automatic exchange of information (AEOI), which covers financial assets, this data includes properties and may trigger scrutiny under FEMA and anti‑money‑laundering laws. Taxpayers are urged to use the FAST‑DS voluntary disclosure scheme, opened on August 16, which allows regularisation of foreign assets up to ₹1 cr with a 60% tax‑plus‑penalty rate, and up to ₹5 cr for assets acquired with taxed funds, to avoid penalties and prosecution. The response to the scheme has been modest due to valuation challenges

UBS Wins Arbitration Cases Against Former Credit Suisse Executives Over Deferred Compensation Write-Downs

ASHIKA BCG

UBS successfully defended its decision to write down contingent capital award certificates (CCAs)—deferred compensation tied to Credit Suisse’s additional tier 1 (AT1) instruments—after the 2023 government-brokered merger. Arbitrators rejected claims by former managing directors Martin Ego and Jeffrey Jennings, who argued the write-downs were unjustified since Credit Suisse met capital requirements during its crisis.

  • Key Points:
    • CCAs (modeled after AT1s) were designed to be written down in viability events, but Ego and Jennings contended no such event occurred.
    • Arbitrators ruled broadly that capital adequacy includes a bank’s ability to absorb losses and operate under stress, justifying the write-downs.
    • UBS seeks court confirmation of these rulings, while a separate arbitrator awarded $1M to former exec Matt Mitchell—a decision UBS is challenging.
    • Switzerland’s top court recently blocked payouts to 1,000+ former employees who lost bonuses post-rescue, aligning with UBS’s stance.
    • UBS faces ongoing legal battles over AT1 bondholder claims (dismissed in U.S. courts) and $300M in contingent liabilities (down from $1.5B), with integration nearing completion.

Outcome: Yes, CCAs were correctly written down as part of the rescue

Pirelli and BKT Forge India‑China Tyre JV

BALKRISIND

Italian tyre giant Pirelli and Indian off‑highway tyre maker Balkrishna Industries (BKT) have signed a strategic partnership to produce up to 800,000 Pirelli‑branded tyres annually by 2030 at a new Bhuj, Gujarat plant. The first phase involves BKT manufacturing Pirelli tyres under an off‑take agreement, with production slated to start next financial year. A second phase envisions a 50:50 joint venture to produce premium passenger‑car tyres under both brands for domestic and export markets, pending final business plans

Prada Posts Modest Q3 Growth Amid Luxury Market Slowdown

Italian luxury group Prada achieved 1% organic revenue growth in Q3, meeting expectations despite a weak market and a 3% sales drop at its Miu Miu brand. Including newly acquired Versace, total revenue reached $1.67 billion. Growth was driven by the Americas, stable Asia‑Pacific sales offsetting a sluggish Chinese market, while Europe remained weak. Integration of Versace is on track ahead of its new creative director’s debut next year
