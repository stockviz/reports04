The 1 Lakh SIP Milestone – Meaning, Strategies, and Common Pitfalls

In this MF Corner episode, co‑founder Aditya Grawal discusses the growing trend of investors committing ₹1 lakh (or more) to monthly SIPs. He sees the figure as a sign of rising investor awareness rather than mere affluence, emphasizing that the true metric is the proportion of income saved and invested. For newcomers, he recommends a step‑up SIP—starting around ₹44 k and increasing 10 % annually—to eventually reach a ₹1 lakh monthly commitment. Allocation should follow a core‑satellite model, with 70‑80 % in 3‑4 core equity funds (large‑cap, flexi‑cap, small‑cap, multi‑asset) and the rest in satellite funds like international or sectoral schemes, keeping the total portfolio to about five‑six schemes and reviewing it every 12‑18 months. Higher income doesn’t automatically mean a more aggressive equity tilt; life stage, risk capacity, and time horizon dictate asset allocation. Over‑diversification—measured by the number of schemes rather than asset classes—is a common mistake, as is chasing trends, which disrupts compounding. Investors should treat the ₹1 lakh figure as a benchmark for the share of income invested, not a destination, and regularly reassess SIPs and fund performance to stay aligned with goals
