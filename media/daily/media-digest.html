Singapore Airlines Endorses Air India’s Turnaround Plan Amid Persistent Financial Pressures

TENNIND

Singapore Airlines (SIA), which holds a 25.1% stake in Air India, has publicly backed Tewolde Gebremariam’s (former Ethiopian Airlines CEO) strategy to restructure the airline, focusing on cost-cutting, operational efficiency, and revenue growth. Key initiatives include: - Rerouting flights from Delhi to Mumbai to avoid Pakistani airspace, reducing fuel costs by an estimated ₹4,000 crore annually. - Expanding international ticket sales by targeting overseas demand (currently underutilized) and restoring capacity to North America/Europe. - Leveraging cargo potential by converting passenger planes into freighters and merging teams to cut duplication across Air India and Air India Express.

While SIA’s CEO, Goh Choon Phong, praised Tewolde’s proactive approach, he acknowledged that results will take time, as the new CEO joined only in October 2026. Despite a 16% decline in SIA’s share of Air India’s losses (S$361.7M in H1 2026 vs. S$429.5M in H1 2025), this improvement stems from provision reversals, not underlying financial recovery. Air India’s consolidated net loss widened to ₹22,238 crore in FY26, with revenue dropping 9% YoY.

Investor scrutiny remains intense, with questions over: - Sustained losses (SIA’s stake implies ~S$1.44B in H1 losses). - Need for further funding and SIA’s long-term commitment. - Operational challenges, including higher costs, currency depreciation, and fleet retrofitting (due by 2028).

Biggest concern: Persistent financial losses and uncertainty over funding, though execution delays also pose risks. SIA’s endorsement is cautious, reflecting skepticism about a swift turnaround
