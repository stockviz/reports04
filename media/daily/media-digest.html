Corporate Debt Surge and AI‑Driven Market Shifts

APOLLO GPPL

Richard Harris of Port Shelter Investment Management discusses the $500 billion AI infrastructure financing led by Nvidia and major Wall Street firms, highlighting a massive influx of liquidity that is crowding out traditional corporate borrowers and inflating a debt bubble. He notes that corporates now raise capital at rates cheaper than sovereigns, fueling AI investments despite uncertainty about job displacement. Regarding India, Harris points to rising bond yields, volatile oil prices, and a shift in foreign investor focus from emerging markets to Western AI opportunities, which has temporarily sidelined Indian assets. However, he suggests that if Western liquidity remains abundant, foreign institutional investors may return to undervalued emerging markets, including India, later in the year

Oil Supply Tensions and AI Bubble: Market Outlook from David Roche

David Roche explains that Middle‑East oil disruptions are creating a significant supply gap, worsened by reduced Russian exports and a rebound in Chinese oil imports, leaving little buffer for inventories and setting up price pressure in the autumn. He sees no immediate global slowdown to offset demand, as AI‑driven capital investment remains strong. Roche recommends a defensive stance: overweight real assets such as copper, gold (as an inflation hedge against higher oil and food prices), and select Chinese AI equities; remain short the US dollar; and be cautious on equities, especially in the US AI sector. He is bullish on Indian equities, viewing India as a consumer rather than a creator of AI, insulated from the US AI bubble, though mindful of inflation risks from energy. Overall, he advises keeping cash (a “mattress”) and focusing on tangible assets while monitoring oil‑related macro risks

US Judge Dismisses Criminal Charges Against Gautam Adani, Cites Procedural Concerns

ADANIENPP1 BCG

Brooklyn U.S. District Judge Nicholas Garaufis granted the Justice Department’s request to drop fraud and bribery charges against Indian billionaire Gautam Adani, finding no evidence that Adani’s promised $10 billion U.S. investment influenced the decision. Garaufis criticized Principal Associate Deputy Attorney General Trent McCotter for bypassing input from investigators and relying on his own judgment, calling the dismissal “irregular.” The judge emphasized his role was limited and did not endorse the DOJ’s rationale, urging the department to provide more information on other defendants. The case, originally alleging bribes to secure a solar plant permit and misleading U.S. investors, was deemed “primarily foreign” and low priority by the DOJ. Separately, Adani settled civil matters, paying $6 million, his nephew $12 million, and Adani Enterprises $275 million for alleged Iran‑sanctions violations. The judge left it to the public to assess the impact of investment pledges on justice
