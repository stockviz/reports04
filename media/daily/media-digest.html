How MRF’s 102‑Member Promoter Family Has Stayed United for 80 Years

MANORAMA MRF COASTCORP TINNARUBR SEIL

MRF Ltd.’s promoter group, comprising 102 individual family members and 13 entities, holds 27.77% of the company—the largest family shareholding base among listed Indian firms. The family’s cohesion over eight decades stems from disciplined governance, a low‑profile public stance, and a broadly distributed share structure that avoids concentration of power. Annual family council meetings, unwritten yet consistently applied rules, and a culture that treats ownership as a generational responsibility further reduce internal rivalries. The founder KM Mammen Mappillai’s descendants, including the current chairman and vice‑chairman, remain actively involved, while two not‑for‑profit foundations (MOWI and Evertrue) hold additional stakes, reinforcing the collective ownership model. This structured approach, coupled with professional management and clear succession planning, has insulated MRF from the disputes that have plagued many other Indian business families

AI‑Driven Productivity Gains Spark Pricing War in India’s IT Services Sector

HCLTECH EVERESTIND TECHM WIPRO TCS INFY

The rollout of AI is compressing revenues in India’s $300 billion IT services industry as firms slash prices to win price‑sensitive contracts, especially for coding, application development and maintenance where AI automates repetitive tasks. Billing rates per employee are falling (4‑5% in renewals) and clients demand that AI‑driven efficiency be reflected in lower fees, leading to margin pressure across the top vendors. Companies are offsetting lower rates by deploying bench staff and focusing on higher‑margin, AI‑centric projects, while some mid‑size firms leverage AI to challenge larger rivals. Impacts on profitability vary: Wipro’s margin fell, Tech Mahindra’s rose, and Infosys trimmed its revenue outlook. Despite the squeeze, firms see AI opening new revenue streams, with TCS reporting $2.6 bn in AI revenue and HCLTech’s AI earnings rising 10.6% YoY

Australia Boosts Osborne Shipyard Funding to Support Nuclear Submarine Build‑Up

Australia will invest an additional A$4.6 billion (US$3.2 billion) in the Osborne Naval Shipyard, expanding it ten‑fold to prepare for domestic construction of nuclear‑powered submarines under the Aukus pact. The funding follows an earlier A$3.9 billion commitment and will finance new infrastructure and workforce training. The move aims to give Australia sovereign capability to build, operate, and sustain its future submarine fleet and to broaden overall shipbuilding capacity, complementing other defence investments such as a A$12 billion shipyard near Perth for surface vessels
