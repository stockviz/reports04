IndiGo Q2 Preview: Higher Fares vs Rising Fuel Costs

INDIGO SPICEJET TCIEXP TENNIND

IndiGo’s September‑quarter results will reveal if fare hikes and surcharges can offset an 86% YoY jump in fuel costs amid a weaker rupee and softer demand. After a Q1 net loss of ₹238 cr despite a 20% revenue rise, analysts are split: Elara sees a ₹580 cr profit, while Nuvama expects a ₹2,490 cr loss. Domestic fares are up 19‑32% YoY and international fares 40‑47%, but fuel cost per ASK may rise 52‑71%, with international ATF up 142%. Capacity constraints support fares, yet departures fell 8% and load factor is projected to dip to 80.1%. The airline, which has not hedged fuel, is also transitioning its international fleet from leased 787‑9s to A321XLRs and pending A350‑900s, testing the profitability of its up‑market expansion. New CEO Willie Walsh’s first analyst briefing will focus on aircraft deliveries, hedging, FX risk and margin protection. IndiGo’s share price recovered to ₹4,619 but remains below its recent peak

Anthropic’s Multi‑Chip Strategy and OpenAI’s Google Chip Deal Pose Long‑Term Questions for Nvidia’s Dominance

AMDIND

Anthropic is now running AI workloads across four chip families—Amazon Trainium, Google TPUs, Nvidia GPUs, and AMD GPUs—using software to allocate tasks to the most efficient platform. This diversification reduces its reliance on Nvidia, potentially weakening Nvidia’s pricing leverage and supply‑risk exposure. Analysts note that while the multichip approach gives large AI firms more negotiating power, switching chips remains complex, especially for model training, and Nvidia still holds advantages in performance, networking, and software. OpenAI is also exploring Google’s chips alongside its own upcoming silicon, adding to the pressure on Nvidia. However, experts caution that Nvidia’s dominance is unlikely to be eroded in the near term, as the effort and investment required for a true multichip strategy may be prohibitive for smaller players, and Nvidia’s technical edge remains strong. The broader risk to Nvidia could arise if AI compute becomes a commodity, leading to oversupply and falling rental prices

Ahammune Biosciences Advances First‑in‑Class Vitiligo Therapy to Phase 2

ANTHEM

The company, funded with $8‑9 million in grants and equity, keeps development costs low, plans to commercialize in India at affordable prices, and seeks partnerships for US/EU markets. Credit Card Boom in India: Banks Rush to Expand Portfolios Ahead of Festive Season

AUBANK KOTAKBANK FEDERALBNK HDFCBANK BANKBARODA ICICIBANK AXISBANK BANKINDIA SBICARD

In September 2026, Indian banks—led by HDFC Bank and Axis Bank—added 1.26 million net credit cards, marking the second-highest monthly growth this year (after July’s 1.27 million). The surge reflects aggressive expansion ahead of the festive season, with HDFC and Axis alone contributing 43% of new cards (539,878 combined). SBI Cards followed as the third-largest contributor.

Growth was uneven: ICICI Bank and Kotak Mahindra Bank saw muted additions, while others like Bank of Baroda, Slice Small Finance Bank, and Federal Bank also expanded their portfolios. Quarterly data (July–September) showed a 47% YoY rise in net additions (3.72 million), up 26% from Q2 2026, signaling heightened festive-season preparations.

Total outstanding credit cards reached 125.32 million by September—10.4% higher than last year. HDFC Bank led monthly additions (280,353), doubling August’s figures, while Axis Bank’s growth (259,525) was eightfold from the prior month. The trend underscores banks’ focus on capturing holiday spending demand
