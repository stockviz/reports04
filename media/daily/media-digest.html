Tesla winds down Cybertruck production amid weak demand

ASKAUTOLTD

Elon Musk announced that the Texas Gigafactory will cease Cybertruck production by mid‑next year to free capacity for the autonomous Cybercab. The pickup, unveiled in 2019 with a bold design and a target of 250,000 annual sales, has dramatically underperformed, with US sales dropping to about 20,000 units in 2025—a 48% decline from the previous year. Quality issues, multiple recalls, and limited market appeal further hampered demand, prompting Tesla to reallocate resources toward AI‑driven autonomous vehicles and other core models

Anthropic IPO Valuation Trimmed Amid Tech Stock Sell‑Off

Investors have cut their expected valuation for Anthropic’s upcoming Nasdaq IPO from a prior $2 trillion target to roughly $1.1‑$1.3 trillion after a steep tech‑stock decline wiped out trillions in market value. The downgrade follows volatility spikes, concerns over AI spending and data‑center expansion, and a comparison with rival OpenAI’s $1.25 trillion valuation. Analysts suggest Anthropic could either price the offering more conservatively or postpone the roadshow, with a lower price offering more comfort and a delay risking longer‑term valuation and reputational costs

Strong Q2 Performance for Indian Mid‑Cap IT Firms, Except Sonata

INFY LTF SONATSOFTW PERSISTENT HEXT TCS TECHM HCLTECH WIPRO COFORGE MPHASIS FSL

Mphasis and Coforge posted robust Q2 results, with Mphasis achieving its best quarter in five years (revenue $486 M, +3.2% QoQ) and Coforge leading growth (+10.1% to $652 M) and raising its FY margin outlook. QatarEnergy in talks to secure up to 2 mtpa from NextDecade’s Train 6

HALDER ATHERENERG

QatarEnergy is negotiating to purchase up to 2 million metric tons per annum of LNG from NextDecade’s Rio Grande LNG export facility in Texas, likely sourced from the pending Train 6. The move aligns with QatarEnergy’s strategy to hedge against potential disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz by diversifying its supply portfolio. Train 6 is still in the permitting stage, with a final investment decision expected around 2027, and could later be followed by Trains 7 and 8, expanding total site capacity to roughly 48 mtpa

Gaurav Munjal Praises Temple by Deepinder Goyal: Potential, Criticisms, and Future Outlook

ZOMATO ETERNAL

Unacademy co-founder Gaurav Munjal gave a largely positive review of Temple, the wearable device by Zomato founder Deepinder Goyal, highlighting its lightweight design, sleep tracking, and food-monitoring capabilities. Munjal praised its comfort during sleep and workouts, noting it stayed securely in place and was barely noticeable. He also lauded its food-tracking feature, which detects chewing and sends meal reminders—a function he called superior to existing apps.

Munjal acknowledged two key concerns: the need for greater brand awareness (as people frequently asked about the device) and app interface improvements, comparing Temple’s UX unfavorably to competitors like Whoop.

Goyal responded to criticism by emphasizing customer validation through a 14-day return policy and $30M in R&D investment (including $20M personal funds). He argued that Temple’s claims would be proven or disproven quickly if flawed, and he defended the product’s scientific validation process, mentioning upcoming preprints and peer-reviewed studies. Goyal also hinted at unreleased features, suggesting Temple’s potential extends beyond current offerings.

Key takeaway: Temple demonstrates strong potential as a global wearable but requires refined UX and broader validation to compete effectively
