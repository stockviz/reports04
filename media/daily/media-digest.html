Paytm’s Wallet Comeback: Prospects and Challenges

MOBIKWIK PAYTM

Paytm is set to relaunch its prepaid wallet after RBI’s in‑principle approval of a PPI licence, aiming to regain control over payment transactions and improve margins that were previously shared with partner banks. While the wallet once drove loyalty and reduced costs, UPI has become the dominant payment method, and consumer adoption of wallets has declined. The new licence could enable Paytm to capture niche use‑cases such as FASTag, offline mobility payments, gifting, and autopay, and reduce reliance on partner banks for these services. However, regaining user stickiness will be difficult given the entrenched UPI ecosystem and emerging card‑linked wallets like Apple Pay and Samsung Pay. Paytm’s growth is now driven mainly by financial‑services distribution and lending, with a 28% YoY revenue rise and a 10% EBITDA margin, while competition in merchant lending and regulatory risks remain key concerns

The Psychology of Investing: How Mind Traps Undermine Returns

TIMESGTY MOKSH

Investor outcomes often diverge not because of different products or market conditions but due to behavioral biases. Five common mind traps—loss aversion, herd mentality, recency bias, overconfidence, and anchoring—lead investors to panic‑sell, chase trends, overtrade, and hold onto losing positions. To protect returns, set clear written goals, automate contributions (e.g., SIPs), limit portfolio checks, apply a 48‑hour cooling‑off rule before major moves, and seek a trusted sounding board. Consistent, patient, goal‑driven investing typically outperforms reactive, emotion‑driven decisions
