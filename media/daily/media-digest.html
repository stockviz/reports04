BEML TIMESGTY

Interiors include ergonomic seats, LED lighting, ample luggage racks, power outlets, bio‑digester toilets, large windows, and a sealed gangway. Banks Raise Deposit Rates as RBI’s Hike Triggers Liquidity Tightening

BANKINDIA INDIANB

About a month after the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) raised policy rates for the first time in four years, domestic banks—including Bank of India (BoI)—are gradually increasing deposit rates amid shrinking liquidity and rising credit demand. BoI’s recent hike (10–35 basis points for 1–3-year tenures) marks the second such move in the current cycle, with rates now ranging from 6.60% to 6.85% for select deposits.

The adjustment reflects higher deposit costs and a shift in the liquidity profile, as systemic liquidity has dropped from ₹11 lakh crore in early September to ₹1.96 lakh crore due to bond sales and reverse repo operations. Tighter conditions are evident in the certificate of deposit (CD) market, where rates have surged to over 8%—the highest since early 2023. Earlier, Indian Bank had raised rates on special term deposits (555/777 days).

BoI’s executive attributed the move to “higher cost of deposits” and the need to align with the RBI’s rate-hike cycle, noting that benefits from the FCNR(B) scheme inflows are fading. While deposit rates lag behind lending rates, the trend signals a broader hardening of monetary conditions
