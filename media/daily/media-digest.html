India’s Push for Home‑Made Tunnel Boring Machines

BEML

BEML Ltd., the state‑owned defence and engineering firm, plans to develop indigenous tunnel‑boring machines (TBMs) ranging from 3.5‑6.5 m in diameter for metro, hydropower, irrigation and water projects, aiming to cut reliance on Chinese imports. The company will start with smaller TBMs before moving to larger ones, partnering with a global consultant and targeting proprietary Indian IP. This effort aligns with broader Indian initiatives to reduce dependence on China in strategic sectors, though past attempts (e.g., rare‑earth magnets, solar upstream materials) show building a full domestic supply chain is challenging. The TBM market is expected to grow about 12% annually in India, driven by a projected $9.6 bn of tunnelling projects over the next five years, especially along Himalayan frontiers. BEML anticipates delivering its first machine within roughly 22 months after securing a contract

SpiceJet’s Financial and Operational Crisis

SPICEJET

SpiceJet is facing a severe cash crunch that has led to widespread delays in supplier payments—over 85% of invoices overdue, with ₹63.03 crore unpaid—delayed employee salaries, and a sharp decline in flight operations. In September, nearly 40% of its flights were delayed over two hours and 20% were cancelled, causing domestic market share to plunge to 0.6%. Only nine of its twelve aircraft are active, while three remain grounded due to lease payment issues. The airline posted a ₹262 crore loss in the Dec‑2025 quarter, total debt exceeds ₹4,200 crore, and it has not disclosed recent financial results, prompting calls for urgent investor funding and regulatory scrutiny

India’s top law firms boost partner ranks amid rising demand for specialised expertise

ASPINWALL KHAITANLTD SHARDUL

Indian law firms are expanding their partner benches, with FY27 sees 6‑20% year‑on‑year growth across major firms such as Khaitan & Co, Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas, Trilegal, JSA Advocates and Shardul Amarchand Mangaldas. After a pandemic‑induced slowdown, the overall partner count rose 14% in FY25, eased to 10% in FY26, and is projected to hit about 12% in FY27 as boutique firms intensify talent competition. CAM increased to 250 partners (≈6% rise) with 23 promotions and six laterals. Trilegal’s partner headcount rose 11.3% to 168, all equity partners. SAM expanded to 194 partners (including 11 salaried and 7 equity promotions) and hired 13 laterals. The trend shows more entry‑level (salaried/retained) partnership promotions, fewer moves into equity, and the emergence of mid‑year promotion cycles to meet growing client demand for niche expertise
