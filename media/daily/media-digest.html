The 3-Bucket Retirement Strategy: A Smart Approach to Managing Your Retirement Corpus

JMFINANCIL

A bucketing strategy is a retirement planning approach that divides your corpus into three (or four) distinct “buckets” to ensure financial stability, liquidity, and growth during retirement. Here’s how it works:

  1. First Bucket (Liquidity Tank – 2-3 years):
    • Purpose: Covers fixed living expenses and short-term obligations.
    • Assets: Parked in ultra-safe, liquid investments like savings accounts, liquid funds, or arbitrage funds to avoid market risk.
    • Key Benefit: Prevents forced selling of equities during market downturns, preserving long-term wealth.
  2. Second Bucket (Stability Tank – 3-7 years):
    • Purpose: Provides steady income for mid-term needs.
    • Assets: Allocated to hybrid funds, multi-asset funds, or fixed-income instruments for consistent returns without high volatility.
    • Flexibility: Can replenish the first bucket if needed, ensuring a buffer against inflation and longevity risks.
  3. Third Bucket (Growth Tank – Long-Term):
    • Purpose: Preserves and grows wealth for future needs.
    • Assets: Primarily equities or equity mutual funds, but managed with caution to avoid excessive risk.
    • Strategy: Avoids aggressive withdrawals; uses judgment-based rebalancing (not rigid systematic withdrawal plans) to maintain asset allocation.

Why Use a Bucket Strategy? - Pros: - Protects against sequence risk (market downturns forcing early withdrawals at low prices). - Balances growth and safety, reducing reliance on fixed income (which erodes due to inflation). - Provides flexibility to adjust spending without compromising long-term wealth. - Cons of Not Using It: - Risk of permanent wealth loss if forced to sell equities during a crash. - Inflation risk if too much is in fixed income. - Liquidity constraints if emergencies arise without a dedicated cash reserve.

Special Considerations: - Feeding the First Bucket: Use systematic withdrawal plans (SWPs) for stable assets (e.g., debt funds) but avoid rigid SWPs for equities—market conditions and rebalancing should guide withdrawals. - Tax Efficiency: SWPs can be tax-efficient for non-equity portions. - Avoid Over-Tinkering: Frequent shifts between buckets can trigger tax liabilities and compounding losses.

Example for an 8-Crore Corpus (Retired Couple): 1. First Bucket: 2–3 years’ expenses in liquid/safe assets. 2. Second Bucket: Hybrid/multi-asset funds for 3–7 years’ needs. 3. Third Bucket: Equities with range-bound allocations to ensure longevity of wealth.

Final Takeaway: A bucket strategy is essential for retirement planning in India, where market volatility and inflation pose significant risks. While DIY is possible, working with a financial advisor is recommended to tailor the approach to individual needs
