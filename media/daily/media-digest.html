India’s OTT Market Welcomes New Niche Players, Yet Monetisation Remains Elusive

TVVISION MAXESTATES PFOCUS

India’s over‑the‑top (OTT) sector continues to draw fresh entrants such as Tiger TV, Brew TV, AVR Cine‑Max and Vihari OTT, which are targeting regional and niche audiences and leveraging the growth of connected TV (CTV). Despite a massive user base—665 million OTT viewers and 207 million CTV households in 2026—and a market size exceeding ₹27,000 crore in 2025, converting scale into sustainable revenue is challenging. Tiger TV aims for 10 million subscribers and ₹500 crore ARR in three years, while Brew TV focuses on independent film distribution. Analysts note that while niche content can thrive internationally, India’s low ARPU hampers profitability, leaving new platforms to grapple with turning audience reach into recurring income amid stiff competition from over 50 established players

Welspun Corp Bets $350M on US Oil & Gas Pipeline Boom Amid Rising Energy Demand

APOLLOPIPE WELCORP

Welspun Corp, a major line pipe manufacturer under the $10B Welspun Group, is investing $300–350M in two US plants to capitalize on surging demand for oil and gas pipelines, driven by: - 70% YoY rise in crude prices (post-Iran war disruptions). - US energy expansion: Increased refining/drilling capacity and infrastructure investments. - Government support: Faster approvals for pipeline projects.

With 1M tonnes of US capacity (second plant to open by year-end), Welspun targets 30–35% market share in the US, backed by a 3-year order book (vs. typical 4–6 quarters). Globally, it has 3.5–4M tonnes capacity (India: 2M, Saudi Arabia: 1M).

In Saudi Arabia, a $100–150M plant supports Vision 2030 infrastructure needs. The company is also expanding into Jordan and diversifying into niche, value-added pipes to avoid commodity risks.

Festive Travel Boom in India Despite Soaring Costs Post-Diwali

ARIS IXIGO STERTOOLS

Despite a 27% spike in airfares and hotel rates after Diwali, travel demand in India is surging for the festive season. Key highlights:

  • Hotel rates (mid-scale) are up 26.94% post-Diwali (Nov 11–15), with premium properties averaging ₹31,163 (vs. ₹25,917 pre-Diwali). Goa’s premium rates may jump 42% (₹24,218 → ₹34,355) by late November.
  • Drive-to destinations like Ooty, Coorg, and wildlife parks (Pench, Tadoba) are gaining traction due to improved highway connectivity.
  • International bookings (Maldives, Thailand, Sri Lanka) rose 80–90% YoY for platforms like Pickyourtrail, while domestic beach/hill station bookings grew 3.5x for Scapia.
  • Trains and hotels see strong demand, with Ixigo noting early Q2 upticks in train bookings. Buses and hotels lead near-term momentum, though flight supply remains constrained.

BARC Registration Delay Deepens TV Ratings Market Rift in India

HTMEDIA SITINET HATHWAY DISHTV 3MINDIA CRISIL

The Ministry of Information and Broadcasting has yet to approve fresh registrations for BARC India and rival TAM India, citing concerns over BARC’s ownership, progress on cross‑media measurement, and the lack of an updated Broadcast India study. BARC’s previous registration lapsed in 2025; despite appointing new independent directors and expanding its panel to 80,000 meters, the ministry remains dissatisfied, leading to a ratings blackout since June (news data withheld since March). This hampers broadcasters’ ability to benchmark performance and prove ad value in a TV ad market worth Rs 30,000‑40,000 crore, threatens smaller and free‑to‑air channels, and could impact advertising revenues, production jobs, and distribution platforms like DD Free Dish, Hathway, and Siti Networks. Financially, BARC’s liquidity appears intact per CRISIL, but many broadcasters have stopped paying fees, raising fears of a collapse. The stalemate underscores calls for increased competition in India’s TV ratings sector

Instacart Settles $5 Million Discrimination Claim Over PERM Hiring Practices

Instacart agreed to a $5 million settlement with the U.S. Justice Department and EEOC over allegations it discriminated against U.S.-born job seekers by requiring mailed applications for PERM‑related positions, while allowing electronic submissions for other roles. The company will pay $1 million in civil penalties, create a fund of up to $4 million to compensate affected applicants, and must post PERM jobs on its public career site and accept online applications. The settlement follows similar actions against Apple and Facebook, highlighting government efforts to enforce fair hiring for U.S. workers
