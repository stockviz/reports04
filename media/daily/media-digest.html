India’s Semicon 2.0 Must Go Beyond Fabs to Design and Advanced Packaging

Chip‑war expert Chris Miller says India’s semiconductor strategy should expand past fabrication to emphasize chip design, equipment, and especially advanced packaging. He notes India’s existing design talent in hubs like Hyderabad and Bengaluru, the AI‑driven shift toward data‑center chips, and the need for deep integration into global value chains—mirroring South Korea and Taiwan—to create niche, high‑value capabilities across the ecosystem

India’s Private‑Bank CEO Talent Crunch

HDFCBANK EMKAY KOTAKBANK TCNSBRANDS AXISBANK

Indian banks, despite a deep bench of banking talent, face a thin pipeline of senior executives capable of running large, complex institutions. Recent CEO turnover at major private lenders like HDFC and Kotak highlights the scarcity of seasoned internal candidates, forcing boards to rely on a narrow pool of repeat prospects that must also clear strict RBI regulatory, age and tenure criteria. The shortage is attributed to long tenures of past CEOs, limited leadership development, competition from fintechs, and a brain drain to global firms, leaving succession planning a critical risk for India’s $2 trillion loan market

TITAN BCG RUBICON NSIL

Aramco Halts Crude Supplies to India Amid Pipeline Attack

Saudi Aramco has stopped all crude shipments to Indian refiners after an alleged drone attack on its East‑West pipeline, ending both Red Sea and Strait of Hormuz deliveries. Supreme Court Backs Himachal HC Decision Limiting Central Drug Regulator’s Enforcement Powers

APLLTD

The Supreme Court rejected the Union government’s challenge to a Himachal Pradesh High Court ruling that a Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSDO) inspector lacks authority to independently inspect, sample, and prosecute pharma firms under Chapter IV of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act. The high court affirmed that such enforcement powers reside with state authorities, which regulate manufacture, sale, and distribution within their territories, while the central regulator handles imports, new drugs, clinical trials, vaccines, and large‑volume parenterals. The Supreme Court found no sufficient ground to overturn the May 7 decision, dismissing the Centre’s plea and maintaining the division of regulatory powers between centre and states. This judgment may subject central enforcement actions, increasingly undertaken by CDSCO, to closer judicial scrutiny

40‑bps UPI Merchant Discount Rate Could Generate Rs 27,000 cr by FY28, Says Bernstein

CCAVENUE

Bernstein estimates that introducing a 40‑basis‑point merchant discount rate (MDR) on eligible UPI merchant transactions could create a Rs 27,000 crore annual revenue pool by FY28. Effective MDR will average about 19 bps due to exemptions and concessions, yielding Rs 10,800 cr for issuing banks, Rs 5,400 cr for consumer‑facing UPI apps, Rs 2,700 cr for payer PSP banks, another Rs 5,400‑6,800 cr for merchant‑side apps, and Rs 1,400‑2,700 cr for acquiring banks. Transactions under Rs 2,000 (≈33% of P2M value) and AutoPay remain free; capital‑market payments attract 2 bps, while categories like fuel, telecom, insurance, education, utilities, and railways will be charged a flat Rs 5. The levy is viewed as a sustenance fee to fund infrastructure, security, and service costs rather than a major profit driver, staying well below card‑payment fees

E‑commerce loses ground to brick‑and‑mortar in India’s smartphone and laptop market as entry‑level demand weakens

RUBICON FRETAIL 3MINDIA

Online share of smartphone sales fell to 41.9% (down from 46.5% a year earlier) and laptop sales to 30% (down from 32%). Weaker demand for entry‑level devices, higher prices (up 35‑40%) and fewer online discounts pushed shoppers back to physical stores, where EMI financing is more available. IDC data shows online smartphone shipments dropped 19.8% YoY in Q2, while offline shipments fell only 3.6%; overall smartphone shipments fell 11%. Laptop shipments rose 6.6% YoY in Q2, driven by trade purchases, but are expected to flatline in H2. TV sales are an exception, with online share rising to 39% despite an 8% market contraction. Analysts expect price‑sensitive buyers to stay pressured through the second half, with a stronger focus on EMI offers

RBI Sets Clear Rules for NBFC Principal Business and CIC Classification After Tata Sons CoR Rejection

NSIL

The RBI rejected Tata Sons’ request to surrender its certificate of registration and, within days, issued FAQs defining a “principal business” for NBFCs—financial assets must exceed 50% of total assets and income. Companies primarily engaged in agriculture, industry, trade, services, or real‑estate, with only minor financial activity, remain outside RBI regulation. For groups with multiple prospective Core Investment Companies (CICs) whose combined assets exceed ₹100 crore, each CIC must obtain a CoR, unless only one holds public funds and does not fund others. NBFCs wishing to become CICs can apply for exemptions on capital adequacy and exposure norms. Entities not meeting the 50% asset/income thresholds are not required to register as NBFCs. Tata Sons, with assets of ₹2.01 lakh crore as of March 2026, exceeds the NBFC‑Upper Layer threshold, prompting continued regulatory scrutiny and a mandated public listing

Parts Suppliers Go Global as Automakers Seek Flexible Sourcing

BIRLAPREC SHRIPISTON SUPRAJIT TENNIND

Indian tier‑1 auto parts makers such as Suprajit Engineering, Highway Roop, SPR Auto and Tenneco are expanding overseas plants and engineering capabilities to meet OEMs’ demand for diversified, multi‑site production. Driven by trade uncertainty, tariffs and supply‑chain disruptions, global automakers want designs developed in one location and manufactured across several countries (“China + 1”). Suprajit’s spread across India, China, Mexico, Hungary and Morocco enables volume shifts without changing suppliers. Tenneco notes Indian‑engineered parts now match European quality. Companies are moving from build‑to‑print to proprietary design and sub‑assembly, covering hybrids, hydrogen, CNG and EV platforms. A BCG‑ACMA report predicts India’s auto‑parts exports to rise from $24 bn in FY26 to $45 bn by decade’s end, closing the gap with China’s 12 % share of global trade. This shift positions Indian suppliers as flexible, high‑tech partners rather than just low‑cost alternatives
