Credit Card Trends in India: Rising Transactions, Smaller Spends, and Growing UPI Integration

SWIGGY VISACHROME AXISBANK

In India, credit card spending has surged by 24% in transaction volume, but the average spend per transaction has dropped by 13%, driven by the rise of UPI-linked credit card payments. This shift reflects a growing trend where consumers use credit cards for smaller, everyday purchases (e.g., groceries, Uber rides, QR-based payments) via UPI, reducing average transaction values while increasing overall usage.

Key insights from Adil Shetty (BankBazaar CEO): 1. Credit card adoption is accelerating, with India becoming one of the fastest-growing markets globally (27% annual growth from 2020–2025). However, only ~50 million Indians (5% of adults) have credit cards—far below global averages (e.g., 70% in the UK). 2. UPI integration enables seamless credit card payments for micro-transactions (e.g., Kirana stores, QR codes), blending convenience with rewards (e.g., points, no-cost EMIs). 3. Consumer behavior: - Existing users are increasing frequency of small spends. - New adopters (expected to grow post-2027) will drive future expansion, potentially reaching 100 million cardholders by 2030. 4. Risks vs. rewards: - Prudent use (paying bills in full, using no-cost EMIs) maximizes benefits (rewards, credit score boosts). - Over-reliance (minimum payments, rolling debt) harms credit health. 5. Categories driving growth: - Large spends: Dining, online shopping, OTT, travel. - Small spends: QR/UPI payments (e.g., groceries, rides), where convenience often outweighs rewards.

Outlook: Credit cards remain aspirational, with UPI integration making them more accessible. However, disciplined usage is critical to avoid debt traps. Future growth hinges on new adopters and **banks’ risk-appetite policies
