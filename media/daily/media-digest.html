China’s Green Hydrogen Drive: A High‑Stakes Bet for Global Energy Futures

BCG

China is rapidly expanding its green hydrogen capacity, now holding about 250,000 t/yr—over twice the rest of the world’s total—and aims to reach 2 million t/yr by 2030. The surge is powered by abundant wind and solar, cheap electrolyzers (four times cheaper than Europe’s), and strong government subsidies. Projects like Envision’s $2.6 bn plant in Inner Mongolia convert excess renewable power into hydrogen and ammonia, addressing curtailment and creating new demand for hard‑to‑decarbonize sectors such as steel, cement, and chemicals. However, green hydrogen remains costlier than gray or blue variants, and demand‑side policies are needed to close the price gap. If successful, China could cement its leadership in clean‑tech and provide a viable path to decarbonizing heavy industry; failure would cast doubt on green hydrogen’s commercial viability worldwide
