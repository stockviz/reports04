Factor Investing in India: Insights from Moneyfront CEO Mohit Gang

Factor investing overlays traditional passive indices with systematic “smart‑beta” screens—such as value, momentum, quality, low‑volatility, etc.—to create factor‑based indices. These can be single‑factor or multi‑factor (e.g., momentum + quality) and act as a quasi‑active, rule‑based approach. Mohit stresses that factor funds are not for beginners; investors must understand each factor’s cyclical behavior and use them tactically rather than as a core strategic hold. By blending factors, investors can achieve negative correlation among equity exposures, diversifying by investment style instead of just market‑cap. Performance varies with market regimes—momentum thrives in bull markets, value in downturns, etc.—so timing and portfolio monitoring are crucial. Recommended exposure is modest: 10‑20% of a seasoned investor’s equity portfolio (or up to 20‑25% for a tactical sleeve) allocated to factor‑based passive products, complementing traditional active and passive holdings
