UPI MDR Targets High‑Value Retailers, Sparing Low‑Ticket Food & Quick‑Commerce

FIRSTCRY NYKAA ZOMATO LENSKART DMART CELLO TRENT SWIGGY

From 15 Oct, a 0.4 % merchant discount rate (MDR) will apply to person‑to‑merchant UPI payments above ₹2,000, ending the zero‑MDR model for larger transactions. The cost is borne by merchants, though they cannot directly pass it to consumers. Retailers with average order values (AOV) above the threshold—such as jewellery, electronics, Nykaa, FirstCry, Lenskart and D‑Mart—face EBITDA hits ranging from ~0.5 % to 2.6 %, while quick‑commerce and food‑delivery firms (Zomato, Swiggy, Blinkit) remain largely unaffected because their orders stay below ₹2,000. Companies may absorb the charge, adjust pricing, encourage bill‑splitting or installments, or use other monetisation levers (convenience fees, higher commission rates) to offset the impact. Smaller retailers with sub‑₹2,000 baskets, like The Organic World, can likely absorb the cost without altering payment behavior

States and Writers Guild Settle Lawsuits, Clearing Path for Paramount‑Warner $81 B Merger

LGBBROSLTD PARACABLES

Twelve U.S. states and the Writers Guild of America have settled antitrust lawsuits against Paramount’s $81 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, removing the final regulatory obstacle. Under the agreement announced by California Attorney General Rob Bonta, Paramount will commit an extra $1.5 billion to U.S. film production over five years, monitor editorial independence of its news operations, prohibit writer layoffs at CBS News for five years, and pay $17.5 million to the WGA health fund and cover litigation fees. While officials say the deal will boost Hollywood and create more entertainment options, critics warn it will further consolidate the industry, reduce competition, and potentially raise costs for consumers
