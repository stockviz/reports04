Regulatory Violations Found in Imported Dialysis Solution in Himachal Pradesh

3MINDIA

A government lab in Himachal Pradesh declared two batches of an imported peritoneal dialysis solution (manufactured in Thailand and distributed by Baxter India) as “Not of Standard Quality” after failing bacterial endotoxin tests under Indian Pharmacopoeia 2022. The solution, used in kidney failure patients, was tested at AIIMS-Bilaspur.

The lab report (dated September 3) and a state department letter (September 7) flagged nine regulatory violations, including: 1. Wrong pharmacopoeia standards – Key ingredients (sodium chloride, calcium chloride, magnesium chloride) conformed to US Pharmacopeia instead of Indian standards, classifying the product as “misbranded.” 2. Labeling errors – The brand name (Vantive) appeared in larger font than the generic name and was placed above it, violating Indian drug labeling rules. 3. Missing pharmacopoeial specifications for sodium lactate on the label. 4. Absent critical safety warnings, including: - “Single Dose Container” label. - Storage temperature limits (risk of crystallization below 4°C). - “Do not Freeze” warning. - Caution against using solutions with visible particles. 5. Missing importer details (name, address, import license number).

The violations raise concerns about patient safety, as the solution is used in critically ill kidney patients. Baxter India noted it spun off its renal division in 2024, with the successor company (Vantive) not responding to queries. The Drugs Controller General of India has been informed

Stellantis Explores Sale of Idle Brampton Plant to Roshel for Armored Vehicle Production Amid US Tariff Fallout

JINDALSTEL

Stellantis NV has signed a memorandum of understanding (MOU) with Roshel Inc., a Canadian armored vehicle manufacturer, outlining steps toward selling its idle Brampton Assembly plant near Toronto. The move follows the plant’s shutdown in late 2023 after Stellantis scrapped plans to produce the Jeep Compass SUV due to US tariffs on Canadian-made vehicles.

Roshel, headquartered near the plant, is pursuing a C$4.9 billion ($3.5 billion) military contract to build light utility vehicles and claims it could reactivate the facility quickly if awarded the deal. The company’s CEO, Roman Shimonov, called the MOU a structured first step, though critics argue Roshel’s defense-focused production doesn’t match the plant’s historic automotive scale (500–1,000 vehicles/day, 3,000+ workers).

Key points: - Stellantis’ rationale: Preserving Canada’s advanced manufacturing sector and avoiding prolonged inactivity. - Union concerns: Unifor (representing workers) was informed of potential closure/sale, but negotiations are ongoing. - Alternatives: Brampton’s mayor mentioned inquiries from China’s BYD, while industry groups like CAPMA oppose Roshel’s bid, arguing the plant’s automotive infrastructure is irreplaceable. - Roshel’s plan: Combine armored vehicle production with potential future automotive partnerships, citing 4,530+ jobs (direct/indirect) if the military contract succeeds. - Government stance: Industry Minister Melanie Joly’s office declined to comment.

The deal hinges on Roshel securing the military contract and balancing defense production with automotive potential, though skeptics warn of mismatched capabilities

GAIL Gas Uses Blended Pricing to Shield Industrial Customers Amid Rising Global Gas Costs

GAIL

GAIL Gas is offering a blended price for up to 80% of industrial customers’ contracted volumes, using a mix of differently priced LNG contracts and contractual flexibility to keep gas affordable despite soaring global oil and LNG prices triggered by renewed US‑Iran conflict. The remaining volume is priced at higher international spot rates. Because GAIL’s procurement portfolio leans more on crude‑linked contracts, which have risen less sharply than gas‑benchmark‑linked contracts, the company can maintain lower blended rates even as overall gas costs climb. Industrial sales are currently about 10% lower year‑on‑year, and force majeure remains in effect due to supply disruptions near the Strait of Hormuz
