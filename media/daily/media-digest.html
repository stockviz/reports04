Renault Duster Hybrid First‑Drive Review – Real‑World Performance, Comfort & Value

The Overdrive team finds the new Duster Hybrid, priced from ₹16.99 lakh, delivers a smooth electric‑first drive with a 1.8 L Atkinson‑cycle engine plus a 50 PS electric motor, offering 160 PS total and five drive modes. It feels quiet, EV‑like in city traffic, and maintains power without noticeable gaps, though engine kick‑in adds slight vibration and engine braking is limited. The added 80 kg weight is well‑managed by revised suspension and FDD dampers, giving a composed, European‑style ride. Interior changes are minor—new tan upholstery, same 10.25‑inch instrument cluster and 10.1‑inch touchscreen (still basic). Cargo space drops to 372 L but remains practical. Overall, the Duster Hybrid retains the original’s rugged styling and dynamics, offers solid fuel efficiency, and presents strong value for a mainstream hybrid SUV in India

Commercial LPG Cylinder Prices Rise to Rs 2,820 Amid Ongoing Volatility

From November 1, the 19‑kg commercial LPG cylinder price increased by Rs 10 to Rs 2,820, marking the third consecutive monthly hike and impacting hospitality and commercial vendors during the festive season. A 5‑kg Free Trade LPG cylinder costs Rs 1,903 for new connections and Rs 782 for refills. 2026 saw extreme price swings, including a Rs 1,000 surge in May that pushed rates above Rs 3,000, followed by July‑August cuts of Rs 380. The latest hikes follow earlier increases in September and October, driven by geopolitical tensions and disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz, which have tightened global LPG supply and raised freight and contract costs

AI‑Powered Personalised Gifting Fuels Gen Z Diwali Spending

5PAISA SANDESH

Gen Z shoppers are reshaping Diwali gifting by using AI to analyse recipients’ social media profiles for hyper‑customised ideas, favoring artisanal, wellness‑focused and story‑rich products that support small businesses. Reliance Industries Redirects Fuel Exports Amid West Asia Conflict, Boosts Market Share

RELIANCE

Reliance Industries (RIL) shifted fuel exports to East and South Africa, the Mediterranean, and Europe, capturing a ~50% market share gain as West Asian supply disruptions drove up global fuel prices. The move, alongside new premium gasoline sales in Australia, Kenya, and Tanzania, and first-time low‑sulphur ATF shipments to South Africa, helped RIL’s oil‑to‑chemicals business surge 68% in EBITDA to a record Rs 25,203 cr. Despite higher crude, freight, and insurance costs, RIL maintained record processing by diversifying its crude import basket and deploying over 20 vessels. Tight global supply, refinery outages, and geopolitical risks keep refining margins elevated, prompting RIL to focus on high asset utilisation, margin maximisation, and end‑to‑end optimisation
