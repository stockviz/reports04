Immediate actions: inventory PHP versions, apply emergency patches, or temporarily shut down affected services. Smaller sites are especially vulnerable due to limited technical resources

India’s Food Regulator Cracks Down on Misleading Labels, Big Brands and Health Claims Under Scrutiny**

The Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) is intensifying its oversight of food labels, nutrition claims, and advertising, issuing over 150 notices to companies for alleged misleading claims, false labeling, and violations. Key areas under scrutiny include:

  • Packaged Foods & Health Claims:
    • ITC’s Aashirvaad atta (challenged in court over “100% atta,” “0% maida” claims).
    • Fortune cooking oil (flagged for “cholesterol-free” and “healthy lifestyle” claims).
    • Dabur (stayed order on “100% pure/organic” claims for honey, ghee, oils).
    • Small brands (Emami, Neuherbs, Troovy) penalized for unauthorized “healthy” and “organic” labels.
  • Nutrition & Infant Products:
    • Horlicks (questioned for bone/muscle health claims, sugar labeling, and pregnancy-related statements).
    • Nestlé (scrutinized for Nescafé mislabeling, Resource Renal compliance, and NAN/Lactogen infant formula claims).
    • A follow-up formula sample failed biotin standards.
  • Cooking Oils & Energy Drinks:
    • Freedom Rice Bran Oil (banned for cholesterol/heart-health claims; FSSAI disallowed “energy drink” descriptor).
    • Red Bull, PepsiCo, Monster, Reliance challenged FSSAI’s ban in court, with interim relief granted but stock shortages looming.
  • Restaurants & E-Commerce:
    • Fast-food chains (KFC, McDonald’s, Dominos) and hotels (Marriott, Hilton) fined for calorie disclosure violations.
    • Amazon/Flipkart issued notices; an Amazon warehouse license was canceled.
  • Prosecutions for Safety Violations:
    • Priyagold biscuits (used cattle feed bran; hygiene failures led to license suspension).
    • Dodla Dairy (milk contained detergent/caustic soda; fat content below standards).
    • Ueir Organic Foods (raisins exceeded pesticide limits).
    • Other firms (Narpadham, Sun Ridges) penalized for storage and documentation lapses.

The crackdown reflects growing consumer demand for transparency, with legal battles ongoing over disputed claims
