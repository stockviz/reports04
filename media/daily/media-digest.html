Shrinking Kaziranga’s Buffer: A Threat to Wildlife, Communities, and Conservation

BCG

The Assam government plans to reduce Kaziranga National Park’s 10‑km eco‑sensitive zone (ESZ) to just 1 km, ostensibly for infrastructure development. In reality, this move would facilitate private projects such as a new stadium and a 100‑crore five‑star hotel chain, jeopardizing critical wildlife corridors that enable rhinos, elephants, tigers, and other species to escape seasonal floods. The proposed shrinkage threatens to increase road‑kills, human‑wildlife conflict, poaching risk, and genetic isolation of the world’s largest one‑horned rhinoceros population. While officials claim 2 million residents would be displaced, existing villages predate the ESZ and are largely allowed under current regulations. Indigenous activists, including Prana Dholi, have protested and faced arrests, highlighting a broader struggle over who decides the park’s future. The issue underscores the tension between commercial tourism ambitions and the urgent need to preserve Kaziranga’s ecological integrity

Make in India at 12 Years: Achievements, Challenges, and the Road Ahead

BEL

The panel celebrated the 12th anniversary of the Make in India initiative, highlighting sectoral gains—particularly in electronics, air‑conditioners, toys, and furniture—where indigenisation has risen sharply.
