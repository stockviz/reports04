IOC to Acquire Own Crude and LPG Vessels Amid West Asia Shipping Risks

IOC

Indian Oil Corp (IOC) plans to buy its own crude oil and LPG carriers after rising freight costs and war‑risk insurance due to tensions around the Strait of Hormuz. The board has approved initial purchases, and tenders were issued months ago. IOC is evaluating three strategies: optimizing current time‑charter contracts, owning vessels, and supporting domestic shipbuilding under Make‑in‑India. Time‑chartered ships currently cost $2‑2.5 per barrel versus $8‑10 spot rates; LPG freight has surged from $40 to $160‑180 per tonne. The move aligns with IOC’s broader push to diversify crude sourcing—55% from Russia, 24% from the Middle East—and to reduce reliance on spot purchases, which now make up ~87% of imports. Despite geopolitical volatility, IOC maintained refinery operations, built inventories, and posted a net profit of Rs 21,630 crore in Q3, driven by inventory gains and higher margins. The vessel acquisition and potential domestic shipbuilding aim to bolster logistics resilience for India’s oil imports

Indian Oil Corp. to Acquire Own Crude and LPG Vessels Amid West Asia Shipping Risks

IOC

Indian Oil Corporation (IOC) plans to buy its own crude oil and LPG carriers after freight rates and war‑risk insurance surged due to disruptions around the Strait of Hormuz. The board has approved initial purchases, and tenders were issued months ago; IOC is also considering domestic shipbuilding under “Make in India.” Currently, IOC charters three crude and nine LPG vessels, saving significantly versus spot market rates. The move aims to secure shipping capacity, diversify crude sourcing (55% from Russia, 24% from the Middle East, etc.), and reduce reliance on spot purchases. Despite volatile oil prices, IOC posted a net profit of Rs 21,630 crore in the September quarter, boosted by inventory gains and higher refining margins, while warning that geopolitical developments remain a key uncertainty. The initiative reflects a broader effort by Indian oil firms to strengthen logistics resilience

Indian VC/PE Firms Eye Equity Stakes in Platform Holdings to Extend Value Creation

HGM BCG GAJA

Indian venture capital and private equity firms, traditionally earning through management fees and carried interest, are now considering a third revenue stream: holding equity in a fund‑management platform that could eventually be listed. Ashish Kumar of Fundamentum Partnership predicts 4‑5 firms will explore this model within 2‑3 years, aiming to retain high‑growth tech investments longer than the typical 5‑6‑year fund life and give limited partners continuous exposure without repeatedly committing to new funds. Public‑market listings of asset‑management firms (e.g., Blackstone, KKR, TPG) illustrate the model’s viability, and India’s first pure‑play PE firm, Gaja Capital, recently listed. Kumar argues entrepreneur‑led firms are better suited to build enduring platforms, using structures like continuation funds, to overcome succession challenges that cause many VC funds to fade after their founders depart. The goal is to create an institution that generates its own equity value and outlives its founders

SBI Narrows NIM Gap with HDFC Bank as Both Face Margin Pressure

SBIN HDFCBANK

State Bank of India has steadily closed the net interest margin (NIM) gap with HDFC Bank, shrinking from a 106‑basis‑point lead for HDFC in FY21 to just 26 bps by Q2 FY27. The convergence stems more from a 94‑bp decline in HDFC’s NIM—driven by the 2023‑24 merger with HDFC Ltd and higher low‑interest housing loans—than from SBI’s modest 14‑bp dip. Both banks confront margin compression from costly FCNR‑B deposits, but RBI’s recent repo‑rate hikes should provide some tailwinds. SBI’s loan book grew 17.8% YoY to ₹52 trn, deposits rose 12.3% to ₹63 trn, and its profit‑per‑employee surged, reflecting strong digital investments and efficiency gains. HDFC Bank, with 70% floating‑rate assets, also expects benefits from rate hikes, while analysts anticipate margin recovery as FCNR‑B funds shift to retail lending

Tata Chemicals posts Rs 50 cr loss in Q2 FY27 amid higher energy, logistics costs and lower realisations

TATACHEM

Tata Chemicals reported a consolidated loss of Rs 50 cr for Q2 FY27, down from a Rs 154 cr profit a year earlier, due to lower realisations, rising energy and logistics expenses, reduced other income and JV earnings. Revenue rose 8% to Rs 4,177 cr driven by higher volumes in India and the US and a temporary shutdown in Kenya. CEO R. Mukundan highlighted resilient operating performance, cost discipline and focus on cash generation, but noted pressure from global soda‑ash oversupply, elevated input costs, and the impact of the Middle East conflict and Kenya suspension on consolidated results. The company remains cautiously optimistic, citing its integrated operations, balanced sheet strength and portfolio transformation as foundations for future growth

ED Raids Expose Unauthorized Cross‑Border Crypto Derivative Trading by CoinDCX and Delta Exchange

AAATECH DELTACORP

The Enforcement Directorate conducted raids on CoinDCX and Delta Exchange premises in Bengaluru on 4‑5 November, uncovering extensive violations of FEMA 1999. Both platforms were found offering futures, options, and tokenised foreign‑stock trading without regulatory approval, using stablecoins such as USDT to pay transaction fees and route trades through overseas exchanges like Binance, KuCoin, Gate.io, and Bybit. Investigators discovered hundreds of crores paid as fees and several thousand crores in cross‑border derivative volumes, indicating large‑scale contraventions of FEMA, SEBI, RBI and securities laws. The findings highlight unauthorized international crypto transactions and potential breaches of Indian financial regulations

Berkshire Hathaway’s Q3 Record Buybacks Fade as Stock Rises

PACIFICI

Berkshire reported $13 billion in operating earnings, beating expectations, and set a record $9.6 billion in share repurchases in Q3, signaling confidence in the stock’s valuation. The buyback pace, roughly 3% of Berkshire’s $1.25 trillion market cap, remains modest compared with peers. The company’s cash fell to $352 billion after a $7 billion acquisition of home‑builder Taylor Morrison. BNSF Railway showed strong profit growth, while insurance underwriting profits fell >40% but beat forecasts. Berkshire’s equity activity was muted, with a net $1 billion sell‑off in Q3. Analysts see limited upside; UBS’s Brian Meredith maintains a Buy rating with a $942,000 target for Class A shares, above his $815,000 intrinsic‑value estimate
