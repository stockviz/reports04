RBI Draft Rules Targeting Revolving Credit in NBFCs

BAJFINANCE IIFL TATACAP

The RBI has proposed draft guidelines that would prohibit non‑bank financial companies (NBFCs) from offering revolving credit facilities, restricting them to term loans only. This change could significantly affect NBFCs that rely on flexi‑loan products, notably Bajaj Finance and Tata Capital, which have substantial exposure—up to 20% of Bajaj’s standalone assets and up to 50% on a consolidated basis, with Tata Capital facing high single‑digit to low double‑digit exposure. The loss of redraw options may slow loan growth, reduce fee income, and pressure yields, while also potentially exposing underlying borrower stress as customers lose a key liquidity tool. The guidelines are still in draft form and are expected to apply prospectively, allowing existing revolving loans to run their course, but the market is reacting to the possible impact on earnings and asset quality

MAG mandates drug testing for pilots after drug‑trafficking incident

Malaysia Aviation Group (MAG), the parent of Malaysia Airlines, has ordered mandatory drug screening for all 1,260 pilots, to be completed by August 15, with non‑compliant staff barred from flying. The move follows the arrest of a 39‑year‑old Malaysian pilot who was found with 26 kg of MDMA and tested positive for cocaine and methamphetamine, allegedly transporting the drugs to Indonesia for a payment of 50,000 ringgit. The pilot was off‑duty and travelling as an observer. MAG says the testing supplements existing random checks and will also apply to cabin crew. Malaysian authorities are tightening airport security and reviewing background and health checks for aviation personnel

Carraro India Q1 Review: Domestic Growth, Export Setbacks, and Outlook for Recovery

CARRARO

Carraro India posted a strong 26% rise in domestic revenue in Q1, while exports fell 14% due to logistical disruptions in Western Asia. Balaji Gopaland expects the export share to rebound to its historical 33‑35% and to recoup lost volumes by the second half of FY27, possibly extending into FY28 if needed. Margin pressure pushed EBITDA below the targeted 12%; the company forecasts an EBITDA margin between 11.3%‑11.6% for the year, with plans to restore localization content and pass through inflationary costs to customers. Overall, Carraro remains confident of returning to growth and margin targets within the next two quarters
