IRDAI Proposes Major Overhaul of Insurance Distribution: Commission Caps, Dark‑Pattern Ban, and MII Framework

HGS

The IRDAI has released a 160‑page consultation paper (feedback open until 25 Oct) calling for sweeping reforms in Indian insurance distribution. Key proposals include stricter caps on commissions and expenses of management (EOM) by line of business, a ban on “dark patterns” that force consumers to share personal data before seeing product pricing and features, and the creation of Mutual Insurance Intermediaries (MIIs) – modeled on Bhima Sugam – with a 5 % commission ceiling. Former regulator Thomas Devasya views the caps as aggressive but necessary, arguing that past EOM limits alone failed to curb arbitrage and that a shift toward risk‑based capital is essential. Swami Saransharma warns that sharp commission cuts could squeeze distributor margins, reduce operational scale, and may not boost penetration as the TER reduction did for mutual funds, given the fundamental difference between investment and risk‑cover products. Both panelists note implementation challenges, especially for digital aggregators needing lead data, and stress that while the reforms aim to lower distribution costs, they could strain intermediaries unless paired with robust capital and data‑infrastructure measures
