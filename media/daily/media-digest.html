Polycab case highlights misuse of IBC for modest dues

POLYCAB INDORAMA 3MINDIA ALKALI PYRAMID

The admission of a ₹2.79 cr operational debt petition against financially robust wire‑and‑cable maker Polycab India sparked concern that India’s Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) is being weaponised as a payment‑pressurisation tool. Despite Polycab’s ₹4,194 cr cash balance and ₹12,009 cr shareholder net worth, the NCLT admitted the case, prompting a swift stay by NCLAT. Experts warn that operational creditors—often MSMEs—can trigger insolvency for debts of just ₹1 cr, creating regulatory fallout, investor unease, and commercial disruption for large corporates. Supreme Court and NCLAT rulings (2026, 2025) have cautioned against using IBC for simple debt recovery, emphasizing its purpose for genuine insolvency. Data show over 4,100 operational creditor petitions since June 2026, with many withdrawals, settlements, or liquidations. For small suppliers, even a few crores can jeopardise working capital, yet insolvency proceedings rarely ensure payment and add legal costs. Recent policy moves—the 2026 amendment to the MSME Development Act, creation of MSEFCs, the Samadhaan portal, and TReDS—aim to speed dispute resolution and financing, but challenges remain as arbitration awards are often contested and many MSMEs still lack access to redress mechanisms. The Polycab episode underscores the need to curb IBC’s misuse while strengthening effective, proportionate remedies for genuine payment disputes

The airport operator has told airlines they may shift operations to the new Navi Mumbai International Airport, suspend flights until Terminal 1 reopens in 2030, or explore other options from summer 2027, as a 5‑million‑passenger capacity cut is needed for Terminal 1’s redevelopment. Airlines, represented by the Airline Operators Committee, reject any forced transfer of international traffic to Navi Mumbai and question the justification for slot reductions, demanding a detailed refurbishment plan and assurance that no unilateral slot changes will occur. A meeting is set for October 28 to resolve the dispute, with carriers citing cost, operational efficiency, and logistical challenges of splitting services between the two airports

Hindustan Zinc Cuts Production Costs Amid Record Q2 Profit

VEDL HINDZINC

Hindustan Zinc, the world’s largest integrated zinc producer, expects zinc production costs of $825‑$850 per tonne for the year, down from earlier forecasts of $975‑$1,000. The decline, driven by strong by‑product realisations amid geopolitical disruptions, lowered Q3 costs to $703 per tonne (29% YoY) and $776 per tonne for April‑September (23% YoY), though high energy prices added $50/tonne. Revenue jumped 77% YoY to ₹15,093 crore, EBITDA more than doubled to ₹9,285 crore, and net profit rose 142% to ₹6,412 crore. Silver contributed 45% of profitability, with sales up 141% YoY. Costs may rise in H2 due to falling sulfuric acid prices and input‑cost inflation

Carmakers Expand Powertrain Options in India to Meet Shifting Consumer Preferences

KIRLOSBROS TATAMOTORS M&M MARUTI BCG

Indian automakers are broadening their offerings beyond petrol and diesel, adding CNG, hybrid, and electric models to address rising fuel costs and stricter emissions rules. European brands like Renault, Volkswagen Group, and Stellantis are introducing multi‑powertrain lineups, while domestic players such as Mahindra, Tata Motors, and Maruti Suzuki have already embraced varied drivetrains. Renault aims to increase market coverage from 30% to 80% with diverse engines, noting CNG accounts for 40% of sales in Mumbai. The Federation of Automobile Dealers Associations reports petrol’s share fell to 41% while CNG, hybrid, and EVs rose to 41% of sales. Manufacturers stress regional preferences and plan numerous new models and upgrades through 2027 to capture these evolving market segments

SRM

The city’s proposed Delivery Protection Act would force Amazon to directly employ over 5,000 drivers now working for independent contractors, prompting Amazon to warn it may shut 15 last‑mile facilities and eliminate thousands of warehouse jobs. The bill aims to improve worker protections, but critics argue it could trigger job losses and higher delivery costs. The dispute echoes Amazon’s 2023 Quebec warehouse closures amid union activity. Negotiations may seek a compromise that retains third‑party contractors while enhancing safety and labor standards, balancing worker rights with the risk of reduced service and higher consumer prices

India Smartphone Shipments Drop to Decade Low Amid Price Shock

VIVOBIOT

India’s smartphone shipments fell 26% YoY to 35.8 million units in Q3 2026, the lowest in ten years, as component shortages drove up prices and dampened festive-season demand. Online sales slumped, hurting Chinese brands whose market share fell to 68%, while Samsung reclaimed the top spot with 6.7 million units and Apple entered the top five with 3.9 million units, boosted by premium models and financing incentives. Vivo, Oppo and Xiaomi also saw sharp declines. Analysts warn the market may further contract in 2027, with pricing pressure and longer device lifecycles remaining key challenges
