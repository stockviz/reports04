India’s Sugar Market Outlook Amid Drought and Price Controls

The panel discussed a looming global sugar deficit of about 3 million tonnes, driven by drought in the EU and reduced Indian output. In India, crop yields are expected to fall 10‑20% in key states (Karnataka, Maharashtra), potentially cutting total production to 27‑28 million tonnes—down from 31 million tonnes last year—raising the risk of India becoming a net importer. Domestic sugar prices are capped around ₹44‑₹46 per kg, limiting upside despite market pressures; the current MSP of ₹31 per kg is deemed out of sync with market prices (mid‑40s). Ethanol diversion is unlikely unless sugar prices rise sharply or ethanol prices are revised upward. Festival demand is expected to be strong, but overall consumption may be tempered by El Niño‑related headwinds and rising edible‑oil costs. Government measures, including bimonthly quotas and higher inventory releases (stock projected at ~3.2 million tonnes), should keep domestic supply ample and curb further price spikes

Rising NCD Burden Threatens Global Economic Growth

Roche executive Jörg Rupp warned that the growing prevalence of non‑communicable diseases—affecting 3 billion people and causing 43 million deaths annually—poses a major economic risk by reducing productivity and straining healthcare systems designed for acute care. With two‑thirds of diseases inadequately treated and half the world lacking essential services, the aging population will exacerbate pressures. Rupp highlighted that innovation offers a “once‑in‑a‑generation” opportunity: over 12,900 medicines in development target NCDs, and shifting mindsets toward continuous care could capture their value. He cited strong economic returns, estimating $7 of growth for every $1 invested in NCD prevention, potentially saving 60 million lives and adding $12 trillion to global GDP by 2040. Roche, with CHF 61.5 billion in 2025 sales and CHF 12 billion R&D spend, aims to prevent, stop, and cure disease across the patient journey
