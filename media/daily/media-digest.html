Manipal Hospitals Targets Expansion and AI‑Driven Care Across India

MANIPALHOS SAHYADRI

Manipal Health Enterprises plans to broaden its footprint in Telangana, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh, Chhattisgarh and other regions while adopting a “physical plus digital” model powered by AI. The firm is pursuing acquisitions in Karnataka, Maharashtra, Goa, and Eastern states, and has secured land for a greenfield hospital in Juhu, Mumbai (Rs 495 cr). It is scaling a unified Hospital Information System, AI‑enabled nursing handovers at 24 hospitals, wireless patient monitoring, and AI chat/voice call‑centre automation. Clinical focus remains on six specialties (“CONGO‑R”), contributing 64.3% of inpatient revenue. Financials for FY 2025‑26 show 25.4% revenue growth to Rs 10,336 cr and EBITDA of Rs 2,644 cr, after integrating Sahyadri and Medica Synergie. The group aims to add 2,426 licensed beds by 2030 (483 brownfield, 1,943 greenfield), bringing its network to 49 hospitals, 13,037 beds across 14 states, serving over 7.6 million patients

India’s Compressed Biogas Potential Estimated at 62 MMT, Animal Waste Leads

India could generate about 62 million metric tonnes (MMT) of compressed biogas (CBG) annually from an estimated 472 MMT of feedstock. Animal waste offers the biggest share—≈190 MMT of dung could produce ≈25 MMT of CBG (41% of total), though only ~15% is currently collectable and dense rural clusters are needed. Agricultural residues (≈150 MMT) could yield ≈20 MMT of CBG, municipal solid waste (≈62 MMT) about 5 MMT, sewage‑treatment‑plant biomass (≈50 MMT) roughly 10 MMT, and sugar‑mill press mud (≈20 MMT) about 2 MMT. Key constraints are feedstock aggregation, limited collection windows, and low waste‑treatment rates. CBG production would curb stubble burning (≈87 MMT burnt annually), cut 0.8‑1.2 t CO₂e per tonne diverted, capture methane from ~2,400 dumpsites, generate organic manure, and help replace imported gas, potentially supporting ≈230 MMSCMD of biogas

Oracle Founder Larry Ellison Cancels $7.5 B Stock Sale

OFSS AMDIND

Executive Chairman Larry Ellison scrapped a 10b5‑1 plan to sell up to 50 million Oracle shares, valued at about $7.5 billion, amid concerns over the company’s heavy debt load and a volatile stock performance. The decision keeps his >40 % ownership intact as Oracle seeks $40 billion in debt and equity financing, including a recent $20 billion stock sale, while navigating restructuring costs and mixed investor sentiment on its AI‑driven growth strategy

Poetry Goes Reel: Social Media Revives Verse

3MINDIA LGHL

Social media platforms, especially short‑form reels, are reshaping how poetry is created, discovered, and consumed in India and beyond. Poets like Midha, Singh, Azher and Bootwala use Instagram, WhatsApp and podcasts to reach millions, blending performance, videography and branding with the craft. The format favors immediacy—gateway lines, relatable themes, and concise forms such as Urdu shers—while still allowing longer pieces to go viral. This visibility has broadened audiences, revived interest in Hindi, Urdu and regional languages, and fostered online communities that drive readers to books, though the translation of online fame into sustained sales remains uneven. Publishers note growth in poetry purchases by young, first‑time readers, yet many still view poetry as niche. Overall, reels have turned poetry from a niche, intent‑driven activity into an everyday, shareable experience that sparks curiosity, belonging and a new wave of poetic expression

RBI Forces Tata Sons IPO, Group Likely to Contest Decision

SUNPHARMA BCG AVAILFC NSIL

The Reserve Bank of India rejected Tata Sons’ March 2024 request to surrender its core investment company licence, directing the holding company of India’s largest business conglomerate to list its shares by September 2025. The move comes amid internal challenges: Chairman Noel Tata must navigate a pending board meeting, the departure of N. Chandrasekaran, and a legal stalemate involving the Sir Ratan Tata Trust. Tata’s major minority shareholder, Shapoorji Pallonji Group (18.37%), could benefit from a public offering. Executives and lawyers expect Tata Sons to challenge the RBI directive in the Bombay High Court, while also preparing for a possible IPO that would bring greater transparency and governance

OpenAI Pushes Back IPO to Prioritize AI Safety

DELTACORP

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman announced the company will defer its planned 2026 IPO to the following year, citing the need to address serious safety risks—including a self‑estimated 10% chance of catastrophic outcomes within the decade. He stressed the importance of avoiding actions that could cede control of the future to AI and hinted at greater coordination with rivals such as Anthropic and DeepMind. Parallel calls for a development slowdown have come from Anthropic’s Dario Amodei, who proposes permanent third‑party safety reviewers for AI firms. The consensus among top AI leaders is to focus on safety and ethical safeguards before pursuing further growth or a public listing
