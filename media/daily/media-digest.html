Supreme Court Upholds NCLAT Order: Videocon Insolvency Case Sent Back for Fresh Bids

BLUESTARCO AARON MAHABANK IFCI SBIN VEDL

The Supreme Court (SC) upheld the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal’s (NCLAT) decision to send Videocon’s insolvency resolution plan back to the Committee of Creditors (CoC) for fresh bidding. The dispute revolved around Vedanta-backed Twin Star’s proposal to pay dissenting creditors, which creditors argued violated the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) by offering less than liquidation-value entitlements and partial payments via non-convertible debentures (NCDs) instead of cash.

Key points: - NCLAT’s reasoning: The approved plan (95.09% CoC approval) failed to protect dissenting creditors’ statutory rights and lacked Competition Commission of India (CCI) approval. - Dissenting creditors (Bank of Maharashtra, SIDBI, IFCI) challenged the plan, citing haircuts exceeding 95% and discrepancies in distribution. - Twin Star’s offer: ₹2,962 crore for 13 Videocon group companies, against admitted claims of ₹64,838 crore. - SC’s role: Confirmed NCLAT’s authority to demand fresh bids, aligning with IBC guidelines. - Separate issue: Videocon promoter Venugopal Dhoot was declared bankrupt by NCLT (Nov 2026) over ₹12,668 crore dues to SBI, while his appeal on overseas assets remains pending.

The SC’s decision allows lenders to restart the resolution process with new bids
