TRAI Tightens Spam Call Rules, Limits Truecaller’s Controls on 1600 & 140 Numbers

The Telecom Regulatory Authority (TRAI) has introduced stricter regulations on unsolicited commercial communications. It bans call‑blocking and identification apps like Truecaller from restricting calls from 1600 (banking) and 140 (utility) number series, deeming them non‑spam. Additionally, such apps must share spam‑report data with the Digital Ledger Technology (DLT) platform maintained by telecom operators to continue offering blocking or reporting services. Truecaller has contested the fairness of these rules, arguing that users frequently mark these numbers as spam

FCNR(B) Scheme Could Yield Trillions in Notional Profit for Banks, RBI Says

SBIN

SBI Research argues that the Foreign Currency Non‑Resident (FCNR) B deposit scheme can generate roughly ₹5 trillion in notional profit for banks over five years and add about ₹50,000 crore to RBI’s balance sheet. Mobilising USD 127 billion, the scheme could create an additional credit multiplier of ~2.5, translating to ₹25 lakh crore in new loans and annual interest income of ₹1.8 trillion at a 7.5% yield. After accounting for interest outgo of ₹75,000 crore and hedging costs of about USD 15 billion, banks could net roughly ₹5 trillion over five years, while RBI could earn ~USD 5 billion (₹50,000 crore) by deploying the funds at a 4% yield. The report rebuts earlier estimates of a ₹5 trillion loss, stating they double‑count the same FX exposure and rely on a severe 5% annual rupee depreciation assumption, whereas a more moderate 3% scenario is used

Sunscreen Moves From Niche to Mainstream in India, Driven by Rising Heat and Consumer Awareness

SANDESH HINDUNILVR LAOPALA HONASA

In four years, Indian households buying sunscreen have tripled to 14.3 million (CAGR ≈ 36%). Rural penetration rose to 2.4 % and urban to 7.1 %, yet overall market share remains low (≈ 6 % of skincare vs ≈ 11 % in the US/Korea), indicating room for growth. Drivers include hotter, unpredictable weather, greater sun‑exposure awareness, and positioning sunscreen as daily anti‑ageing care. FMCG giants (HUL, L’Oréal, Shiseido) are expanding affordable and premium lines, while D2C brands (Honasa’s Derma Cos, Mamaearth) target 100‑crore‑ARR categories. Digital education and legal disputes over SPF claims are sharpening product scrutiny, but the sector is poised for continued expansion across urban and rural India

India’s IEX HP‑DAM Hits 20‑Unit Ceiling Amid Record Demand

IEX TIMESGTY

The Indian Energy Exchange’s High Price Day‑Ahead Market (HP‑DAM) repeatedly hit its 20 Rs/unit ceiling from Sept 6‑17 as non‑solar demand surged. Peak demand reached a record 269 GW on Sept 10, driven by cooling, irrigation, and a 15 % rainfall deficit. Supply tightened due to lower hydro output and seasonal coal availability disruptions, prompting higher purchase bids that outpaced sell bids. The regular day‑ahead market saw average clearing prices of Rs 7.83/unit for Sept 1‑17—more than double the same period last year—while HP‑DAM helped meet critical power needs during this tight‑supply phase

Uber Found Vicariously Liable for Fatal Accident, Parents Awarded $40 Million

Parents of Emily Normandin‑Parker received $40 million after an arbitrator ruled that both Uber and driver Vu Tran were jointly responsible for her death when Tran illegally stopped on a highway gore point after she exited the vehicle. The decision rejected Uber’s claim that it is merely a platform connecting riders with independent contractors, emphasizing the company’s duty to ensure passenger safety. The family plans to use the settlement to advocate for stronger ride‑hailing safety standards, fund scholarships, mentorships, and support LGBTQ organizations through the Emily Normandin‑Parker Foundation

Dubai Hotels: Occupancy Recovers but Rates Lag Ahead of Winter Season

SANDESH INDHOTEL TAJGVK

Dubai’s hotel industry is seeing occupancy rates rebound toward pre-disruption levels—hitting 70% in September and projected to reach 80-85% in December—but room rates are expected to remain 10-15% lower than 2025 due to aggressive discounting to fill rooms. Key drivers include Indian travelers, who are a major source of demand amid lingering international flight disruptions.

Executives from Taj Dubai, NH Collection Dubai The Palm, and Dukes The Palm noted that while occupancy is improving (August 2026: 66%, up from 36% in March), revenue recovery lags. Festive seasons like Diwali, Christmas, and New Year will boost bookings, though average daily rates (ADR) will stay suppressed. Indian demand—particularly in leisure, MICE (meetings, incentives, conferences, exhibitions), and weddings—is critical, with hotels prioritizing occupancy over rate hikes.

The market has exited crisis mode but remains short of full recovery, with international air connectivity cited as a key recovery factor. December projections target 75-80% occupancy, though executives emphasize that **rates will normalize gradually
