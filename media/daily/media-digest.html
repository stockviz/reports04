Dopamine Sites: Simulated Consumption for Anticipatory Pleasure

Dopamine sites are digital platforms that mimic the rituals of shopping, ordering food, or planning travel without any real transaction or delivery. Originating in South Korea and now spreading in India, sites like FoodNeverComes and TripNeverLeaves let users browse, add items to carts, and experience simulated checkouts, capitalising on the brain’s “anticipatory utility” – the pleasure of wanting rather than actually receiving a product. Founders argue the act of ordering itself can be rewarding, especially for impulsive or bored consumers. While the novelty may fade, researchers see potential for these platforms to serve as a release valve for consumer urges, though their long‑term efficacy and monetisation remain uncertain
