Maharashtra Charity Commissioner’s Non‑Proposal Sparks Trust Board Freeze and Tata Sons Deadlock

NSIL

The Maharashtra Charity Commissioner’s office confirmed it never proposed the Maharashtra Public Trusts (Amendment) Ordinance, 2025, which caps perpetual trustees at one‑fourth of a trust’s board. Introduced by the state’s Law and Judiciary Department and enacted on 1 Sept 2025, the rule led the commissioner on 15 May to halt Sir Ratan Tata Trust’s (SRTT) board meetings, despite the trust’s three perpetual trustees being appointed before the amendment. The freeze has stalled SRTT’s decisions, prevented it from sending representatives to Tata Sons’ AGM, and caused a quorum failure, delaying the meeting and prolonging a leadership dispute in Tata Sons. The deadlock has drawn political commentary, with opposition leaders accusing political interference, while the finance minister rejected such characterizations. The charity commissioner’s office reiterated it had not submitted any proposal to the Law and Judiciary Department, which declined to disclose drafting details

India’s First Indigenous Bullet Train: Inside the High‑Speed Rail’s Design, Comfort and Tech

BEML TIMESGTY

The new eight‑car, 202‑metre long bullet train for the Mumbai‑Ahmedabad corridor, built by BEML, will run at up to 249 km/h (tested to 280 km/h) with a capacity of about 557 passengers. It features four motor and four trailer cars, with standard‑class 3‑2 seats, an executive 2‑2 coach, and two spaces for passengers with reduced mobility. Interiors include ergonomic seats, LED lighting, ample luggage racks, power outlets, bio‑digester toilets, large windows, and a sealed gangway. Passenger information systems provide multilingual LCD screens, PA announcements, Wi‑Fi infotainment, and emergency talk‑back units. Accessibility is ensured through level boarding, retractable steps, wheelchair‑friendly doors, dedicated seating, accessible toilets, and Braille signs. HVAC units, sound insulation, noise‑damping coatings, skirts, bogie covers and fairings reduce temperature variations, noise and vibration. Technologically, the train uses a 25 kV AC traction system, regenerative braking, and aerodynamic shaping (CFD‑optimised nose, roof panels, airflow deflectors) to lower air resistance and tunnel pressure. Two eight‑car sets can be coupled for a 16‑car formation, offering operational flexibility

Banks Raise Deposit Rates as RBI’s Hike Triggers Liquidity Tightening

BANKINDIA INDIANB

About a month after the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) raised policy rates for the first time in four years, domestic banks—including Bank of India (BoI)—are gradually increasing deposit rates amid shrinking liquidity and rising credit demand. BoI’s recent hike (10–35 basis points for 1–3-year tenures) marks the second such move in the current cycle, with rates now ranging from 6.60% to 6.85% for select deposits.

The adjustment reflects higher deposit costs and a shift in the liquidity profile, as systemic liquidity has dropped from ₹11 lakh crore in early September to ₹1.96 lakh crore due to bond sales and reverse repo operations. Tighter conditions are evident in the certificate of deposit (CD) market, where rates have surged to over 8%—the highest since early 2023. Earlier, Indian Bank had raised rates on special term deposits (555/777 days).

BoI’s executive attributed the move to “higher cost of deposits” and the need to align with the RBI’s rate-hike cycle, noting that benefits from the FCNR(B) scheme inflows are fading. While deposit rates lag behind lending rates, the trend signals a broader hardening of monetary conditions
