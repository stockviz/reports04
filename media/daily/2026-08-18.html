Emergency in the Skies: Investigation into Air India Flight 2379’s Sudden Hydraulic Failure and Pilot Testing Scrutiny**

On 4 August 2026, Air India Flight 2379 (Phuket to Delhi) experienced a sudden loss of hydraulic pressure in all three systems (Green, Blue, Yellow) within seconds, rendering flight controls unresponsive for four seconds. The Airbus A320 pitched up, lost altitude, and injured 24 people (20 passengers, 4 crew). The autopilot disconnected at 04:02:46 GMT, but systems recovered shortly, allowing a controlled landing.

Key Findings & Investigation Focus

  • Hydraulic System Failure: Airbus’ preliminary report confirms a simultaneous pressure drop in all three systems, though the root cause remains unclear. Investigators are examining:
    • Genuine pressure loss vs. faulty sensors/transducers (pressure-measuring devices).
    • Leak-detection switches and wiring integrity.
    • Maintenance history and flight data/voice recorders for clues.
  • Pilot Actions: While the technical failure occurred independently of pilot input, the pilot-in-command tested positive for a psychoactive substance (sleep medication). The investigation will assess:
    • Whether the pilot was impaired during the incident.
    • Why the crew continued to Delhi instead of landing at nearby Kolkata (a safer option).
    • Separation of issues: The hydraulic failure and pilot’s condition are being treated as distinct but potentially compounding factors.

Potential Industry Changes

  • Technical Safeguards: Airbus may mandate wider inspections of A320 hydraulic systems, pressure switches, and transducers across fleets.
  • Stricter Pilot Testing: Air India has expanded drug/alcohol screening for pilots beyond standard protocols. Globally, countries like the US (25% annual random drug testing), Australia (no-notice testing), and UK (random screening during medical renewals) enforce rigorous checks.
  • Regulatory Review: The Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau (AAIB), with Airbus and France’s BEA, will determine if systemic flaws exist or if this was an isolated event.

Outcome Uncertain

The final report will clarify whether the hydraulic failure, pilot’s condition, or both contributed to the severity of the incident—and whether new safety protocols are needed for India’s aviation sector

India’s Shadow Loan Dilemma: Balancing Risk and Financial Inclusion

ABCAPITAL BAJFINANCE

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is considering stricter rules on flexi loans (revolving credit with repayment flexibility) offered by nonbank financiers, proposing to restrict them to banks and credit-card issuers only. The move aims to curb evergreening of bad debt—where borrowers roll over loans to avoid defaults—given the challenge of supervising over 9,000 nonbank lenders compared to just 100 banks.

However, critics argue the ban overlooks India’s precarious employment landscape, where 200 million self-employed individuals (freelancers, small business owners) lack access to traditional banking. Nonbank flexi loans, used responsibly by firms like Bajaj Finance and Aditya Birla Capital, offer lower interest costs (200–250 bps savings) and structured repayment schedules, reducing systemic risk. A blanket ban could push borrowers to fragmented term loans, increasing costs and regulatory complexity without addressing root issues.

The RBI is urged to use its regulatory sandbox to test flexi loans in real time, gathering data on borrower behavior. Instead of banning innovation, the focus should be on guardrails—like capping limits, enforcing amortization, and mandating external repayments—to protect against misuse while expanding financial inclusion. The debate highlights a broader need: formalizing credit for India’s informal economy as AI reshapes employment, without stifling competition or excluding vulnerable groups
