Supreme Court Rejects Karnataka Discoms’ Plea to Halt Adani Power Invoices

KTKBANK ADANIPOWER

The Supreme Court dismissed a petition by Karnataka electricity distributors seeking to stay Rs 1,005 crore invoices issued by Adani Power. The bench upheld the Appellate Tribunal for Electricity’s decision, which had refused to stay the invoices and affirmed the Central Electricity Regulatory Commission’s order requiring the discoms to pay the full amount within 45 days, including carrying costs and late payment surcharge. The court directed the tribunal to deliver a final order within three months. Karnataka discoms had argued they owed nothing and that the order was unjust, warning that paying the sum would strain their finances and affect consumer supply

Fertiliser Giants Expand into Specialty Products

DEEPAKFERT SMLISUZU COROMANDEL BCG

India’s leading fertiliser manufacturers—including Coromandel International, Deepak Fertilisers, ICL Group, and SML—are scaling up production of specialty, crop‑specific, water‑soluble and high‑efficiency fertilizers. Lab‑Grown Diamonds Gain Momentum in India’s Tier‑2/3 Cities

BROOKS-RE SHRINGARMS

Lab‑grown diamonds (LGDs) are rapidly expanding beyond metros, capturing nearly 10% of India’s ₹80,000 crore diamond market. Prices have widened, offering up to 90% discount versus natural stones—₹50‑80 k per carat versus ₹2‑3.5 L. Brands like Aukera, Limelight, and House of Quadri report strong uptake among 25‑45‑year‑olds in North, South, West, and emerging markets such as Pune, Indore, Jaipur, and Hyderabad. Rising gold prices, increased awareness, design flexibility, and domestic production of ~20 million carats annually are driving adoption, mirroring US trends where LGDs are now mainstream for engagement jewelry. Companies plan aggressive store expansion, targeting 100 outlets by 2026

Renewable Energy Developers Get Extension Relief Under New CERC Rules

HEROMOTOCO

The Central Electricity Regulatory Commission (CERC) has revised its General Network Access (GNA) regulations, allowing renewable energy developers more time to secure interstate transmission connectivity if they demonstrate progress and pay daily extension fees. Extensions include up to 3 months for land acquisition (with at least 20% land documentation), up to 6 months for financial closure, and up to 12 months for commissioning, with escalating charges of ₹1,000–₹1,300 per MW per day. Failure to meet milestones within the granted extensions will result in revocation of connectivity. The changes aim to reduce regulatory cliff risk and alleviate bottlenecks caused by delayed projects
