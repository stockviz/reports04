Regulatory Roadblock Threatens Tata Sons AGM

NSIL

The Tata Sons annual general meeting scheduled for August 18 is at risk because the Sir Ratan Tata Trust (SRTT), a 23.56% shareholder, cannot hold a board meeting or jointly nominate a representative with the Sir Dorabji Tata Trust (SDTT) due to a Maharashtra Charity Commissioner order. The order stems from alleged violations of the amended Section 30A(2) of the Maharashtra Public Trusts Act, which limits lifetime trustees to 25% of a trust board. Without the joint nominee, the required quorum of five members—including a trust‑jointly nominated representative—cannot be met, potentially forcing the AGM to be adjourned. The impasse coincides with Chairman N Chandrasekaran’s decision not to seek another term, adding urgency to resolve the trust governance issue, possibly by reappointing trustees on fixed terms. If the AGM fails to achieve quorum, Chandrasekaran will remain a director until a valid meeting is held

Tata Sons AGM in jeopardy over trust board dispute

NSIL

The Tata Sons AGM scheduled for August 18 may be postponed because the Sir Ratan Tata Trust (SRTT), a 23.56% shareholder, cannot nominate its representative due to a Maharashtra Charity Commissioner order that blocks SRTT from holding a board meeting. The order stems from alleged non‑compliance with Section 30A(2) of the Maharashtra Public Trusts Act, which limits lifetime trustees to 25% of a trust board; SRTT has three lifetime trustees out of six. Without a joint nominee from SRTT and the Sir Dorabji Tata Trust (SDTT), Tata Sons cannot meet the quorum of five members required by its Articles of Association. The impasse could be resolved if SRTT’s lifetime trustees step down and are re‑appointed on fixed terms, allowing a board meeting and joint nomination. Until quorum is secured, the AGM may be adjourned, leaving Chairman N. Chandrasekaran in place until a valid meeting is held. Tata Sons has not yet notified shareholders of any change to the AGM date

Maharashtra Cracks Down on Quick‑Commerce Food Safety

SWIGGY ETERNAL

Maharashtra’s new food safety chief, Tukaram Mundhe, intensified inspections, suspending permits for 12 warehouses of grocery giants Eternal (Blinkit), Swiggy’s Instamart and Zepto after finding cockroach infestations, rodent droppings, rotten produce and other unhygienic conditions. The raid, part of a broader crackdown that also shut four Domino’s outlets, covered 86 establishments, issued 60 improvement notices, and signals tougher enforcement of food‑safety standards in India’s fast‑growing “quick commerce” sector

Redcliffe Labs and Skyroot Launch Space‑Based Diagnostic Reagent Test

HALEOSLABS UNIMECH

Redcliffe Labs has partnered with Skyroot Aerospace to study how its dry diagnostic reagents perform in Low Earth Orbit through the DRIFT‑1 mission, which will expose the reagents to space for 90 days and compare results with ground controls. The project aims to create a flight‑heritage dataset, improve reagent stability for space applications, and ultimately develop more resilient point‑of‑care diagnostics for astronaut health, microgravity research, and challenging environments on Earth

M&M

Meta Shuts Down 750,000 Youth Accounts in Australia Amid Age‑Restriction Law

TANLA

Meta has removed access to over 750,000 Instagram and Facebook accounts in Australia deemed to belong to users under 16, following the country’s new law banning minors from social media. The deletions include 462,000 Instagram and 294,000 Facebook accounts, with 500,000 removed before the law took effect in December and the rest since. Despite these actions, a recent eSafety survey shows only a modest drop in under‑16 usage— from 85.9% to 81.5% —and many children remain on platforms because age‑verification prompts are not yet presented. The effectiveness of the ban is questioned, with regulators, parents, and academics seeking clearer evidence of compliance and impact

Luigi Mangione Pleads Guilty to Federal Stalking Charge in UnitedHealthcare CEO Murder

BCG

Luigi Mangione, 28, admitted to stalking and shooting UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson in Manhattan on Dec. 4, 2024, pleading guilty to a federal stalking charge. He used a fake investor ruse to learn about an investor conference, then ambushed Thompson, who died. Prosecutors seek life imprisonment; a state murder case remains pending, with defense arguing double jeopardy. Evidence includes a 3D‑printed pistol and a notebook detailing his motive. The case highlights tensions over health‑insurance practices, though officials condemn the violence
