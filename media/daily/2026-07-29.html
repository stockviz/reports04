ACME Solar Secures ₹3,405 cr PFC Loan for 250 MW Rajasthan‑Gujarat FDRE Project

URJA NHPC PFC ACMESOLAR

Renewable energy firm ACME Solar Holdings has obtained a ₹3,405 crore long‑term loan from Power Finance Corporation to finance its 250 MW Firm and Dispatchable Renewable Energy (FDRE) project across Rajasthan and Gujarat. The project, developed by subsidiary ACME Urja One, features a 25‑year PPA with NHPC at ₹4.33/kWh, a 19‑year repayment term, and combines solar, wind and battery storage to deliver firm power. With this financing, ACME’s total project funding this fiscal year reaches ₹6,051 crore. The 250 MW plant, located in Jaisalmer (Rajasthan) and Devbhumi Dwarka (Gujarat), has land and grid connections ready and is slated for commissioning next year. ACME Solar’s overall portfolio now stands at 8,070 MW, with 2,990 MW operational, 5,080 MW under construction, and about 3.62 GWh of battery storage under development

Capri Global Q1 Earnings Call: Strong Growth Amid Rising Gold‑Loan Delinquencies

CGCL

Management attributed the delinquency rise to the gold‑loan segment, noting gold prices fell 4% and LTV remains at ~71%, keeping actual credit losses minimal. Net interest margin held near 9.7‑10% despite low funding costs, expected to stay stable as the loan mix settles. The company targets ₹65,000 cr AUM by FY28, sees no immediate need for external capital, and remains confident in managing gold‑price volatility and sustaining profitability

Ekart Leverages Its Core Logistics Network to Power Flipkart’s Quick‑Commerce Expansion

NYKAA 3MINDIA

Ekart, Flipkart’s in‑house logistics arm, has repurposed its existing fulfillment centres to feed over 1,000 dark‑stores for the Flipkart Minutes quick‑commerce service, enabling 10‑minute to 2‑day deliveries across 130 cities, including tier‑III and IV towns. By treating fulfillment centres and dark‑stores as a single supply chain, Ekart reuses warehousing, line‑haul and last‑mile capacity, achieving 60‑70% automation (targeting 80‑90%) and cutting unit costs by ~40%. While the network gives Minutes a geographic head start, experts warn that true competitive advantage will depend on building dark‑store density, hyperlocal demand forecasting, and dedicated resources to avoid operational strain during peak periods. The franchise model now opens the supply chain to MSMEs, D2C, and FMCG brands, aiming for 1,000 outlets by end‑2026

Ellisons Brace for $9.8 Billion Hit if Paramount‑Warner Deal Falls Apart

LGBBROSLTD 5PAISA DELTACORP PARACABLES

Larry Ellison and his family could be liable for a $9.8 billion payout—$7 billion termination fee plus a $2.8 billion Netflix settlement—if Paramount’s acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery collapses over regulatory challenges. The fee would be covered by Ellison purchasing new Class B shares at $16.02 each, far above Paramount’s current $8 share price. If the deal succeeds, ongoing quarterly “ticking” fees of about $650 million will also be backed by Ellison’s equity commitment. The potential loss comes as Ellison’s fortune has fallen 31% this year, partly due to concerns over Oracle’s AI and data‑center spending
