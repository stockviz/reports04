India’s Beverage Industry Turns to Glass and PET as Aluminium Can Shortage Persists

VBL KNACK HINDNATGLS UBL

Rising costs and supply disruptions from the West Asia conflict have pushed aluminium can prices up by about 20%, prompting Indian cola and beer makers to shift orders to glass and PET bottles. Companies like Hindusthan National Glass report earlier‑than‑usual demand for glass, while brewers cite import challenges and limited domestic can capacity. The shortage, intensified since April due to Strait of Hormuz disruptions, has forced firms to secure raw materials and production slots well in advance of the festive Diwali season, highlighting the risks of over‑reliance on a single packaging format

Changing User Habits to Fuel HUL’s Next Growth Cycle

HINDUNILVR

Managing Director Priya Nair says Hindustan Unilever is targeting new consumption pockets by leveraging shifts such as wider LPG and tap‑water use, which alter cooking and cleaning habits, and the influence of India’s 377 million Gen‑Z consumers on brand discovery. While analysts question the gap between HUL’s stated growth opportunity and recent performance, Nair acknowledges a subdued demand environment, the need to revamp underperforming brands, and the challenge of scaling amid rising complexity across income segments and channels. HUL is also transforming its portfolio toward higher‑growth, premium categories—highlighting Dove as its fastest‑growing brand and potential second‑largest. The company will assess the impact of the 11,000 new brands launched since 2020 on a category‑by‑category basis

Weak Green Hydrogen Demand Delays L&T’s 1 GW Electrolyser Expansion

ATHERENERG LTF LT

Larsen & Toubro has postponed plans to expand its electrolyser manufacturing capacity from the current 400 MW to 1 GW at its Hazira, Gujarat plant, citing weaker-than-expected domestic green hydrogen demand. The company had earmarked Rs 450‑550 crore for the expansion by March 2027 and has already invested Rs 293 crore. While the existing assembly line can meet current demand, L&T’s MD says the facility can be scaled to 1 GW within 12‑18 months if more green hydrogen, ammonia, and methanol projects materialize, noting that such projects typically take 30‑40 months to complete. The electrolyser technology is based on pressurised alkaline systems licensed from France’s McPhy Energy
