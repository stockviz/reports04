Tata Motors eyes 80% shareholder approval to clinch €3.82 bn Iveco takeover

TATAMOTORS BCG

Tata Motors has cleared all regulatory hurdles and secured Exor’s 27.06% stake for its €3.82 bn acquisition of Iveco. The tender offer opens on Sept 7, requiring acceptance of at least 80% of the remaining public shares (52.94% of total) to trigger a post‑offer restructuring that lets Tata gain full operational control and delist Iveco. The offer price is €14.10 per share, deemed fair by Goldman Sachs and Rothschild & Co. If the 80% threshold is met, Tata will combine the two firms—selling over 590,000 vehicles annually and generating ~€21 bn in revenue—while preserving Iveco’s Turin headquarters and employment. Financing of up to €3.825 bn is secured through Morgan Stanley and MUFG Bank. The tender closes Oct 26, with possible secondary acceptance in early November

Air India A320 Hydraulic Failure Leads to Pilot Dismissal

TENNIND TIMESGTY DCXINDIA ABMINTLLTD

A preliminary AAIB probe found that Air India flight AI2379 lost pressure in all three hydraulic systems at 36,000 ft, causing altitude swings, autopilot disengagement, a brief stall warning and injuries to 24 passengers. The aircraft recovered after the systems restored pressure, landing safely in Delhi. The pilot‑in‑command tested positive for a psychoactive substance and was immediately terminated, with the airline initiating voluntary pilot testing. Investigators note the simultaneous hydraulic loss is highly unusual and are examining possible common causes, including a recent Power Transfer Unit issue, while awaiting further technical data from Airbus and CFM International. The incident was classified as an accident, and the DGCA has been urged to take priority action against substance abuse

Trump Trade Policy Risks Undermining U.S. Chip Supply Chain

SURANASOL SPELS

A White House proclamation to boost U.S. polysilicon production has driven away the last two customers of Germany’s Wacker Chemie’s Charleston, Tennessee plant, putting the 600‑worker facility at risk of closure. The measures impose a price floor and tariffs on polysilicon and related products, but treat foreign‑sourced material the same as domestic, leaving U.S. polysilicon at a cost disadvantage of up to four times. Industry analysts and advocacy groups warn the policy may fail to spur demand and could weaken efforts to secure semiconductor and solar supply chains against Chinese competitors. Wacker has not yet decided on closure and says it is in talks with the administration, while other U.S. producers like Hemlock Semiconductor are less affected. The outcome remains uncertain as the rules take effect in December

Hindustan Unilever’s Growth Revival Plan

HINDUNILVR

Hindustan Unilever (HUL) aims to revive growth after two muted years by cutting costs, sharpening its portfolio, and boosting spend on brands, distribution, and new categories. Capital expenditure will rise to 3% of turnover, with over 75% earmarked for growth and savings. The company targets a 500‑basis‑point “fuel for growth” via better margins, efficiencies, and savings, redeploying gains into product, packaging, media, pricing, and channel investments. HUL sets a medium‑term EBITDA margin goal of 22‑24% (23.6% in FY26) and seeks higher operating leverage. Growth will be driven by four pools: increasing consumption, premiumisation (each 40% of incremental turnover), expanding categories, and entering new spaces (20%). Focus areas include premium beauty, functional deodorants, and repositioning foods like Horlicks toward lifestyle nutrition, superfoods, and protein, with a heavy emphasis on digital media and premium brand investment

Mandatory Co‑Located Storage for New Solar and Wind Projects in India

COSMOFIRST

The Central Electricity Authority (CEA) proposes that all solar and wind plants commissioned from July 2027 must include co‑located storage equal to at least 10% of installed capacity with a minimum two‑hour duration (e.g., a 100 MW solar plant needs 10 MW, 2‑hour storage). The draft amendment to the CEA technical standards, open for comments until 4 Oct, aims to improve dispatchability and revenue stability. A later phase (July 2029‑June 2031) will raise the storage duration requirement. The rule makes what was previously advisory now mandatory, aligning with utilities’ and industry’s demand for firm, continuous renewable power
