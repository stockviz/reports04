Mahindra Group’s Electric Unicorn: MLMM’s Rise to a $1 Billion Valuation

M&M 3MINDIA DELTACORP

Mahindra & Mahindra’s last‑mile mobility arm, Mahindra Last Mile Mobility (MLMM), was carved out in 2023 and has since attracted over ₹2,200 crore in equity—from ₹900 crore put in by the parent and ₹1,322 crore from external investors such as the India‑Japan Fund, IFC and Lightrock. The infusion helped the electric three‑wheeler business double its revenue in two years, lifting its valuation five‑fold to more than ₹10,000 crore (≈$1 bn). By FY26, external investors held roughly 22 % of MLMM. The group is gearing up for an IPO in the second half of 2027, positioning MLMM as a flagship unicorn in India’s EV ecosystem

CSB Releases Final Report on 2025 Clairton Coke Works Explosion

JINDALSTEL

The U.S. Chemical Safety and Hazard Investigation Board (CSB) issued a final report on the August 2025 blast at U.S. Steel’s Clairton Coke Works that killed two workers and injured 11. The explosion occurred when workers used pressurized water to flush a 70‑year‑old cast‑iron gas valve, which ruptured and released combustible coke‑oven gas. The CSB found that U.S. Steel lacked written procedures, training, and safe work practices for flushing gas valves, and that an industrial cleaning contractor also had no formal policies. The board recommends both parties develop detailed written procedures and train employees for such operations. OSHA had previously fined U.S. Steel $118,214 for inadequate safety documentation. The report also highlighted the plant’s history of accidents, underinvestment, and prior explosions, noting that better siting evaluations and equipment upgrades could have reduced the severity of the incident

Kennedy Center Ordered to Pay $250,000 Legal Fees to Musician Who Boycotted Trump‑Named Venue

MUKTAARTS

A Washington D.C. Superior Court judge ruled that the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts must pay more than $250,000 in attorney fees to jazz drummer Charles “Chuck” Redd, who canceled his annual Christmas Eve Jazz Jam after the Center’s board added former President Donald Trump’s name to the building. The Center’s breach‑of‑contract lawsuit was dismissed as baseless and retaliatory, with the court finding no contract existed and that Redd’s refusal was protected political speech. The fee award, deemed reasonable, is being appealed by the Center. The case highlights ongoing litigation over Trump‑related controversies and the legal costs incurred by opponents of the name change
