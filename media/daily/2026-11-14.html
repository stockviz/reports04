US Airspace Ground Stop Lifted After Nationwide ATC Outage

MCDOWELL-N DELTACORP

A nationwide ground stop was imposed after radar and communication failures caused instability across FAA control centers, grounding over 5,000 flights and stranding thousands of passengers. The outage, traced to overloaded analog telecom hardware, prompted an emergency shutdown and selective allowance of critical medevac and cargo movements. Officials, including FAA Secretary Sean Duffy and White House advisors, emphasized no safety risk to the public, blamed under‑funded legacy systems, and called for substantial new congressional funding (up to $30‑50 billion) to replace aging equipment with modern digital infrastructure. The stop has now been lifted, with international flights already resuming at airports like Chicago O’Hare, while many domestic flights remain delayed or canceled as the system recovers. The incident has reignited political debate over FAA modernization funding

TNPL

SpaceX Targets First Full Rocket Recovery with Double Catch Test Flight

BLUESTARCO

SpaceX plans a 22‑hour orbital test of Starship that will attempt to catch both the massive booster and the spacecraft mid‑air using arms on the launch tower, marking the first time every stage of an orbital rocket would be retrieved for reuse. The flight, approved by the FAA, will carry 26 Starlink satellites, complete 15 Earth orbits at about 170 mi altitude, and return for a dual-stage recovery—booster back to its Texas pad six minutes after launch and the spacecraft the following day. Success would demonstrate complete reusability, accelerating launch cadence for lunar bases, Mars missions, and future NASA Artemis contracts, while previous tests have only recovered boosters or lost spacecraft
