Key recommendations include: - Counseling and healthcare access - Flexibility where possible - Managerial training to recognize support needs - Removing stigma around seeking help

Sudakshina Bhattacharya (HDFC ERGO) reinforced this, stating that employee wellbeing must be a leadership and board-level priority, directly impacting growth, risk, and adaptability. She cited the CII Panchakosha study, which linked daily work conditions (e.g., workload management, recognition) to employee energy and engagement. The CII India Wellbeing Charter aims to help organizations define commitments, track progress, and ensure tangible improvements in employee experiences

To mitigate operational challenges, some banks and corporates are exploring whether “contracted exposure” (hedging for confirmed imports/exports) could be treated as “anticipated exposure”—allowing higher hedging limits without upfront documentation, though losses from adverse rate movements would fall on the corporate if contracts fail.

While RBI’s direct dollar supply to oil firms may stabilize the rupee, experts like Samir Lodha (QuantArt) argue that importers hedge to protect margins—not speculate—given thin profit margins.

Industry concerns, discussed in a Forex Dealers Association of India (FEDAI) meeting, include implementation gaps, such as documentation requirements for smaller deals, risk of speculative rebooking, and cost allocation if contracts are canceled. Banks may now demand upfront documents even for deals below $5 million, adding complexity.

Historical parallels, like China’s 2015 forex reserve policy, show limited success in reversing currency depreciation, raising doubts about the rupee’s sustained stability post-RBI curbs
