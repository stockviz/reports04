Long‑Term Stock Investing: Quality, Valuation & Margin of Safety

JSWSTEEL BAJAJ-AUTO RELIANCE BHARATFORG MINDACORP M&M BCG ATULAUTO TRENT TVSELECT HDFCBANK GRASIM TATAMOTORS TCS

In this NDTV Profit interview, Alex Mathieu talks with Parag Takkar, senior fund manager at Fort Capital, about building a durable, long‑term equity portfolio. Parag’s two‑word philosophy—“good quality” and “good price”—focuses on buying established, cash‑generating companies at attractive valuations, especially during market downturns. He stresses the importance of strong operating cash flow, low net‑debt‑to‑EBITDA (under 3‑3.5×), and avoiding firms vulnerable to technological disruption. He evaluates sectors differently: for banks, he uses ROA and price‑to‑book; for auto components, he looks at ROC and content‑per‑vehicle growth; for cyclicals, he seeks bottom‑of‑cycle opportunities; and for IT services, he values cheapness, dividend yield, and free‑cash‑flow yield. Parag shares lessons from past mistakes, such as over‑paying for a risky auto stock and selling winners too early, and highlights the need for a margin of safety and discipline. He advises retail investors to stay patient, buy during euphoria‑free periods, and be cautious with small‑cap IPOs, while recognizing that selling should be considered when a stock becomes overly euphoric or reaches a reasonable valuation multiple
