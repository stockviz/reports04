Vodafone Idea Hit with ₹26.8 Cr DoT Notice Over Spectrum Rollout

JIOFIN IDEA AIRTELPP

The Department of Telecommunications has served Vodafone Idea (VIL) a notice demanding ₹26.83 crore in liquidated damages for allegedly failing to meet minimum rollout obligations tied to spectrum acquired in the 2022 auction. VIL stated the notice will not materially affect its finances or day‑to‑day operations and is reviewing its options. The company’s shares rose slightly after the filing, despite a recent month‑long decline. Nissan Tekton 1.0 MT Ladakh Road Test Review

The Overdrive team put the Nissan Tekton (1.3 L turbo petrol, 163 PS, 6‑speed manual) through a 700 km Ladakh drive, tackling high‑altitude, rough mountain roads and steep climbs. The engine delivers strong mid‑range power, though low‑end torque is thin and the gearbox can lag, especially on inclines, occasionally triggering an over‑heat warning. Steering is precise and the chassis feels solid, giving confidence on hairpins and uneven surfaces. Cabin ergonomics are comfortable with good headroom, but rear space is limited and overall interior size could be larger. Despite the low‑end grunt, the car is enjoyable for daily commutes and occasional adventure trips, and its pricing is competitive. The reviewer recommends the manual version for its engaging drive, while noting the need for Nissan to improve low‑rpm response and gearbox cooling

NTT Data Payments Expands into Merchant Financing and Cross‑Border Services in India

AAATECH YESBANK BCG DATAPATTNS RELIABLE CONCOR SUMICHEM SHRIRAMFIN GANGESSECU

NTT Data Payment Services India, a subsidiary of Japan’s NTT Data, aims to grow its domestic payments footprint and enter new segments such as merchant financing, cross‑border payments and eventually small‑business lending over the next three years. After entering India in 2019 via the Atom Technologies acquisition, the firm has focused on back‑end infrastructure for NPCI and other clients, but low UPI merchant discount rates have limited revenue. To boost growth, NTT Data will invest organically in payments infrastructure and seek partnerships or acquisitions for lending licences. It launched the Adaptis platform—already used in Southeast Asia—for merchant payments, cash‑flow‑based credit, and cross‑border UPI‑linked transactions. The company leverages its long‑term, non‑venture‑capital backed strategy and Japanese banking relationships (e.g., SMBC) to secure quality partnerships. Despite a challenging monetisation environment, NTT Data remains bullish on India’s high transaction volume, digitisation, and strategic importance for AI and technology investments

Kia Cerossi EV Review: Range, Features & Real‑World Performance

TATACAP TV18BRDCST HYUNDAI

The overdrive team tests the sub‑4 m Kia Cerossi EV, which uses the same front‑mount motor and battery tech as the Clavis MPV. Two trims are offered: a 42 kWh pack (135 PS, 400 km claim) and a 51.4 kWh pack (171 PS, 526 km claim) with 255 Nm front‑wheel torque and 0‑100 km/h in 8.1 s. Aerodynamics (Cd 0.34‑0.35) and packaging keep interior space generous, featuring a panoramic sunroof, versatile storage, dual cup‑holders, a 100 W USB‑C charger, and V2L capability. On‑road testing around Nandi Hills yielded about 7 km/kWh, confirming decent efficiency but leaving real‑world range open‑ended. Acceleration is smooth, braking blends regen naturally, and cabin noise is low except for tire hum at highway speeds. Charging: 10‑80 % in ~30 min on 100 kW DC, 10‑100 % in 4 h 15 min on an 11 kW three‑phase AC wallbox; slower on a standard 7 kW home charger. Ground clearance rises by 7 mm thanks to battery‑protective suspension travel. Pricing starts around ₹20 lakh for the larger battery, positioning the Cerossi above Tata’s Nexon EV (₹12‑13 lakh) but targeting families seeking a premium, well‑equipped sub‑4 m EV. The review concludes that the Cerossi delivers a compelling blend of range, features, and practicality for buyers under ₹23 lakh

Staveley’s 25% West Ham Stake Sparks Ownership Clash with Kretinsky

BFINVEST MCDOWELL-N 5PAISA

This move threatens Czech billionaire Daniel Kretinsky, who was negotiating to increase his stake and could invoke pre‑emption rights to buy the shares first. The pre‑emption process, expected to take about two months, will determine whether Staveley’s deal proceeds or Kretinsky expands his holdings. Both parties claim they will act in the club’s best interests, while fans worry about stability and the club’s direction amid the ownership dispute
