IDBI Bank Strategic Sale: Ministry Defends Valuation, No Reserve‑Price Change

IDBI CHOLAHLDNG LICI

The finance ministry clarified that the Core Group of Secretaries on Disinvestment (CGD), empowered by the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs, oversaw the valuation and bidding process for the 60.72% IDBI Bank stake sold to Fairfax Financial. The reserve price remained unchanged; the initial 2025‑26 round failed to meet it, prompting a second round in 2026‑27. The process was double‑blind, with only the CGD seeing both bids and valuations. Fairfax’s ₹81 per share bid exceeded benchmark and fair‑value estimates. Additional tax‑demand adjustments added ₹3,917 crore to the transaction value, while potential liability was limited to ₹627 crore, benefitting the government and LIC, which retain residual holdings

Centre to Tap IITs, NITs for Nuclear Tech Courses to Power India’s 100 GW Dream

India’s goal of 100 GW nuclear capacity faces a major manpower shortage, prompting the government to ask IITs, NITs and other top institutes to introduce a range of nuclear‑technology programmes—from PhDs to certificate and ITI‑style courses. An expert committee under Niti Aayog is drafting a plan for a dedicated Indian Institute of Nuclear Technology and will pitch the framework to the Education Ministry and DST. Estimates suggest 2.5 lakh skilled workers will be needed over 20 years, with 13,600 required by 2032, yet few institutions currently offer nuclear courses. Recent steps include new nuclear engineering programmes at IIT Bombay, IIT Kanpur, IIT Madras, IIT Jammu and upcoming ones at IIT Gandhinagar, alongside on‑the‑job training modules for vendors and existing staff

Global FMCG Giants Turn to India as Western Markets Slow

HINDUNILVR NESTLEIND

More than a dozen top consumer‑goods CEOs—including those of Coca‑Cola, L’Oréal, Pernod Ricard, Nestlé, Unilever, PepsiCo and others—highlighted India as a key growth market in their July‑September earnings calls. While sales are faltering in the US and Europe, the companies see strong demand for premium products, expanding distribution, and new e‑commerce and quick‑commerce channels in India. PepsiCo praised its Indian foods and beverages performance; Pernod Ricard reported a 19% sales rise; Nestlé is focusing on “billionaire” brands like Maggi and KitKat; Coca‑Cola is boosting execution and distribution; and Unilever noted significant market‑share gains in beauty and laundry. The optimism comes despite inflation‑driven headwinds, with India’s economy projected to grow around 7.3% in Q2 FY 2026‑27, outpacing global slowdowns

Handset and TV Makers Cut Production by Up to 20% Amid Festive Sales Slump

3MINDIA BCG

Smartphone and television manufacturers in India are trimming output by as much as 20% over the next 2‑3 months after festive-season sales fell about 25% YoY, leaving excess inventory. Soaring memory‑chip prices—up two‑to‑three‑fold in the past 10 months—have pushed phone prices up 35‑40% and TV prices up 20‑25%, crushing demand in the entry‑mid segment that dominates the market. Overall smartphone and TV unit sales are down over 10% YoY for Jan‑Sept, with TV sales down 15% YoY for the festive period. PCB costs for 32‑43‑inch TVs have doubled, and producers expect a modest recovery next year as consumers eventually replace devices despite higher prices

IndiGo Reports Rs 1,879‑Crore Loss Amid Soaring Fuel Costs and Geopolitical Turmoil

INDIGO

IndiGo posted a net loss of Rs 1,879 crore in the September quarter as jet fuel prices jumped 46% year‑on‑year, offsetting a 26% rise in revenue. The airline cut capacity by about 1.6% and used yield management, but these measures only partially mitigated the cost surge. CEO Willie Walsh expects low‑single‑digit capacity growth and a >25% boost in passenger revenue for the festive quarter, with fare hikes to offset fuel expenses. Despite the challenges, IndiGo will continue taking new aircraft deliveries and plans to launch its first Airbus A350 by 2028, maintaining a focus on long‑term international expansion and disciplined growth

Health Insurers Request Expense‑Cap Adjustments for Mandatory Products

STARHEALTH

Standalone health insurers, led by Star Health, have urged the IRDAI to exclude mandatory government‑backed products such as motor third‑party, crop insurance and government health schemes from the premium base used to compute expense‑of‑management (EoM) limits. They argue these products involve no intermediaries and should not count toward the 25% cap, which would otherwise disadvantage them under the regulator’s proposed common EoM framework for general and health insurers. The IRDAI plans to lower expense limits to 20% over five years, with an interim target of 25% within two years, and shift the calculation from gross written premium to gross direct premium income, excluding reinsurance premiums. Currently, EoM caps stand at 30% for general insurers and 35% for standalone health insurers. The insurers seek a level playing field, citing different distribution economics between mandatory products and retail health policies

India’s CBG expansion hits manure market bottleneck as plants struggle to sell organic fertilizer

India’s push to scale compressed biogas (CBG) faces a critical hurdle: while plants can generate clean gas, they produce large volumes of solid and liquid manure that lack a reliable off‑take market. With only 217 of 1,908 registered CBG plants commissioned and many more under construction, operators risk mounting storage costs, reduced capacity, and shutdowns. Farmers prefer subsidised chemical fertilizers, and recent government manure‑sale incentives have been withdrawn, threatening the sector’s financial viability and jeopardising continued state support

Unauthorised cross‑border crypto transfers: ED raids Mudrex and Instadapp

The Enforcement Directorate (ED) raided three premises in Bengaluru and one in Madhya Pradesh on Oct 26 under FEMA, alleging that Mudrex and Instadapp facilitated unauthorised crypto‑based cross‑border money transfers worth over ₹4,900 crore. The probe claims Mudrex advertised instant overseas transfers without RBI approval and used crypto tokens to bypass the regulated foreign‑exchange system. Instadapp, a DeFi platform, is accused of similar violations, with preliminary findings showing contraventions amounting to about $40 million (₹380 crore). The ED’s investigation focuses on potential breaches of FEMA, including transfers involving tax‑haven jurisdictions

DECNGOLD

Weil Gotshal & Manges Plans Wind‑Down Amid Partner Exodus

GRAUWEIL

Weil, Gotshal & Manges, a storied New York firm with $2 billion in 2023 revenues, has recommended winding down after a cascade of partner defections and a failed merger. The partnership must still vote on the plan, and the firm will pursue an out‑of‑court wind‑down with restructuring advisers, avoiding bankruptcy if solvency is maintained. Hundreds of associates and dozens of partners will flood the legal job market, while rival firms race to recruit them. Weil must honor professional duties to clients such as Apple and Goldman Sachs, continue paying staff for 90 days plus 2026 bonuses, and settle obligations to landlords, lenders, and other creditors. Its biggest challenge will be retaining and placing its lawyers and staff while ensuring continuity of client service and meeting financial and contractual commitments
