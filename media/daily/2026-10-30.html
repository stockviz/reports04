Tamil Nadu’s Stone Transport Ban Delays Bengaluru’s Blue Line Metro, Disrupts Airport Connectivity

METROBRAND

Tamil Nadu’s continued ban on transporting stones and construction aggregates to Karnataka has severely disrupted Bengaluru’s Namma Metro Blue Line project, particularly the 58-km stretch connecting Silk Board to Kempegowda International Airport (KIA). The restrictions—imposed in July 2026 and extended—have led to a 40,000-tonne supply shortfall, affecting 600–700 truck operators and pushing up material costs.

Key impacts: - 30–40% of construction materials (quarry dust, crushed stone, aggregates) were sourced from Tamil Nadu’s Krishnagiri district; shortages now hinder concreting, track work, and architectural finishing. - Aggregate prices have doubled (from Rs 800/tonne to over Rs 1,600/tonne) as suppliers shift to Bidadi (Karnataka), reducing efficiency. - Depot construction and railway ballast procurement for track laying are delayed, with Outer Ring Road (ORR) projects facing setbacks. - Contractors face financial strain, while BMRCL officials report delays in the KR Pura–KIA stretch, though the Silk Board–KR Pura elevated corridor is advanced.

Broader challenges for the Blue Line: - Engineering hurdles: A 1-km three-pier portal structure (first of its kind in Namma Metro) is being built to accommodate four tracks (for future Orange Line integration). - ORR construction complexities: Heavy traffic, utility relocations, and land constraints slow progress. - Future benefits: Once operational, the driverless Blue Line will offer direct Metro access to KIA, reducing road congestion for airport commuters.

The ban, aimed at curbing illegal mining and addressing domestic supply shortages, has inadvertently escalated costs and timelines for Bengaluru’s high-priority infrastructure projects
