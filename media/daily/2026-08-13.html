Goldman Sachs Finds Limited AI Job Threats in India, Highlights Productivity Gains

MOTILALOFS

A Goldman Sachs report, explained by Chief India Economist Shantanu Sengupta, estimates that only 8‑12 % of India’s non‑agricultural workforce faces a genuine risk of substitution by generative AI, with about 45 % of tasks likely to be augmented and another 40‑45 % remaining untouched. Overall labor productivity is projected to rise by roughly 0.4 % annually over the next decade. The impact is uneven: low‑skill, repeatable roles in finance, media, and low‑end IT are most exposed, while sectors such as health‑care, education, and many knowledge‑intensive services stand to benefit from AI‑driven complementarity. India’s IT and GCC sectors comprise just ~2 % of total employment; despite modest job cuts in a few large firms, total tech‑related jobs have grown, and revenues continue to rise, signalling early productivity gains. The discussion draws a parallel with India’s past digitalisation—mobile internet diffusion spurred a platform economy—suggesting that a well‑sequenced, cost‑effective AI rollout could replicate that boost. Key constraints include the need for expanded data‑center infrastructure (power, cooling, water) and potential protectionist moves in services exports, which could hamper growth more than AI itself. Nonetheless, India’s deep talent pool of engineers and developers positions it well to develop and deploy AI across professions
