India’s Cancer Drug Crisis: Sky‑High Prices Push Patients Toward Bankruptcy

MAXHEALTH ABANSENT

The article highlights the crippling cost of cancer treatments in India, illustrated by the case of 65‑year‑old Surekha Panicker, who spent over ₹35 lakh on immunotherapy, surgery, and related care, exhausting her savings. Supreme Court observations have exposed massive mark‑ups, with drugs bought by retailers for ₹2,700 being sold to patients for ₹27,000. The government proposes capping trade margins for non‑scheduled cancer drugs at 30 % of MRP, which could cut prices by up to 70 % and save ₹2,500 crore annually, but critics say this won’t affect patented drugs whose base prices remain exorbitant. Patient‑assistance schemes are limited, and the Jan Aushadhi scheme offers drastically lower prices for generic drugs. Experts call for broader price‑control mechanisms, production‑cost‑based pricing, and standardized hospital reimbursement to prevent patients from facing financial ruin while seeking life‑saving cancer care

Litigation Funding Fuels High‑Stakes Inheritance Battles in India

FIVESTAR SHAREINDIA

Litigation financiers are increasingly backing costly inheritance disputes, offering to cover legal expenses in exchange for a share of any recovery. Cases like Amit Aggarwal’s Rs 250 crore land fight illustrate the appeal for cash‑poor but asset‑rich claimants. Firms such as LegalPay, Five Rivers Capital, Elf Partners and Share Samadhan evaluate each claim’s legal merit, evidentiary strength, stage of litigation, opponent’s finances and exit prospects, often demanding potential returns of 5‑10 times the capital. They typically fund high‑value assets (Rs 50 crore+), prefer clear monetisation routes (buyer, court‑ordered sale, co‑heir buy‑out), and reject cases lacking a viable exit despite strong legal standing. While the model can accelerate settlements, investors remain cautious, as successful litigation does not guarantee recoverable cash
