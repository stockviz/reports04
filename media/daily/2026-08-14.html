The Rise of Decentralized Wastewater Recycling in India

INDIANB

In a Climate Clock podcast episode, founder and CEO Vikas Brahmavar of Boson Whitewater discusses his company’s journey since 2011 to turn municipal wastewater into high‑quality, reusable water. He explains how perception (“yuck factor”) shifted from drinking‑water activism to industrial applications like HVAC, laundry and cooling towers, where recovered water meets strict IS 10500 standards. Boson Whitewater installs and funds specialized tertiary treatment units in apartment complexes, captures excess treated water, stores it, and transports it via dedicated tankers to nearby industries, creating a zero‑liquid‑discharge model. The approach addresses India’s water scarcity by reducing groundwater extraction and preventing lake contamination. He outlines the economics: municipal water is heavily subsidized, tanker water costs ₹130‑250 per kL, while Boson’s product sells at ₹200‑250 per kL, delivering cost savings and eliminating waste. Capital recovery is ~4‑4.5 years per apartment. The company processes ~1.4 million L/day, has saved over 140 crore L, and sees a ₹4 billion specialized treatment market and a ₹30,000 crore broader recycling opportunity, with current projects in Bangalore, Chennai, and upcoming ones in Hyderabad, Pune, and Delhi. He also covers water‑ATM initiatives for indirect potable reuse, robust IoT‑based quality monitoring, and the challenges of scaling against larger players, emphasizing impact‑driven investment and the need for supportive policies

July WPI Beats Poll but Food Prices Remain Elevated

ICRA TV18BRDCST MOTILALOFS

July’s Wholesale Price Index (WPI) rose 9.78%, undercutting the CNBC‑TV18 poll estimate of 10.10% and even the June figure, offering a modest relief. However, food inflation stayed high at 6.65% (MoM 6.14%), driven by El Niño‑linked price spikes, especially in sugar. Manufactured goods inflation ticked up to 8.3% from 7.48% in June, posing a risk of feeding into CPI if sustained. Fuel prices fell at the wholesale level, providing some balance. Experts highlighted that the lower WPI eases pressure on consumer prices and may temper future CPI rises, while noting the shift toward using the new Output/Input Producer Price Index (PPI) for GDP deflation. Overall, the data suggest a peak in wholesale inflation, but food price pressures persist
