Tesla Drops Solar Roof Tiles, Refocuses on Traditional Panels Amid Viability Issues

Tesla is winding down its Solar Roof tile product, citing financial infeasibility, and will now only supply conventional solar panels. The move follows years of underperformance, price inflation, and a class-action settlement. Despite abandoning the roof tiles, Tesla continues investing in solar, planning a $10 billion Texas factory. Concurrently, Elon Musk’s broader strategy leans toward fossil‑fuel‑based power for AI and chip manufacturing, reflecting a shift away from the company’s earlier sustainability emphasis

Beyond the Blue: Levi Strauss Expands Beyond Jeans in India

LAXMIMACH TCNSBRANDS TIMEX

Levi Strauss & Co. is boosting its Indian business by moving past its core denim line into women’s apparel, non‑denim items, and premium “Red Loop” products, which now account for 15% of sales. Revenue rose 20.6% to ₹2,223.4 crore in FY25, with net profit up 26.9%. The company notes strong double‑digit growth in women’s wear and mid‑double‑digit growth in denim and non‑denim, and sees significant headroom in tops and premium segments. Levi is shifting to larger 5,000 sq ft “Icon” stores, now operating 500 locations, and relies on a localized supply chain—over 95% of products are made in India—insulating it from global disruptions. It also launched a kids’ line via a partnership with Apparel Group India, focusing primarily on men’s and women’s categories as part of its strategy to help the firm reach a $10 billion valuation, with India among its top six markets

Nvidia Announces 15% AI Server Price Hike Amid Soaring Memory Costs

OFSS BEL IDEAFORGE

Nvidia has warned its biggest customers that AI server prices will rise by over 15% for shipments early next year, affecting systems built with its Vera Rubin and Grace Blackwell chips. The increase, tied to the generation of Nvidia chips and memory configurations, reflects surging DRAM prices driven by high demand and limited supply from Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron. Contract manufacturers for major data‑center operators like Microsoft, Google, and Oracle have relayed the news. The hike adds pressure to AI data‑center expansion plans and may push customers to consider alternatives or in‑house chip solutions, while rivals also face memory‑supply constraints

Paramount‑California Antitrust Talks Over Warner Bros Deal

PARACABLES LGBBROSLTD

Paramount and California AG Rob Bonta will meet with Paramount executives on Aug. 24 for preliminary settlement talks in the state antitrust suit seeking to block Paramount’s $110 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery. The lawsuit, filed by 12 state AGs, argues the merger would reduce competition in film distribution and cable TV, raise consumer prices, and harm theaters and workers. Pressure to settle has grown, with Gov. Newsom, L.A. Mayor Bass, and the DGA urging a deal, though officials say discussions must focus on the three markets cited in the complaint. Paramount has delayed the deal to as late as June 2027; a further delay past Sept. 30 triggers a $650 million quarterly penalty, and a breakup would cost $7 billion. The DOJ and EU regulators have approved the merger, but state officials and industry groups remain opposed

Indian Car Buyers Face a Paradox of Choice Amid Exploding Variant Options

BDL MARUTI HYUNDAI

In India, the number of passenger‑vehicle models has risen 8% since 2021, but the real explosion is in variants—up 56% to 1,923—covering diverse powertrains, transmissions, features, and price points. Automakers favor creating new trims over whole new models to quickly meet varied consumer demands and maximize existing platforms. While this broadens options and lets buyers find a tighter fit for their needs, it also creates complexity: overlapping trims make it harder to discern value differences, dealers must manage larger inventories, and consumers spend more time weighing modest price premiums (₹50‑75 k). Industry leaders warn that clearer variant hierarchies are needed to ease decision‑making despite the market’s competitive push to continually add more options

FSSAI Issues Over 150 Notices for Misleading Ads and Labelling Violations

India’s food safety regulator, the FSSAI, has served more than 150 notices to major food and beverage companies—including Nestle India, PepsiCo, Mondelez, Coca‑Cola, Danone, Ferrero, Diageo, Pernod Ricard, Red Bull, Monster, and Hell Energy—for false advertising, misleading claims, and labelling breaches.
