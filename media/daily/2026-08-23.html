Legal Limits on Competitive Advertising in India

BMWVENTLTD ZOMATO

The discussion explains that while Indian Constitution guarantees freedom of speech, advertising is restricted by trademark law (Section 29) and fair trade practices. Brands may “puff” their own products, but cannot disparage competitors with false or unsubstantiated claims. Truthful, fact‑based statements may be defended, but courts assess whether the claim is misleading, harmful to the competitor’s reputation, or influences consumer perception. Examples like Apple vs Samsung, Coke vs Pepsi, and a recent Delhi High Court detergent case illustrate how courts differentiate friendly banter from unlawful denigration, emphasizing that advertisers must stay within the “Lakshmana Rekha” of permissible competition
