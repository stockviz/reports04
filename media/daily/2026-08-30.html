Young India Shifts from Crypto Hype to SIP Discipline

CAMS 3MINDIA GROWW TIMESGTY PAYTM

India’s Gen‑Z and millennial investors, initially drawn to crypto and high‑frequency trading, are increasingly adopting systematic investment plans (SIPs) as a disciplined, long‑term wealth‑building tool. Data from CAMS shows live SIP accounts for under‑20s almost doubled in FY26 to 2.9 lakh, with a 20.9% rise in the first half of 2026, while 20‑30‑year‑olds’ SIPs grew modestly. Monthly SIP sales rose 9% for under‑20s and 4% for 20‑30‑year‑olds. Real‑life stories—from 15‑year‑old crypto enthusiast Srushti moving to SIPs, to Khwaish Mittal’s daily metal investments—illustrate the trend. Fintech platforms report higher SIP counts among younger users, with flexi‑cap funds and gold/silver SIPs gaining popularity. Educators and distributors note a shift to short‑form, engaging content on Instagram to attract youth, and rising education costs are prompting parents to start SIPs for children. However, volatility has led to a high SIP stoppage ratio (91% in June, easing to 82% in July) and short average SIP lifespans of 2‑3 years, highlighting the need for better investor education to sustain the habit

Automakers Hedge Bets Amid Fragmented Powertrain Landscape in India

HYUNDAI BDL MARUTI

As petrol’s dominance wanes, Indian carmakers are diversifying rather than committing to a single alternative. MG launched an SUV with both PHEV and EV options, Skoda revived a diesel model, Hyundai expanded its CNG lineup while prepping new EVs, and Maruti Suzuki continues to push CNG alongside other variants. Experts say this reflects a shift toward a fragmented market where CNG, diesel, hybrids, EVs, and PHEVs all compete for space, especially across diverse consumer needs—from city commuters to highway and small‑town drivers. While this optionality offers flexibility, it also entails costly, long‑term investments in multiple engine programs, battery capacities, platforms, and supply chains, with automakers awaiting clearer consumer preferences to emerge

Temasek backs Singapore Airlines’ stake in Air India despite losses

NSIL TENNIND

Singapore’s state investor Temasek, which holds a 50.41% stake in Singapore Airlines (SIA), reaffirmed support for SIA’s long‑term strategy despite mounting losses from its 25.1% holding in Air India. Air India is seeking about $1.5 billion in fresh equity, sparking concerns in Singapore, with opposition lawmaker Kenneth Tiong urging that Temasek’s funds not be used to bail out the Indian carrier. Temasek’s Juliet Teo emphasized a long‑term view, noting the complex, multi‑year nature of Air India’s transformation and external industry challenges. SIA has recorded roughly S$1.3 billion in operating losses linked to Air India, contributing to its first quarterly loss since 2022, though the figure has improved from an earlier S$2.02 billion balance‑sheet hit. Despite the financial burden, Temasek remains supportive, citing the importance of strategic moves for long‑term growth and global competitiveness

HDFC Bank CEO Sashidhar Jagdishan to Step Down in October

GMDCLTD HDFCBANK

HDFC Bank’s managing director and CEO Sashidhar Jagdishan has informed the board that he will not seek reappointment when his term ends on October 26, ending months of speculation about an extension. The board will fast‑track the selection of his successor, with deputy managing director Kaizad Bharucha seen as a leading internal candidate. The board must recommend at least three candidates to the RBI, possibly including external names. Jagdishan’s decision follows recent board actions, including penalties imposed on him and other senior executives over a review of deposit mobilisation arrangements, and indications that the RBI might favor a shorter tenure. His departure coincides with the upcoming retirement of long‑serving CFO Srinivasan Vaidyanathan

Air India Teases Arrival of Second Boeing 787‑9 Dreamliner

BCG TENNIND

Air India posted a teaser showing a fresh Boeing 787‑9 Dreamliner from Everett, signaling another addition to its fleet as part of its transformation plan. The airline earlier received its first factory‑built 787‑9 (VT‑AWA) in January, a 296‑seat aircraft with Business, Premium Economy and Economy cabins. Air India aims to acquire six wide‑body jets in 2026, including A350‑1000s and 787‑9s, while refurbishing 26 older 787‑8s. By the end of 2026, about 60% of its wide‑body fleet will feature modern interiors, bolstering its long‑haul market position
