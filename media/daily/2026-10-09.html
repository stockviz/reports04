Thermax seeks partner to boost First Energy’s renewable business

THERMAX ENRIN COSMOFIRST

FSSAI Issues Notices Over Misleading Claims on Celevida GLP

DRREDDY NESTLEIND

India’s food regulator, FSSAI, has served notices to Dr Reddy’s, Nestle Health Science, Flipkart, Amazon and three others for allegedly deceptive advertising of the nutrition product Celevida GLP+. The ads claimed the product supports lean‑muscle preservation during GLP‑1/GIP weight‑loss therapies, is a “complete daily nourishment,” and boasts “100% RDA BCAA” and European purity—claims the regulator says are misleading. FSSAI has ordered the companies to stop using “100%” on packaging and promotional material and to justify why further action under the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 should not be taken

Tech Giants Propel India’s Renewable Energy Surge as Data Centre Demand Explodes

4THDIM ATHERENERG RELIANCE CLEANMAX

Major tech firms—Google, Amazon, Meta, and Apple—are driving India’s renewable energy boom by securing long-term clean power contracts to fuel their expanding data centres and AI infrastructure. Demand for renewable capacity could triple to 32.4 GW by 2030 (from 16.1 GW in 2026), with data centre IT load rising to 5.4 GW (from 2.7 GW in 2026), requiring 4 GW of additional 24/7 clean power.

Key trends: - Hyperscalers (Google, Meta, Amazon) are bypassing state grids, signing 15–20-year Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) with renewable developers, ensuring stable revenue streams. - Meta leads with ~1 GW of renewables (837 MW solar/wind + 168 MW AI data centre in Gujarat). - Amazon has 199 MW wind PPAs and 50+ solar/wind projects. - Google backs 150 MW solar (Rajasthan) and prior 186 MW wind/solar deals. - CleanMax (Mumbai) dominates hyperscaler deals (35%+ market share), with 42% of its capacity tied to data/AI.

Economic & structural shifts: - Virtual PPAs and energy attribute deals let firms meet carbon goals without direct physical supply. - Group-captive/open-access models cut costs vs. grid power. - Developers gain predictable demand, while tech firms hedge against rising energy expenses.

The surge reflects AI/data centre growth and corporate push for sustainability, reshaping India’s energy market

OpenAI’s Revenue Growth: $50B Run Rate Falls Short of Earlier Estimates

OpenAI’s annualized revenue is now estimated at about $50 billion, up from prior projections but lower than recent reports suggesting nearly $70 billion. The discrepancy stems from investor adjustments to align OpenAI’s figures with rival Anthropic’s accounting methods, particularly regarding cloud provider sales.

Both companies compete fiercely for enterprise clients, with Anthropic’s revenue hitting $65 billion by July. OpenAI has delayed its IPO plans, citing safety concerns, while Anthropic is poised for a potential fall IPO. OpenAI is also in talks for a $30 billion+ funding round, valuing it at $1.4 trillion.

The company’s revenue growth has accelerated due to demand for newer models and coding tools, though earlier estimates (e.g., $40B+ in August) have been revised downward. OpenAI did not comment on the report

Festive Travel Surge Triggers Higher Airline Fuel Surcharges

SKYWAYS INDIGO TCIEXP TENNIND

Indian airlines—including Air India, Air India Express, Akasa Air, and previously IndiGo—are raising domestic and international fuel surcharges ahead of the Diwali and year‑end travel peak. ATF prices jumped about ₹16 per litre from October 1, boosting a cost component that accounts for 30‑40% of airline expenses. New surcharges start October 9: Air India/Express will charge ₹400–₹1,200 on domestic routes (up to 500 km to over 1,500 km) and $55–$215 internationally; Akasa Air will levy ₹375–₹1,150 domestically and ₹2,500 on flights to select Middle Eastern and Asian destinations. The hikes aim to offset rising fuel costs amid global energy price spikes and geopolitical tensions

Airlines Suspend Riyadh Flights Over Security Concerns

PACIFICI SKYWAYS INDIGO TCIEXP

Multiple Asian and European carriers—including Air India Express, IndiGo, Akasa Air, Philippine Airlines, KLM, Air France, and Cathay Pacific—have cancelled or suspended services to Riyadh following attacks on Saudi airports. Air India Express halted flights until Oct 10, IndiGo cancelled specific routes on Thursday‑Friday, Akasa Air suspended its Kochi‑Riyadh flights, and Philippine Airlines cancelled Manila‑Riyadh services on Oct 8‑10. Passengers are offered refunds or free rescheduling. The moves reflect safety worries for the major hub serving Indian expatriates and business travelers, while India’s Foreign Ministry condemned the attacks
