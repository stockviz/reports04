Banks Brace for Hit to Bancassurance Fees Amid IRDAI Commission Cuts

HDFCBANK CANBK AXISBANK CENTRALBK

Indian banks are evaluating the impact of proposed IRDAI insurance commission caps. Central Bank of India projects a 33% fall in bancassurance fees (from ₹76 cr to ₹49 cr), while Canara Bank foresees a ₹145 cr hit to fee income. Axis Bank plans to offset losses through higher sales volumes, product‑mix changes, and alternative fee sources, and HDFC Bank says it’s too early to gauge effects. The regulatory move aims to curb distribution costs, potentially pressuring a key non‑interest income stream for banks

South Film Producers Favor Streaming Over Extended Theatrical Runs

PVR INOXINDIA TIMESGTY

Southern producers are opting out of longer Hindi theatrical windows, as seen with Rajinikanth’s “Jailer 2,” which skipped major northern multiplexes after refusing to extend its run from four to eight weeks. The move reflects a shift toward guaranteed streaming revenue over uncertain box‑office returns. While a wider Hindi release could boost pan‑India earnings (potentially adding 10‑50% of total revenue), producers prefer the financial security of streaming deals, despite concerns that this trend may harm the broader film ecosystem

Anup Bagchi Prepares to Lead HDFC Bank Post‑Merger

HDFCBANK HDFC PRUDENT ICICIBANK

Anup Bagchi, newly appointed MD & CEO of HDFC Bank effective Oct 27, is familiarising himself with the bank’s balance sheet, profitability and leadership after the HDFC‑HDFC Bank merger. He notes much of the merger’s integration work is completed, though portfolio rebalancing and retail lending momentum remain priorities. Bagchi, a veteran of ICICI Bank, will first focus on understanding the bank’s mechanics and engaging regulators, customers and stakeholders before any strategic shifts, emphasizing the institution’s strong, process‑driven management team. Industry observers praise his people skills, ability to mobilise teams and technology acumen, viewing them as assets for the bank’s continued growth

Defence Canteens Halt Orders for Three Old Monk Rum Variants After Maharashtra FSSAI Action

BLUESTARCO ROHLTD

The Defence Ministry’s Canteen Stores Department (CSD), a major buyer of Mohan Meakin’s products, has suspended orders for Old Monk Gold Reserve Rum, Old Monk The Legend Rum, and Old Monk XXX Matured Rum following enforcement actions by the Maharashtra Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI). The directive, detailed in an August 11 letter to area managers and depots, also includes McDowell’s No 1 Celebration Matured XXX Rum, Antiquity Blue Whisky, and Royal Challenge Whisky. The FSSAI’s action targets product nomenclature, deceptive blending claims, and flavor additions, affecting sales in Maharashtra and parts of Madhya Pradesh, with potential nationwide supply disruptions through CSD’s network

Haier India to Invest ₹7,000 Crore in Andhra Pradesh’s Largest Manufacturing Push

3MINDIA AMBER DIXON BLUESTARCO HAVELLS LGEINDIA

Haier India is planning its biggest investment in India—₹7,000 crore—by establishing a new manufacturing facility in Satyavedu, Tirupati district (Andhra Pradesh). This marks the company’s entry into South India, complementing its existing plants in Pune (Maharashtra) and Greater Noida (Uttar Pradesh).

The project, spread over 250 acres, will be executed in two phases: - First phase (₹4,500 crore) by 2028 - Second phase (₹2,500 crore) by 2032

The facility will produce air conditioners, washing machines, TVs, air purifiers, and kitchen appliances, targeting ₹22,000 crore in revenue by 2028. It is expected to generate 6,000 direct jobs and 15,000 indirect jobs by 2032.

Strategically located in the Tirupati-Chennai-Nellore electronics manufacturing hub, Haier joins major players like Foxconn, LG, Panasonic, and Daikin, leveraging Sri City’s 7,500-acre industrial park and proximity to Chennai ports, highways, and airports for export-oriented production

Govt Says No General Airfare Regulation, Only Emergency Caps

TENNIND INDIGO SKYWAYS

The Indian government, while approving new air‑fare rules under the Bhratiya Vayuyan Adhiniyam, 2024, confirms it will not regulate airline ticket prices in the free market. However, it reserves the right to impose fare caps during extraordinary events such as natural calamities. Under the rules, airlines must submit fare structures to the DGCA a month in advance and display them online, with DGCA monitoring compliance. Airfares have surged 37‑68% year‑on‑year due to high fuel costs and limited options, prompting concerns from the civil aviation minister about volatile ATF prices
