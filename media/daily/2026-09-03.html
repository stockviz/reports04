SBI’s Ghosh on Misreading GDP Growth

SBIN

Dr. Soumya Kanti Ghosh, chief economic advisor at SBI, says the widely cited 2.6 % growth figure is a result of comparing incompatible GDP series—old base-year data with the newly revised 2022‑23 series. He explains that quarterly revisions, base‑year changes, and methodological updates (deflator, IIP, CPI) regularly alter GDP numbers, and using outdated baselines inflates the perceived slowdown. Even using the correct bases, real growth is around 7.3 % (nominal 9.7 %). Ghosh stresses that revisions are normal worldwide, that the new series actually shows a lower nominal base, and that leading indicators (electricity use, vehicle sales, GST, etc.) confirm robust economic activity. He urges critics to examine the data dispassionately and focus on constructive reforms rather than clinging to misleading numbers

Revolver Misfire Hits Indian Credit Card Issuers

HDFCBANK SBICARD

India’s credit‑card boom is increasingly driven by payment usage rather than borrowing, causing interest‑bearing balances (revolvers and EMI loans) to plunge from around 21% to 11% of annual spend. HDFC Bank’s portfolio yield dropped 50‑60 basis points as its advances‑to‑spends ratio fell to 17% from 27%, and its credit‑card share of total loans shrank to 4% from 6%. SBI Cards saw a 14% rise in spend but only a 3% rise in receivables, with revolvers falling to 22% of receivables (down from 40% in 2020) and interest‑earning assets to 55% of receivables. Banks are seeking to boost EMI conversions, but overall margins are compressing due to cheaper, seamless payment alternatives
