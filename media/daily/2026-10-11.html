One-Year Review of Specialized Investment Funds (SIFs): Growth, Performance, and Investor Guidance

EDELWEISS

The inaugural cohort of Specialized Investment Funds (SIFs) launched in October last year has surged to roughly ₹40,000 crore AUM, with Altiva alone managing about ₹14,000 crore. Their rapid uptake is driven by a ₹10 lakh minimum ticket, a flexible product structure, favorable tax treatment, and the ability to use derivatives (up to 25 % naked) for both hedging and return enhancement. Investors are a mix of high‑net‑worth individuals, family offices, and retail investors accessing the funds through distributors and online platforms; most transactions are in the ₹10 lakh–₹1 crore range.

SIFs are split into three broad categories—equity‑oriented (long‑short, X‑top‑100, sector‑rotation), hybrid (long‑short, active asset allocator), and debt (yet to launch). Hybrid SIFs now dominate AUM (≈ 60‑70 %). Performance data shows that despite a bearish market (Nifty down ≈ 10 % YTD), most equity SIFs outperformed their benchmarks, with ≈ 90‑91 % beating the index over 3‑ and 6‑month periods; top performers like Quant’s equity long‑short fund delivered ~30 % returns over six months.

However, experts caution against judging SIFs on short‑term returns alone. Each fund’s risk profile can vary widely even within the same sub‑category due to differing long‑short ratios, sector exposures, and strategy nuances. Investors should evaluate beta, standard deviation, exposure breakdown, and manager intent, and match the product to their time horizon (e.g., hybrid long‑short for 18‑month‑plus fixed‑income needs, X‑top‑100 for mid‑/small‑cap exposure).

Key advice: ask advisors three concrete questions—(1) What is the closest mutual‑fund analogue and its risk/return profile? (2) What is the fund’s long‑short allocation, beta, and volatility? (3) How does the strategy align with my investment purpose and horizon? By conducting nuanced, purpose‑driven due diligence, investors can navigate the heterogeneous SIF landscape and select products that truly meet their financial objectives

Early‑Bird Fare Advantage Fades on Indian Domestic Routes as Capacity Cuts and Fuel Costs Push Prices Higher

SPICEJET SKYWAYS INDIGO TENNIND BLUEJET RATEGAIN COSMOFIRST 3MINDIA

Airlines in India are abandoning cheap advance‑booking fares, with one‑way economy tickets on the Delhi‑Mumbai corridor now costing around ₹6,200–₹6,900 even for flights a day ahead—prices that were once available for under ₹4,000 months in advance. The shift is driven by reduced flight capacity (IndiGo cutting slots, Air India not expanding), the consolidation of carriers (Jet Airways and Go First out, Vistara‑AirAsia merging into Air India Group) and soaring jet‑fuel costs linked to the US‑Iran conflict. With the market dominated by IndiGo (70%) and Air India (25%), competition is limited, allowing airlines to maintain higher fares. Revenue per available seat kilometre (RASK) has risen over 50% since 2018, and daily domestic flight numbers have slipped from ~3,200 to 2,800‑3,000, with no sign of recovery in the near term. Consequently, the traditional early‑bird discount is disappearing for travelers

AI Can Boost 5G Performance by 30% Without New Hardware, Says Ericsson

JIOFIN AIRTELPP

Ericsson claims telecom operators like Reliance Jio and Bharti Airtel can enhance their 5G networks by 25–30% using AI-driven software upgrades, avoiding costly hardware investments. The Swedish firm’s 2025–2026 equipment already supports AI features, with gains delivered via software alone—no GPUs or 6G required.

Key points: - Cost-effective upgrades: AI optimizations (e.g., traffic management, energy efficiency) can be deployed progressively through software, depending on existing hardware age. - 6G readiness: While AI can be integrated now, new hardware will eventually be needed for 6G’s advanced frequency bands. - Specialized AI models: Telecom networks use smaller, task-specific AI models (unlike general-purpose models like those from OpenAI). - Cybersecurity focus: Ericsson is testing AI systems against threats and implementing safeguards to prevent misuse. - India’s 6G potential: The country could adopt 6G as early as 2029–2030, pending device ecosystem maturity.

Andhra’s Hidden Tea Powerhouse: How Everton Tea Exports 3 Million Flavored Bags Daily to Walmart, Aldi & Carrefour**

Everton Tea India, based in Tirupati’s Sri City, operates a massive tea-processing factory that produces 3 million flavored tea bags daily—exporting them to global retailers like Walmart (Mexico), Aldi, and Carrefour (Europe). Unlike traditional tea-growing states, Andhra Pradesh hosts this facility, which sources bulk tea from Nilgiris, Darjeeling, Assam, and China before adding flavors, herbs, and value.

The company, now a subsidiary of Italy’s Clessidra Group, has grown from 2.5 million bags/day (2023) to 3 million/day, with 30% herbal blends and 40+ flavors (green, black, and flavored). Expansion includes gift-packaged teas, catering to Europe’s gifting trends. Strategically located near Chennai port, the factory began in 2012 with 2 machines and 20 workers; today, it employs 300+ and uses 24 machines.

While 90% of tea is Indian-sourced, the remaining 10% comes from Kenya/China to meet niche European tastes. Despite challenges for bulk exporters, Everton sees growth in health-conscious markets, where Europeans are shifting from coffee to tea. The facility exemplifies Andhra’s role as a logistics and processing hub, not just a tea-growing region
