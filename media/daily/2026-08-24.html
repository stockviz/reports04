AI, Trust, and the Future of Money: How Delegated Commerce Is Redefining Payments

SIL VISACHROME

AI is transforming payments from digital commerce to delegated commerce, where machines autonomously discover, negotiate, and transact on behalf of consumers. Executives from Visa, Standard Chartered, JP Morgan, and Coinbase discussed this shift at The Economic Times World Leaders Forum, highlighting trust, intent, and real-time controls as the new pillars of the financial system.

Key insights: 1. Trust as the new currency – Payments will rely on intent verification (aligning human and AI decisions) and real-time behavioral intelligence to combat fraud, replacing static rules. 2. Speed vs. safety – While AI enables personalized, low-cost transactions, security remains critical. Standard Chartered trained 55,000 employees in AI (225,000 hours) to improve fraud detection, achieving a 30% tech implementation boost. 3. Invisible rails – Consumers care only about outcomes (speed, reliability, cost), not payment methods (UPI, cards, etc.). Banks must evolve from infrastructure providers to trusted value enablers by ensuring identity verification, settlement certainty, and auditability. 4. Stablecoins as a complementary layer – Coinbase’s John O’Loghlen noted stablecoins (like USD-backed tokens) will enhance AI-driven M2M (machine-to-machine) transactions without replacing systems like UPI. 5. Guardrails for autonomy – Autonomous transactions need human oversight (e.g., Coinbase’s AI + human confirmation for customer-facing code) and real-time fraud controls to adapt to sophisticated cyber threats. 6. Future of banks – Traditional banking may disappear in 3–5 years, reshaped by AI adoption speed. Those that embed trust into transactions (not just authentication) will thrive.

Fraud evolution: AI will make both consumers and criminals smarter, forcing payment networks to shift from post-facto checks to **dynamic, real-time monitoring
