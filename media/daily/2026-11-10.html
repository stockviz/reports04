Yes Bank AT‑1 bondholders still await restitution after Supreme Court reversal

SBIN YESBANK HDFCBANK AXISBANK

BNPL Revives in India Through Integrated Shopping Platforms

AVAILFC BCG

Indian payment apps Flipkart‑backed SuperMoney and Razorpay‑backed POP are re‑introducing buy‑now‑pay‑later by bundling credit with their own e‑commerce stores. They focus on small, short‑term instalments for low‑ticket items (fashion, gadgets, supplements) and earn sizable brand commissions (20‑25%) alongside modest merchant fees, allowing them to absorb funding costs and credit losses. Both firms use UPI‑linked autopay, rich transaction data and incremental credit limits (≈ ₹5,000) to manage risk, targeting young first‑time borrowers. Early results show rapid growth in purchase value and revenue, but the model still faces the same credit‑risk challenges that plagued earlier BNPL players, prompting tighter RBI regulations and heightened scrutiny

Pay by the Mile! GMR Proposes Distance‑Based UDF at Bhogapuram Airport, Sparking IndiGo Opposition

USHAMART GMRAIRPORT INDIGO

GMR Airports seeks to almost double user development fees (UDF) at the new Bhogapuram airport over five years and introduce a distance‑based structure for domestic passengers—lower fees for trips up to 1,000 km and higher fees for longer routes. Proposed departure fees rise from ₹1,000 to ₹2,090 (short) and ₹1,230 to ₹2,460 (long) by FY31; arrival fees also increase. International fees would double as well. The plan would make Bhogapuram’s charges far higher than those at other Indian airports. IndiGo has objected, arguing the hikes will make flying less affordable and hurt demand. GMR argues the fees are needed to recover its approved revenue requirement. Regulators must decide whether the investment recovery is justified and if distance‑based charges are appropriate, amid concerns that higher fees could push passengers to alternative transport and undermine passenger growth

Leak, Conflict and Espionage Allegations in Tata Power‑Kleros Arbitration

TATAPOWER 5PAISA

Tata Power has filed a Mumbai FIR accusing a Singapore arbitrator, Stuart Isaacs KC, and Kleros Capital directors of conflict of interest and corporate spying, alleging they traveled to Russia together and that former Tata employees leaked confidential documents to Kleros, undermining Tata’s defense in a Singapore arbitration. The dispute stems from a 2013 NDA‑based joint venture on a Russian coal project; Tata later secured the mining licence alone, prompting Kleros to sue for breach. An SIAC tribunal awarded Kleros $490 million (now $650 million with interest). Singapore courts upheld the award and ordered seizure of Tata Power’s shares in two Singapore subsidiaries, despite Tata’s appeal. The FIR claims bias and fraud, but the arbitrator denies the allegations, which were previously rejected by SIAC and not raised before the Singapore High Court. Legal experts note that overturning an arbitral award on bias requires a high evidentiary threshold and that Indian courts may consider fraud as a ground for non‑enforcement
