Yeet Protein Gains Ground as Brands Shift from Costly Whey

MARICO SANDESH HINDUNILVR

Indian food and nutrition companies are turning to fermented yeast protein to offset rising whey prices and supply constraints. Hindustan Unilever launched Horlicks Protein (RTD and powder) using yeast protein, targeting everyday consumers with affordable pricing (₹83 per sachet, ₹120 per shake) and aiming to expand the low‑penetration (3%) protein market. The Indian protein supplement market, valued at ₹7,461 cr in 2024, is projected to reach ₹13,186 cr by 2033 (CAGR 6.6%). Whey costs have surged from ₹1,000/kg in 2023 to over ₹4,500/kg, prompting brands like Marico’s Cosmix, SuperYou, Mille, and The Good Bug to adopt yeast protein for its whey‑like amino‑acid profile, smoother texture, digestibility, and lower raw‑material cost (₹700‑800/kg vs. ₹2,800‑2,900/kg for whey). Yeast protein products are priced around ₹2,000/kg, offering margin headroom. Companies report strong repeat purchases (60%+ for SuperYou, ~45% for Mille) and growing sales volumes (≈35 t/month for The Good Bug), though supply gaps and rising yeast‑protein costs (+20% in six months) remain challenges. Domestic manufacturing is being explored to secure supply and scale
