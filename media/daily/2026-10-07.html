GST Council’s Upcoming Agenda: Structural Reforms Across Sectors

ZOMATO SWIGGY

The GST Council’s October 8 meeting will focus on structural reforms rather than rate cuts. Key proposals include extending the 5% GST rate to food‑delivery services (currently taxed at 18%), offering dual‑rate options for EV rentals (5% with limited ITC or 18% with full ITC), reducing GST on tractor tires to 5%, and allowing certain hotel, fitness and restaurant services to claim ITC under specific conditions. In healthcare, seven additional rare diseases may be added to the GST exemption list. Defense could see IGST exemptions for missile parts, spares and testing equipment, and some services to foreign shipping lines may also be exempted. Precious‑metal compliance rules may be tweaked without affecting jewelry prices. The council will also target faster registration, quicker refunds, and de‑criminalisation of GST offenses, with any future rate changes likely limited to an annual review for greater business certainty

360ONE BCG

High‑yield private‑credit deals in India are declining, with 67% of H1‑2026 volumes priced below 18% versus 80% above 18% in 2023. Deployment fell 54% to $4.6 bn across 144 deals, after a record $15.6 bn in 2025. Regulatory changes—banks allowed to finance acquisitions from July 2026 (up to 75% of value, capped at 20% of capital) and relaxed external commercial borrowing rules—are expanding financing sources. Houlihan Lokey expects more yield differentiation rather than uniform compression, with private credit still needed for complex, tailored transactions that command an “illiquidity + complexity” premium. The sector remains a small share of corporate financing despite the shifting landscape

Codelco Launches Probe into Possible Double‑Counting of Copper Output

BCG

Codelco discovered evidence that copper production may have been recorded twice in 2024‑25, potentially inflating reports from its Ministro Hales and Salvador mines. The firm has referred the issue to prosecutors, hired Ernst & Young to assess the magnitude, financial impact, and responsibility, and pledged to sanction offenders and recover any losses. The investigation follows earlier irregularities that mis‑reported about 27,000 t of 2025 output, leading to dismissals and revised incentive payments. Ongoing reviews by KPMG and EY will examine production, inventories, and incentive effects as Codelco shifts its strategy from volume growth to profitability and value creation
