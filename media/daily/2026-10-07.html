Supreme Court Rules Tax Rate Depends on Product’s Form at Sale, Not Its End Use

CADILAHC

The Supreme Court held that tax liability is determined by the physical form of a product at the point of sale, not by how the consumer later uses it. Rejecting revenue authorities’ attempt to tax Cadila Healthcare’s GRD Powder and GRD Mix as “non‑alcoholic drinks” at a 10% rate, the court said the items, sold in powder/biscuit form, must be taxed at the 8% rate applicable to powders. The decision emphasizes that tax classification hinges on the goods’ form when supplied, not on subsequent consumer preparation

Private Credit Faces Growing Competition as New Funding Options Emerge

360ONE BCG

Houlihan Lokey expects more yield differentiation rather than uniform compression, with private credit still needed for complex, tailored transactions that command an “illiquidity + complexity” premium. The sector remains a small share of corporate financing despite the shifting landscape
