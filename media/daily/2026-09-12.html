Regulatory Violations Found in Imported Dialysis Solution in Himachal Pradesh

3MINDIA

A government lab in Himachal Pradesh declared two batches of an imported peritoneal dialysis solution (manufactured in Thailand and distributed by Baxter India) as “Not of Standard Quality” after failing bacterial endotoxin tests under Indian Pharmacopoeia 2022. The solution, used in kidney failure patients, was tested at AIIMS-Bilaspur.

The lab report (dated September 3) and a state department letter (September 7) flagged nine regulatory violations, including: 1. Wrong pharmacopoeia standards – Key ingredients (sodium chloride, calcium chloride, magnesium chloride) conformed to US Pharmacopeia instead of Indian standards, classifying the product as “misbranded.” 2. Labeling errors – The brand name (Vantive) appeared in larger font than the generic name and was placed above it, violating Indian drug labeling rules. 3. Missing pharmacopoeial specifications for sodium lactate on the label. 4. Absent critical safety warnings, including: - “Single Dose Container” label. - Storage temperature limits (risk of crystallization below 4°C). - “Do not Freeze” warning. - Caution against using solutions with visible particles. 5. Missing importer details (name, address, import license number).

The violations raise concerns about patient safety, as the solution is used in critically ill kidney patients. Baxter India noted it spun off its renal division in 2024, with the successor company (Vantive) not responding to queries. The Drugs Controller General of India has been informed
