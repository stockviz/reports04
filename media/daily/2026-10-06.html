India’s Telecom Evolution: From Landlines to 6G

TEJASNET JIOFIN

India’s telecom journey began with scarce landlines in the early 1990s, spurred by the 1994 National Telecom Policy and the 1999 New Telecom Policy that opened the sector to private players. The shift from dial‑up to GPRS enabled mobile data, while 2G introduced digital voice and basic data services. 3G, launched in 2008, brought high‑resolution video and mobile internet, accounting for 41% of connections by 2015. The 4G era, accelerated by Reliance Jio’s 2016 entry, dramatically lowered data costs and boosted consumption. Indigenous 4G rollout by BSNL and equipment from firms like Tejas Networks laid groundwork for 5G, which reached 99.6% of districts and 25 crore users by early 2025 with 4.69 lakh base stations. Looking ahead, the Bharat 6G Vision aims to position India as a global contributor, targeting ultra‑low latency, immersive XR, digital twins, autonomous systems, and AI‑native networks. The narrative reflects a continuous push toward faster, more accessible, and deeply integrated connectivity

Hospital Pricing Must Reflect Full Clinical Value Chain

MAXHEALTH

Max Healthcare MD Abhay Soi emphasized that Indian private hospitals should not be judged solely on the price of drugs, injections, or consumables. He argued that these items are part of a broader value chain that includes specialized training, storage, administration, and proper waste disposal, all of which contribute to patient care. Soi noted that hospitals handle thousands of billing items with varying margins, ultimately targeting an 8‑9% net profit.
