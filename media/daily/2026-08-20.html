Ambani’s Rs 2.7 Lakh‑Cr Underground Coal Gasification Bet Could Shield India from Energy Shocks

RELIANCE

Reliance Industries has won rights to the Chintalapudi and Recherla coal blocks in Andhra Pradesh and proposes a Rs 2.73 lakh‑crore, 30‑year underground coal gasification (UCG) complex. The plan involves a Rs 3,000‑crore exploration pilot (2026‑27), followed by up to Rs 2.7 lakh‑crore for development and production from 2028 onward. UCG would convert deep‑lying coal into syngas, which can be processed into hydrogen, ammonia, methanol, synthetic natural gas and other chemicals, offering a domestic feedstock to cut India’s heavy reliance on imported LNG, urea, ammonia and methanol (≈Rs 2.77 lakh crore import bill in FY2025). While the Indian government backs coal gasification through incentives, most schemes target surface gasification, leaving UCG to rely on separate policy support. If even a fraction of the estimated 3.13 billion tonnes of coal is gasified, the project could meet 10‑25% of India’s 100‑MT national gasification target, reducing import vulnerability and providing strategic energy security. However, technical risks—deep seams, geological suitability, groundwater contamination, gas leakage, and subsidence—make the initial pilot phase crucial; failure would curtail the massive investment. Success could give India a sizable domestic source of critical gas‑derived molecules, mitigating exposure to geopolitical and price shocks, though it would not achieve full self‑sufficiency

Connected TV (CTV) in India is expanding rapidly, now reaching 62‑65 million households and 207 million viewers, with audience size tripling since 2022. While urban penetration sits at about 47%, the strongest growth comes from non‑metro areas: CTV audiences grew 21% in metros, 46% in mini‑metros, 55% in small towns, and a staggering 110% in rural regions. States such as Bihar, Jharkhand, Rajasthan, Kerala, and parts of the Northeast show above‑average growth. Most CTV users (68%) also watch linear TV, and many households are “cord‑cutters” or light linear viewers. Prime‑time activity is high on both screens, with 78% of CTV viewers active between 8‑10 pm on weekdays. Measurement is challenging because CTV is a shared, household experience—average households have 3.3 members, and each impression reaches about 2.5 people—necessitating cross‑screen frequency management and unified measurement for advertisers

RIL Plans ₹2.73 Lakh Crore UCG Complex in Andhra Pradesh

RELIANCE

Reliance Industries Ltd. proposes a ₹2.73 lakh crore (≈$330 bn) 30‑year investment to develop India’s first underground coal gasification (UCG) complex in Eluru district, Andhra Pradesh. After securing the Chintalapudi (≈905 Mt) and Recherla (≈2,226 Mt) coal blocks, RIL will spend up to ₹3,000 cr on exploration (Q3 2026‑Q4 2027), followed by ₹1.2 lakh cr for development (2028‑30) and ₹1.5 lakh cr for production (from 2030). The project aims to tap >1 bn t of deep coal, create 3,000‑5,000 direct and 20,000‑35,000 indirect jobs, and support India’s goal of 100 MT of coal gasification by 2030, reducing reliance on crude imports

Puma India Refocuses on Value and Hybrid Fitness Under New Leadership

ROHLTD

Puma India’s new managing director Ramprasad Sridharan is reviving his fitness routine as the brand pivots to a “value” strategy. After a four‑fold growth to ~₹3,200 crore and 11 million pairs sold in FY26, Puma is trimming stores, cutting discounts and emphasizing running (now 30% of retail sales), hybrid fitness (partnering with Hyrox) and team‑sports merchandise (RCB jersey sales). Sridharan stresses offering better‑priced, high‑value products rather than competing on price, expanding locally‑made goods (now 75% of output) and enhancing in‑store experiences. Online sales remain around 50% of the business as Puma seeks to cement a stronger, culturally‑driven sportswear presence in India

LG Energy Shifts Focus to Energy‑Storage Batteries, Plans New EV Cell Production for Tesla and Toyota

GMDCLTD LGBBROSLTD EICHERMOT HYUNDAI

LG Energy Solution, once racing to supply EV batteries, is now concentrating on energy‑storage systems (ESS). Five of its eight North American plants will produce ESS batteries by year‑end, driven by booming demand from AI data centers. The new Lansing, Michigan plant will begin producing cells for utilities and is slated to start output for Tesla and Toyota, while also supplying batteries for Toyota’s EVs. To support the ESS shift, LG has developed lithium‑iron‑phosphate (LFP) chemistry, a segment previously dominated by Chinese firms, offering safety and durability despite lower energy density. The company continues nickel‑based EV cell production, with its joint‑venture plant in Ohio recently resuming output for GM after a shutdown. While ESS demand is set to double to about 125 GWh in five years, analysts say it may not fully absorb the excess capacity built for EVs, leaving EV battery production still essential for LG’s long‑term strategy

Esteé Lauder’s Turnaround Gains Momentum as Q4 Results Beat Expectations

LAOPALA

Esteé Lauder reported stronger-than-expected fiscal Q4 results (ending June 2026), with $3.6 billion in revenue (above analyst estimates) and adjusted EPS of $0.39, driving a premarket stock surge of 8.6%. The company ended a three-quarter revenue decline streak, marking progress in its turnaround under CEO Stéphane de La Faverie, who took over in 2025.

Key strategies include: - Cost-cutting: A 10,000-job reduction (top of prior guidance) to improve profitability. - Brand revitalization: Faster product launches and marketing buzz (e.g., TikTok Shop, Amazon). - Organic growth: 5% organic sales growth for the fourth straight quarter, alongside raised 2027 margin guidance.

While the stock had dropped ~20% year-to-date, the results signal momentum—but long-term success hinges on sustaining consumer engagement and execution
