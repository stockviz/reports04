Hindalco Launches New Flame‑Retardant Alumina Plant

HINDALCO ABCAPITAL

Hindalco Industries has commissioned a 30,000‑tonne‑per‑year precipitated aluminium trihydrate (PAT) facility in Belagavi, Karnataka, for flame‑retardant specialty alumina used in fire‑safety, insulation and EV applications. The plant, built with a ₹400 crore investment, can be expanded to 60,000 tonnes, and several more facilities are planned, pushing Hindalco’s total specialty alumina capacity above 900,000 tonnes—about 9‑10% of the global 8 million‑tonne market

Flu Vaccine Demand Soars Amid H1N1 Surge

MOLBIO APOLLOPIPE TATACAP

Demand for flu vaccines in India has tripled year‑on‑year, driven by a sharp rise in H1N1 and other influenza cases during the monsoon. Digital health platforms like Tata 1mg, Apollo Homecare and PharmEasy now offer at‑home vaccinations, but supply constraints are emerging in many cities. The newly updated 2026‑27 vaccine, aligned with WHO strain recommendations, is boosting awareness. Rapid molecular testing for influenza A, B and subtypes is also increasing, especially in tier‑2 and tier‑3 areas, to differentiate flu from diseases with similar symptoms such as dengue and typhoid. Health experts stress annual vaccination for high‑risk groups and basic preventive measures, while ICMR assures the situation is not a panic

NTPC Targets 244 GW Capacity by 2037 with ₹16.86 Lakh Crore Investment

NTPC MIDHANI POWERGRID

NTPC plans to triple its installed capacity to 244 GW by 2037, aiming for 149 GW by 2032—including 60 GW of renewables. It will invest ₹16.86 Lakh crore across thermal, hydro, pumped storage, renewables, battery storage, mining and nuclear projects. The company targets 30 GW of nuclear capacity, with an immediate focus on the 2.8 GW Mahi Banswara plant, and is developing long‑duration storage technologies. NTPC will also launch a 5.75 Lakh‑tonne‑per‑year coal‑gasification synthetic natural gas plant to replace imports, while emphasizing financial discipline, project completion, fuel security and fleet flexibility

SBI Chairman CS Setty on Growth, Resilience, and Future Priorities

SBIN

Setty says India’s economy has shown strong resilience since 2020 despite Covid, tariff issues, the Russia‑Ukraine war and Middle‑East tensions, and that banks are well‑capitalised with deleveraged corporates. He projects growth of at least 7.5% (Q1 8% debated) and backs RBI’s 5% inflation target for FY27, noting rate moves will be data‑driven. Credit growth of 14‑15% will be driven mainly by retail, agriculture and SME lending, complemented by a Rs 4‑5 trillion corporate pipeline in renewables, power and data centers. SBI’s FCNR(B) window has already disbursed >$9 bn of the $10 bn target, with $4 bn ECB demand visible. Asset quality remains benign, but future risks include operational threats like cyber‑security. The bank aims to expand its MSME share (currently 13% of a 20% loan‑market share), become the top credit‑card issuer, and maintain a 3% NIM and up to 20% fee‑income contribution. Profitability goals are ROA 1% and ROE 15% through cycles. Setty stresses a shift from pure lending to full‑service corporate banking, calls for impact‑oriented priority‑sector lending, and highlights the need to design products and outreach for younger customers, who now constitute about 35% of new accounts

Croma’s Turnaround Accelerates with Profitability Gains and Measured Expansion

BCG NAUKRI FRETAIL

Tata‑owned Croma (Infiniti Retail) swung to an operating profit of ₹159 cr in FY26, narrowing its net loss to ₹614 cr from ₹1,091 cr a year earlier. Revenue rose 13% to ₹21,784 cr, driven by broad‑based category growth and lower GST boosting consumer sentiment. The company closed 35 loss‑making stores, plans to open 40‑45 new outlets—half large‑format Croma and half small‑format “Edge by Croma” stores—while instituting cost‑optimisation habits. Despite industry headwinds like higher smartphone prices, chip shortages, and a weak rupee, Croma’s margins improved, signaling a higher‑quality growth trajectory

Singapore Airlines Retains Control Over Further Air India Investment Amid Losses and Scrutiny

TENNIND NSIL

Singapore Airlines (SIA) will keep final authority over any additional investment in loss-making Air India, with Chairman Peter Seah confirming that no further funding decisions have been delegated to management. Seah emphasized the airline’s long-term potential in India’s growing middle-class market, noting the rare opportunity for a foreign airline to hold a stake in India’s domestic sector.

Despite Air India’s persistent losses (over ₹22,000 crore) and SIA’s S$945.2 million share of its losses in FY26, SIA CEO Goh Choon Phong reiterated belief in its growth potential, calling it SIA’s “new Indian growth engine.” However, he stressed that any further investment would require a “robust business case” and remain within SIA’s financial limits, declining to disclose a maximum tolerance threshold.

The move comes amid scrutiny in Singapore, with opposition MP Kenneth Tiong questioning whether Air India’s losses could impact SIA’s core operations. Air India is seeking ~$1.5 billion in fresh equity from Tata Sons and SIA, per Reuters. SIA’s own net profit dropped 57.4% to S$1.184 billion in FY26
