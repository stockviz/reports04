India’s TV Industry Faces Shrinking Audience and Regulatory Uncertainty

TATACAP ZEEL GTPL SUNSHINE AIRTELPP

India’s Rs 60,000‑crore television broadcasting sector is grappling with a steep decline in viewers as OTT platforms grow, prompting broadcasters (JioStar, Zee, Sony) and DTH/cable operators (Tata Play, Airtel, GTPL) to seek regulatory forbearance on pricing, packaging, and licence fees. They await decisions from the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting and TRAI on tariff flexibility, renewal of BARC’s licence, regulation of application‑based linear TV (ALTD/FAST) services, and reduction of DTH licence fees. The pay‑DTH subscriber base fell 13.8% year‑on‑year, raising concerns about revenue, content investment, employment, and rural access. Industry bodies urge TRAI to grant parity with OTT platforms, streamline BARC’s registration, and finalize pending fee reductions to provide certainty and support the sector’s survival

Leading Indian appliance manufacturers—including LG, Daikin, Samsung, Voltas, Bosch, Havells, and Godrej—are raising prices by 4‑10% from October 1 due to sharp increases in raw‑material costs (copper up 34%, steel 24%, aluminium 16%, resin 17%) and higher logistics, energy, and FX expenses.
