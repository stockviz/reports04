Byju’s Aakash Stake: Why It Matters to Creditors and Why Cash‑Out Is Complex

BMWVENTLTD CAPTRUST MANIPALHOS ZEELEARN AAKASH FEDDERSHOL DHUNINV

A long‑running dispute between Byju’s parent Think & Learn and former subsidiary Aakash Educational Services over a rights issue has been settled, but the exact size of Think & Learn’s remaining stake in Aakash remains undisclosed. The stake is crucial because international creditors—GLAS Trust (a $1.2 bn term‑loan lender) and Qatar Holding (seeking to enforce a $235 m arbitration award against founder Byju Raveendran)—are trying to recover money through Aakash shares. GLAS’s claim depends on whether its security documents cover the Aakash shares, which would then have to be sold under insolvency rules, while Qatar Holding targets a separate block of shares held indirectly by Raveendran’s Singapore entity. Both blocks are entangled in legal and ownership complexities, and Aakash’s weak financials (negative net worth, high liabilities) further depress valuation. Even a tentative $2 bn company valuation would make the combined shares worth far less than Qatar Holding’s claim, and any monetisation would require clear title, court orders, and a buyer willing to assume Aakash’s liabilities. Thus, while the settlement may clarify Think & Learn’s exact ownership, turning those shares into cash for creditors will be a difficult and uncertain process
